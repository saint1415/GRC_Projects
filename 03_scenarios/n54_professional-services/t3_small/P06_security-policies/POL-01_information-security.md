## 1. Purpose
Set up the firm's written information security program (WISP), assign who is accountable, and give every other security policy its authority. The program protects the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of client tax return information, customer information, and firm information. It replaces the 2023 WISP.

## 2. Scope
All Cris Santos Company workforce members (partners, employees, seasonal preparers, interns, and contractors) at the Main and Branch offices and when working remotely. Covers all systems and data, including systems that service providers operate for the firm. It applies to customer information as defined in 16 CFR 314.2(d), tax return information as defined in 26 CFR 301.7216-1(b)(3), and all other firm information.

## 4. Policy statements
4.1 The firm must maintain a written information security program made up of this policy set, the System Security Plan (P02), the risk register (P01), and the incident response plan (POL-03 and P08). The Qualified Individual must keep an index of these parts where every workforce member can reach it. (PM-1; PL-2; GV.PO-01; 314.3(a))
4.2 The IT Manager is the Qualified Individual. The designation must be in writing and signed by the Managing Partner. If the role is ever given to a service provider, the firm keeps responsibility for compliance and the provider must maintain a compliant program. (PM-2; GV.RR-02; 314.4(a))
4.3 A written risk assessment must be performed at least annually (each July) and after material changes, using NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1. It must state the criteria for rating risks, the criteria for assessing confidentiality, integrity, and availability, and how each risk will be mitigated or accepted. Risks must be tracked in the risk register with an owner and treatment. (RA-3; PM-9; 314.4(b))
4.4 Risk acceptance authority: the IT Manager may accept Low risks; the Firm Administrator, Moderate; the Managing Partner, High and Very High. A High risk may be accepted only temporarily with a dated treatment plan. (PM-9; GV.RM-04)
4.5 Security controls must be assessed at least annually by someone independent of their operation, and key controls must be monitored continuously or tested by an annual penetration test and vulnerability assessments at least every six months. (CA-2; CA-7; CA-8; RA-5; 314.4(d))
4.6 The Qualified Individual must report in writing to the Partner Group at least annually, covering the program's overall status and compliance with 16 CFR Part 314, and material matters: risk assessment, risk decisions, service providers, test results, security events, and recommended changes. (PM-9; GV.OV-01; 314.4(i))
4.7 Security policies must be reviewed at least annually and updated after material changes to operations, test results, or security events. (PL-1; GV.PO-02; 314.4(g))
4.8 Exceptions to any security policy must be requested in writing, risk-rated, approved per 4.4, recorded in the risk register, and limited to 12 months or less. An exception to encryption or MFA also needs the Qualified Individual's written approval of the compensating controls (16 CFR 314.4(c)(3) and (c)(5)). (PL-1)
4.9 **Service providers.** Before a service provider receives customer information or tax return information, the Firm Administrator must confirm it can protect the data, and the contract must require it to maintain safeguards and to notify the firm of security events. Service providers must be assessed at least annually based on risk (SOC 2 report or questionnaire). (SA-9; SR-6; GV.SC-05; GV.SC-07; 314.4(f))
4.10 **IRC 7216 notice to contractors.** Every contractor employee who may see tax return information while servicing the firm's software or equipment must receive written notice of 26 U.S.C. 6713 and 7216 before access, as 26 CFR 301.7216-2(d)(2) requires. (PS-6; SA-9)
4.11 **Sanctions.** Workforce members who break security or confidentiality rules must be sanctioned in proportion to intent and harm: retraining, written warning, suspension, or termination. The Risk and Quality Partner and HR must document each sanction. Knowing or reckless misuse of tax return information is also a federal offense (26 U.S.C. 7216). (PS-8; GV.RR-04)
4.12 Security and qualified staff: the firm must use qualified security personnel, employed or through a service provider, sufficient to manage its risks, and must verify each year that they keep current on threats and countermeasures. (PM-13; 314.4(e)(2)-(4))
4.13 Program records (risk assessments, test results, incident records, reports to the Partner Group, and policy versions) must be kept for at least 5 years. (SI-12)

## 5. Compliance and enforcement
Violations are handled under section 4.11. Compliance is checked through the annual control assessment (P07), the access reviews in POL-02, and the Qualified Individual's annual report.

## 6. Exceptions
Exceptions follow section 4.8. They must be written, risk-rated, approved by the policy owner (or by the Managing Partner for High risk), and expire within 12 months.
