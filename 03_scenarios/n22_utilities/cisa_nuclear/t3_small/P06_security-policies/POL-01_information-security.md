## 1. Purpose
Set up the Cris Santos Company information security program, assign who is accountable, and give every other security policy its authority. The program protects the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of company information and systems. It also covers the information and systems that support the physical protection of the radioactive material the company is licensed to possess.

## 2. Scope
All Cris Santos Company workforce members (owners, employees, contractors, and temporary staff) at the Plant, in company vehicles, and at customer sites. Covers all company systems and data, including systems that vendors operate for the company, the plant OT network, and the physical security systems. It applies to Part 37 security-related information and all other company information.

## 4. Policy statements
4.1 The company must maintain an information security program documented in this policy set and the System Security Plan. The program must cover business systems, the plant OT network, and the physical security systems. (PM-1; GV.PO-01)

4.2 Named roles:
- The IT Manager is the designated cybersecurity lead.
- The Maintenance and Controls Supervisor is the OT security lead.
- The General Manager is the individual with overall responsibility for the Part 37 security program.

Each designation must be in writing. (PM-2; GV.RR-02; 10 CFR 37.43(a)(2))

4.3 A cybersecurity risk assessment must be performed at least annually and after major changes, using NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1. Risks must be tracked in the risk register with an owner and treatment. (RA-3; PM-9; GV.RM)

4.4 Risk acceptance authority:
- The IT Manager or RSO may accept Low risks in their own areas.
- The General Manager may accept Moderate risks.
- The President may accept High and Very High risks.

A risk that could lead to theft, diversion, or sabotage of category 2 material may not be accepted at High without a dated treatment plan. (PM-9; GV.RM-01)

4.5 The company adopts NIST CSF 2.0 as its cybersecurity benchmark. For OT and physical security systems it also uses NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3. Where Part 37, the license, or the RCRA permit sets a stricter rule, that rule governs. (PL-1; GV.OC-03)

4.6 Security policies must be reviewed at least annually. They must also be updated after major changes, incidents, or license amendments that affect security. (PL-1; GV.PO-02)

4.7 **Exceptions.** Exceptions to any security policy must be requested in writing, risk-rated, approved under 4.4, recorded in the risk register, and time-limited to 12 months or less. No exception may waive a requirement of Part 37 or the license. (PL-1)

4.8 **Sanctions.** Workforce members who break security policies must be sanctioned in proportion to intent and harm: retraining, written warning, suspension, or termination. HR must document each sanction. Unauthorized disclosure of Part 37 security-related information is a serious violation and is also reviewed by the RSO for reporting. (PS-8; GV.RR-04)

4.9 **Vendors.** Before any vendor connects to company systems, the OT network, or the security systems, or receives Confidential or higher data, the vendor must pass a security review and sign security terms: named accounts, MFA, and incident notice within 24 hours. (SA-9; GV.SC-05)

4.10 **Assessment.** Security controls must be assessed at least annually (P07). The results must feed the annual Part 37 security program review. (CA-2; 10 CFR 37.55)

4.11 **Retention.** Security program records must be retained for at least 3 years, or longer where a regulation or the license requires it. Examples: the Part 37 record periods; retention until license termination where Part 37 sets no period (37.103); RCRA records per 40 CFR 264.74. (SI-12)

## 5. Compliance and enforcement
Violations are handled under the sanctions statement (POL-01 section 4.8). Sanctions range from retraining to termination, depending on intent and harm. Compliance is checked through the annual control assessment (P07), the Part 37 program reviews, and the access reviews in this policy set.

## 6. Exceptions
Exceptions follow POL-01 section 4.7. They must be written, risk-rated, approved by the policy owner (or by the President for High risk), and expire within 12 months.
