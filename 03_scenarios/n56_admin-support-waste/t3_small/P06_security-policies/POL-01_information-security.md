## 1. Purpose
Set up the Cris Santos Company information security program, assign who is accountable, and give every other security policy its authority. The program protects the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of candidate, associate, client, and company information, and meets the firm's legal duties for Form I-9, E-Verify, background check, and personal information records.

## 2. Scope
All Cris Santos Company workforce members (owners, internal staff, contractors, and interns) at headquarters and all branches, and temporary associates when they use firm systems (self-service, timekeeping). Covers all systems and data, including systems that vendors operate for the firm (the ATS, payroll, timekeeping, screening, and AI vendors).

## 4. Policy statements
4.1 The firm must maintain an information security program documented in this policy set and the System Security Plan, and designed to take "reasonable measures" to protect personal information (Fla. Stat. 501.171(2)). (PM-1; GV.PO-01)
4.2 The IT Manager is the designated Information Security Lead. The designation must be in writing. (PM-2; GV.RR-02)
4.3 A risk assessment must be performed at least annually and after major changes, using NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1. Risks must be tracked in the risk register with an owner and treatment. Entering a new state, starting a new service line (for example, health care staffing), or changing how the AI screening tool decides counts as a major change. (RA-3; PM-9)
4.4 Risk acceptance authority: the IT Manager may accept Low risks; the COO, Moderate; the President, High and Very High. A risk that could stop associates from being paid on time may not be accepted at High without a dated treatment plan. (PM-9; GV.RM-02)
4.5 Security policies must be reviewed at least annually and updated after major changes or incidents. (PL-1; GV.PO-02)
4.6 Exceptions to any security policy must be requested in writing, risk-rated, approved per 4.4, recorded in the risk register, and time-limited to 12 months or less. (PL-1)
4.7 **Sanctions.** Workforce members who break security or records policies (for example, sharing E-Verify credentials, opening I-9 or background check records without a job need, or pasting personal information into unapproved AI tools) must be sanctioned in proportion to intent and harm: retraining, written warning, suspension, or termination. HR must document each sanction. (PS-8; GV.RR-04)
4.8 **Vendors.** Before a vendor receives candidate or associate personal information, it must pass a security review (SOC 2 report or questionnaire), and the contract must include security duties, a data use limit (no training of AI models on firm data without written approval), breach notice to the firm within 72 hours (and never later than the 10 days in Fla. Stat. 501.171(6)), and data return and deletion at the end of the contract. Reports are reviewed every year. (SA-4; SA-9; GV.SC-05; GV.SC-06)
4.9 Security controls must be evaluated at least annually (P07) and after major changes. The electronic Form I-9 system must also get a quarterly quality check (8 CFR 274a.2(e)(1)(iii)). (CA-2)
4.10 **Records retention.** Security documentation is kept for at least 6 years. Forms I-9 are kept for 3 years after hire or 1 year after employment ends, whichever is later, and then purged; E-Verify documentation and verifications at least 3 years (Fla. Stat. 448.095(2)(d)). Other schedules are in POL-04. (SI-12)

## 5. Compliance and enforcement
Violations are handled under the sanctions statement (POL-01 section 4.7). Compliance is checked through the annual control assessment (P07), quarterly access reviews, and the quarterly I-9 quality check.

## 6. Exceptions
Exceptions follow POL-01 section 4.6. They must be written, risk-rated, approved by the policy owner (or by the President for High risk), and expire within 12 months.
