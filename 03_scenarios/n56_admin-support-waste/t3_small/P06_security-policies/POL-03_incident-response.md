## 1. Purpose
Make sure the firm detects, contains, reports, and recovers from security incidents quickly and lawfully, keeps associates paid, and meets breach notice deadlines.

## 2. Scope
All workforce members and every system in the APATP boundary, including incidents at vendors that hold firm data.

## 3. Roles and responsibilities
| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| IT Manager | Incident commander for security incidents; coordinates the managed IT provider, vendors, and forensic firm |
| HR and Compliance Manager | Breach determination and notices with outside counsel; DHS E-Verify notice |
| Payroll Manager | Payroll containment (freezing bank changes, holding payments) and pay continuity |
| COO | Engages counsel and the cyber insurer; approves external communications |
| All workforce | Report suspected incidents immediately |

## 4. Policy statements
4.1 The firm must maintain an incident response plan and runbooks for its most likely incidents, starting with a payroll and HR system breach (P08). (IR-8; RS.MA-01)
4.2 Workforce members must report any suspected incident **immediately**, and within 1 hour at most, by calling the IT Manager's incident line. Examples: entering a password on a suspicious page, an associate reporting a missing paycheck or a bank change they did not make, a lost phone or laptop, misdirected I-9 or background check records. Good-faith reporting is never sanctioned. (IR-6; RS.MA-02)
4.3 Every incident must be logged, categorized, and tracked to closure in the ticketing system under the Security category. (IR-5)
4.4 The HR and Compliance Manager, with counsel, must decide whether an incident is a breach of security under Fla. Stat. 501.171(1)(a) and under the laws of other states where affected people live, and must document the decision. A decision that notice is not required because the breach will not likely result in identity theft or financial harm must be in writing, kept at least 5 years, and sent to the Department of Legal Affairs within 30 days (501.171(4)(c)). (IR-6)
4.5 Notices to individuals, the Florida Department of Legal Affairs, consumer reporting agencies, **DHS E-Verify (immediately, for any loss of control of E-Verify data)**, clients under their contracts, and other states' regulators must meet the deadlines in the P08 notification matrix. Counsel must confirm each notice. (IR-6; RS.CO-02)
4.6 No payment to an extortionist may be made without approval from the President, counsel, and the cyber insurer, and an OFAC sanctions check. (IR-4)
4.7 The incident response plan must be tested at least annually by tabletop exercise, and after any major incident. (IR-3; ID.IM-02)
4.8 Lessons learned must be documented within 30 days of closing an incident and added to the risk register. (IR-4)

## 5. Compliance and enforcement
Violations are handled under the sanctions statement (POL-01 section 4.7). Compliance is checked through the annual control assessment (P07) and the annual tabletop.

## 6. Exceptions
Exceptions follow POL-01 section 4.6. They must be written, risk-rated, approved by the policy owner (or by the President for High risk), and expire within 12 months.
