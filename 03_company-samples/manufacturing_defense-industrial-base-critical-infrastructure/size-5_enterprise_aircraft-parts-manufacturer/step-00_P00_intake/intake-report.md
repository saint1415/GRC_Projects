## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the company's own records before the enterprise risk analysis, the BIA and the gap analysis began on 2026-06-01, and before Internal Audit's control assessment began on 2026-07-13. It covers the organization (8 operating sites, the acquired KS-1 plant, the AZ-1 additive center that opened during the window, and the 2 data centers), the systems that receive, create, store or share CUI and the systems that support tier-1 processes, the suppliers that touch them, and the rules that may bind the company and its two commercial service lines (SL-1 and SL-2). Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (BIA interviews, risk workshops, gap analysis samples and walk-throughs, the KS-1 and Level 3 reviews, Internal Audit tests, AI pilot tests) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

At this size the sources are enterprise systems of record: the identity platform, PAM and identity governance, the HR system, the endpoint, EDR and MDM consoles, the CMDB and OT asset inventory, the cloud consoles and SIEM, the GRC platform, the ERP vendor master and contract management system, SPRS and the CMMC Program Office files, the Trade Compliance and FSO files, the Integration Management Office tracker, board and committee records, prior Internal Audit and SOX workpapers, and the SEC filing.

The [asset inventory](asset-inventory.csv) reconciles the CMDB with the OT asset inventory, the cloud account inventories, the endpoint consoles and the AI register; the [vendor register](vendor-register.csv) reconciles the third-party risk register with the accounts payable vendor master and the contract management system.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the gap analysis (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**Identity and access.** The government-community tenant holds about 3,600 CEE accounts, each with a FIDO2 hardware security key registered; keys are issued in person after identity proofing, and corporate users sit in a separate commercial tenant (EV-001). A security key is required for every CEE application from compliant company devices, and accounts lock for 15 minutes after 5 failed attempts (EV-002). About 412 privileged CEE accounts are separate identities checked out through PAM with just-in-time elevation and session recording; about 610 service secrets are vaulted (EV-003). Employee joiner, mover and leaver events flow from the HR system, CEE access needs U.S.-person verification and manager approval, certifications run quarterly, and accounts inactive for 45 days are disabled. Contractor workers are not in the HR feed, and their accounts are disabled from separation notices that suppliers send by email (EV-004, EV-005). HR lists 12,000 employees, including about 640 at KS-1 (EV-005). PLM has about 3,100 named users, about 2.4 million controlled documents and about 2,860 project folders whose export attributes drive access (EV-006). The CUI suite blocks external sharing outside approved domains (EV-007). The CUI exchange gateway runs in FIPS mode on 4 VMs behind the web application firewall, keeps staged files up to 14 days, carries about 9,800 transfers a week, and uses the same managed file transfer product as a separate corporate instance (EV-008). The KS-1 directory is not connected to HR, is reconciled by hand each week, and local sign-in has no MFA (EV-009).

**Endpoints, configuration and OT.** About 2,650 CAD workstations and enclave laptops and about 310 CEE servers report full-disk encryption, secure boot and EDR; device control allows only approved company-encrypted drives (EV-010). Baselines exist for each CEE and MOZ asset class, and the repository has no baseline for the AZ-1 build-prep workstation class (EV-011). The CMDB holds about 4,200 CEE and MOZ components; automated discovery feeds it at 5 sites and the cloud subscriptions, not at FL-3 or AZ-1 (EV-012). About 1,880 mobile devices are enrolled (EV-013), and allowlisting runs on CEE endpoints and servers (EV-014). The MOZ has a central MES in DC-1 with a standby in DC-2, DNC servers at each plant, about 1,150 CNC machines, 240 CMMs, 36 metal additive printers and 24 build-prep workstations at AZ-1; the build-prep workstations were installed from the printer manufacturer's image; CMM results sit on local PCs until a daily upload; the PLM to MES sync allows manual overrides; and file integrity monitoring runs on the gateway and PLM servers, not on MES or DNC servers (EV-015). The 2 GA-1 autoclave controllers run an unsupported operating system on an isolated segment under an exception (EV-016). AZ-1 printers are loaded only by USB from a labeled drive set scanned at a kiosk, under exception EXC-2026-017 (EV-017).

**Network and remote access.** Each site has two carriers; 802.1X and NAC quarantine run at FL-1, FL-2, GA-1, AL-1 and TX-1, not at FL-3 or AZ-1; MOZ segments are separated from corporate at the 6 certified sites, while at AZ-1 the build-prep workstations and printers share one plant segment; KS-1 runs one flat network (EV-018). Boundary rules are deny-by-default with quarterly reviews, and the AZ-1 firewall allows the printer manufacturer's remote support endpoint (EV-019). The cryptography register lists 14 CUI paths with FIPS validation certificate numbers (EV-020). Of 6 manufacturer remote support paths, 5 go through the PAM vendor gateway with company MFA and recording, and 1, an AZ-1 printer manufacturer, uses its own tool (EV-021). The external connections register lists 38 connections; public AI and file-sharing services are blocked in the CEE and reachable from corporate endpoints (EV-022).

**Cloud, logging, monitoring and recovery.** CEE subscriptions are created by vending with data-class and export tags, with daily posture scans, a PLM tier with a second-region standby and a 1,400-session virtual desktop pool (EV-023). Cloud provider A hosts SL-1 in two regions and SL-2 in one (EV-024). Keys are customer-managed with dual control (EV-025). The SIEM lists about 1,940 log sources in the CMMC scope, keeps 1 year online and 6 years in write-once archive, and does not list KS-1 legacy systems as log sources (EV-026). SIEM use cases cover bulk downloads from the CUI suite and gateway transfer anomalies; none covers bulk exports from PLM (EV-027). The SOC is staffed 24x7 at FL-1 and TX-1 (EV-028). Threat intelligence comes from DoD, CISA, the primes and commercial feeds (EV-029). Scans run weekly with 15-day and 30-day SLAs for critical and high findings (EV-030), and patches follow monthly windows (EV-031). The last CEE penetration test was on 2025-06-12; the 2026-03 test covered corporate systems (EV-032). Backups sit in separate immutable accounts, with PLM log shipping every 15 minutes (EV-033). The PLM failover test on 2026-05-09 recovered in 9.5 hours against a 24-hour RTO, and the MES failover on 2026-03-28 took 6.5 hours against 8 (EV-034). Normal changes carry a security impact analysis; emergency changes are reviewed after the fact (EV-035).

**Governance, CMMC status and disclosure.** POL-01 to POL-05 are in force with standards and procedures and annual attestation (EV-036); the exception register includes EXC-2026-011, EXC-2026-017 and EXC-2026-019 (EV-037). The GRC platform holds the common control catalog for 9 providers, the POA&M and monthly metrics (EV-038). The board approved the risk appetite in 2026-02 (EV-039). The risk and technology committee receives quarterly cyber reporting; the disclosure committee has 7 members (EV-040). The Chief Audit Executive reports to the audit committee, and FY2025 SOX ITGC testing is on file (EV-041). A C3PAO assessed the CEE and MOZ at 6 sites from 2026-02-09 to 2026-02-20; Final Level 2 (C3PAO) has a CMMC Status Date of 2026-03-20, and the COO affirmed the same day (EV-042). SPRS shows 110 for the CEE and MOZ CAGE codes and 72 (2025-01-28) for the KS-1 CAGE code (EV-043). The AZ-1 scope change of 2026-05-18 added AZ-1 to the CEE and MOZ after the certification assessment (EV-044). KS-1 keeps CUI drawings on a legacy file server, runs a legacy MES, DNC and SFTP server on a flat network, backs up weekly to disk in the same building, and is due to migrate by 2027-03-31 (EV-045). The materiality playbook was exercised on 2026-04-22 with a ransomware scenario; it has no step to review a draft 8-K for CUI, export-controlled or classified content and no path for a delay request to the U.S. Attorney General (EV-046). The FY2025 Form 10-K includes Item 1C (EV-047).

**Incident response and DoD reporting.** Incident response plan v5 covers DFARS, NISPOM, export and state duties, with a forensic retainer (EV-048). Four medium assurance certificate holders can file DIBNet reports, and the 2026-04-15 drill met its 4-hour target; supplier incident notices arrive by email (EV-049).

**Workforce.** Annual awareness training includes CUI handling, export control and insider threat modules, phishing simulations run monthly, and role-based curricula cover privileged and OT roles (EV-050). Background checks precede access, and exit checklists cover employees (EV-051).

**Third parties and contracts.** About 1,400 active suppliers include 380 flagged as receiving CUI and 6 equipment manufacturers with remote support; 48 vendors hold employee personal information (EV-052). The supplier status tracker lists the 380 CUI suppliers, and an SPRS check has been part of the award workflow for new awards since 2025-11 (EV-053). Current contracts carry DFARS 252.204-7012, 252.204-7019 and 252.204-7020, FAR 52.204-21 and 52.204-25, and awards from 2025-11-10 add 252.204-7021, most at Level 2 (C3PAO) (EV-054). The ERP clause library flags CUI purchase orders, manual purchase orders are possible, and a routing rule keeps 7021 work away from AZ-1 and KS-1 (EV-055). The government-community offering is FedRAMP authorized at Moderate or higher with a CRM on file (EV-056). SOC 2 reports are on file for Cloud provider A, the DC-2 colocation provider, the payroll SaaS and the notification messaging service (EV-057). The managed file transfer, PLM, CAD and CAM contracts have no vulnerability notification term (EV-058). One supplier provides about 70% of titanium forgings for Prime A parts with no cyber assurance evidence on file (EV-059).

**Facilities, maintenance and media.** Badge access, quarterly reviews, cameras and visitor records with escort and sign-out fields are in place at every site (EV-060). Sanitization records and destruction certificates are kept, including for machine controller drives sent for repair (EV-061). Maintenance tools are registered and diagnostic media are scanned at a kiosk (EV-062).

**Export control and classified program.** DDTC registration is current; technology control plans cover every site; about 90 foreign-person employees work under EAR deemed-export licenses or with technology needing none (EV-063). FL-1 holds a facility clearance for Program K, with an FSO, ITPSO and ISSM, a stand-alone classified system, a 2026-05 self-inspection, about 310 cleared employees and an SF 328 on file (EV-064).

**Business volume and structure.** FY2025 revenue was about $4.8 billion, 56% defense, or about $19.2 million of shipments per production day (EV-065). About 1,100 DoD spare and repair orders are open, SL-1 serves about 140 customers and SL-2 serves 19 operators (EV-066). The company runs 8 operating sites in 6 states plus 2 data centers; KS-1 closed 2025-06-30 with its own CAGE code (EV-067).

**Obligations.** Counsel's view on each candidate rule is in EV-068. Benefits data is held by the benefits program, and no consumer payment cards are accepted (EV-069). Insurance covers hurricanes, and hurricane plans exist for the Florida sites (EV-070). The 2026-03 covered telecommunications review covered the enterprise network, not KS-1 (EV-071). The results are in [`obligations-register.csv`](obligations-register.csv).

**Service lines.** SL-1 has a 2025 SOC 2 Type 2 report (Security, Availability) with no exceptions, and customers ask for Confidentiality (EV-072). SL-2 runs in one region, its operators ask for a report that includes Processing Integrity, and it has no model registry (EV-073).

**AI and data.** The AI governance committee register lists 8 reviewed use cases, including AI-001, approved on 2026-05-20 for a pilot of up to 300 U.S.-person engineers (EV-074). Feature discovery found AI features in the supply chain analytics SaaS, the HR SaaS (resume ranking switched on), the contract management SaaS and the printer manufacturer's optimization software, with no committee review record (EV-075). Data loss prevention blocks CUI markings in the commercial productivity suite (EV-076). The data flow inventory lists CUI locations (EV-077).
