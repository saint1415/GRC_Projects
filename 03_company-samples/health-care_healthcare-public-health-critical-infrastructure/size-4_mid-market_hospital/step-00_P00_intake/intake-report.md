## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the hospital's own records before any assessment work began on 2026-06-22. It covers the organization, the systems that create, receive, maintain or transmit ePHI (including the on-premises clinical servers, medical devices and building OT), the suppliers that touch them, and the rules that may bind the hospital. At this size the sources are systems of record: the identity provider, the HR system and Medical Staff Office rosters, the endpoint, EDR and MDM consoles, the CMDB, the clinical engineering inventories, the cloud organization console, the backup service, the SIEM, the accounts payable vendor master, the contract register and BAA register, the emergency preparedness program files, prior risk analyses and internal audit reports, scan results and the incident log. Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (interviews, samples, tests) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the gap analysis (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**Users and access.** The identity provider holds active accounts for all 600 employees on the HR roster, plus credentialed physicians, contracted clinicians, agency nurses, about 240 affiliated practice users, and vendor and service accounts. Every active user has MFA registered. Non-employee accounts carry no end date or sponsor value (EV-001). MFA is required for email, remote access, VPN, the cloud consoles and EHR access from outside the hospital network; on-campus directory administrator sign-in is outside the MFA policy (EV-002). The HR roster lists 600 employees by department (EV-003). The HR feed disables employee accounts on the termination date. On a transfer, EHR roles from the prior department change only by ticket, and non-employee accounts are created and removed by ticket outside the feed (EV-004). The Medical Staff Office and staffing records list about 280 privileged physicians, 5 contracted groups, agency nurses in about 25 positions, and about 240 practice users at 18 practices (EV-005). The last access review ran in February 2026, and the campaign is scheduled once a year (EV-006). 58 accounts hold administrator roles; 31 have standing domain administrator rights, 12 of them vendor service accounts; just-in-time elevation covers the 5 cloud accounts only (EV-007). The EHR uses a 64-role catalog with break-the-glass on restricted records (EV-008). Agency nurses are screened by their agencies, and practice and staffing contracts require notice when a user leaves (EV-009).

**Devices, network and OT.** The endpoint console lists 960 workstations and laptops (including 260 workstations on wheels and 12 downtime workstations), 380 clinical smartphones and 120 barcode scanners, with disk encryption reported on all workstations and laptops. ED tracking board and nursing display workstations are exempt from screen lock and signed in to a generic account (EV-010). EDR reports on all managed workstations and servers; medical devices have no agent (EV-011). The CMDB lists 52 servers in scope (EV-012). The clinical engineering maintenance inventory lists about 1,650 networked medical devices, and the security inventory holds about 1,150 of them. 210 devices are recorded on operating systems past manufacturer support, and MDS2 forms are on file for 35% of models (EV-013). Infusion pumps and imaging modalities have their own VLANs; monitors, fetal monitoring, the cath lab system, laboratory analyzers and dispensing cabinets share the clinical workstation VLAN, and building OT shares the IT network at the main campus (EV-014). 46 vendors have remote access: 32 through the vendor access platform with MFA and recording, and 14 through persistent VPN accounts with no MFA or recording (EV-015). The building automation vendor reaches its system through a cloud portal with one shared login, and no OT configuration export is on file (EV-016). Analog lines and the EMS radio exist only in the ED and the house supervisor office (EV-017). Quarterly scans found 48 Critical findings in Q1 and Q2 2026, averaging 41 days to close; medical devices, OT and vendor-managed servers are outside the scan scope, and no penetration test report is on file (EV-018). Gold images exist for workstations and servers; no baseline was provided for network devices, the virtualization cluster or vendor-managed clinical servers (EV-019).

**Cloud, backups and monitoring.** The landing zone has 5 accounts with organization guardrails, hospital-managed keys and control-plane logs in a write-once archive (EV-020). Cloud workloads back up daily and on-premises servers nightly to the backup appliance, which replicates to a second-region backup account with 35-day write-once retention and separate credentials. The appliance is joined to the hospital directory, and the restore records show 2 single-file restores in 2026 and no application restore (EV-021). The SIEM receives EHR audit, identity, directory, firewall, VPN, EDR, email and cloud logs. Medical devices, OT, the LIS, the other on-premises clinical servers, the interface engine, PACS and the data warehouse are not connected. The MSSP must call within 30 minutes of a high-severity alert (EV-022, EV-023). EHR privacy monitoring alerts cover VIP-flagged patients and employees viewing their own or coworkers' records (EV-024). The interface engine test environment holds copies of production messages containing PHI, and the reference laboratory interface produced 12 patient-matching errors in 2026 (EV-025). DMARC is at p=none, and external sender tagging is off (EV-026).

**Programs and documents.** Five security policies were adopted in 2023 with no review record in 2024 or 2025. Four standards date from 2023, and the library holds no configuration, logging, vendor, medical device or AI standard (EV-027). The request for an IT disaster recovery plan and restore test records for the on-premises clinical servers returned the 2022 unit downtime procedures, the emergency operations plan and the EHR vendor's disaster recovery summary only (EV-028). 12 downtime workstations print from an hourly extract, and no downtime drill has run longer than 4 hours (EV-029). The emergency operations plan and hazard vulnerability analysis were last reviewed in 2025. The analysis lists "IT outage" as a short, low-ranked event, and there are no criteria for diversion caused by an IT outage (EV-030). The county full-scale exercise (hurricane, 2026-05) and a mass-casualty tabletop are held each year; no exercise has simulated a multi-day EHR loss (EV-031). The June 2025 risk analysis was prepared by a consultant and used for the Promoting Interoperability attestation; 7 of its 18 actions were open in June 2026 (EV-032). The 2025 internal IT audit was issued to the audit committee (EV-033). The IT Director was designated Security Officer on 2026-01-05 (EV-034). The 2023 incident response plan is general; the 2025 tabletop tested phishing only (EV-035). The incident log holds 41 incidents for 2025 and 2026 (EV-036). There are 9 privacy incident files, and 2 breaches under 500 were notified in 2025 (EV-037). Training completion is 96% for employees and 82% for contracted clinicians; affiliated practice users and agency nurses have no training assignment; the June 2026 phishing click rate was 6.9% (EV-038). 11 sanctions were recorded in 2025-2026 (EV-039).

**Suppliers and contracts.** Accounts payable lists about 1,400 active vendors, including the AI scribe and coding assistance vendors (EV-040). Compliance flags about 210 vendors with PHI access; BAAs were located for 168 and not for 42. The AI scribe vendor's standard BAA permits de-identified data use, and the hospital is itself the business associate of 18 practices (EV-041). Vendor files hold onboarding questionnaires and SOC 2 reports received at onboarding, with no reassessment records; the PACS vendor has no SOC 2 report (EV-042). The EHR vendor's SOC 2 Type 2 report (12 months ending 2026-03-31) states RTO 12 hours and RPO 15 minutes (EV-043). The cloud provider's report and shared responsibility descriptions are on file (EV-044). The practice services agreements commit to restoring practice access within 8 hours of EHR availability and to notice within 5 business days of confirming an incident (EV-045). The cyber insurance policy has a $15 million limit and a $500,000 retention, with a hotline call required first (EV-046). The hospital is licensed for 112 beds with no substance use disorder program, participates in Medicare and Florida Medicaid, and uses one clearinghouse for all payers (EV-047). The employee health plan is fully insured (EV-048). The two largest practices and their ACO asked for a SOC 2 Type 2 report (EV-049).

**Facilities and media.** Badge readers protect the data center, pharmacy, nursery, ED entrance and 32 network closets (EV-050). Certificates of destruction are on file for IT media; 2 of the 6 device returns in 2026 have a sanitization certificate (EV-051). The data center is on the generator-backed critical branch with redundant cooling (EV-052).

**Business volume.** Revenue was $100.0 million in FY2025, about $274,000 a day: inpatient about $52 million, emergency about $14 million, outpatient surgery and endoscopy about $12 million, outpatient imaging and laboratory about $14 million (outpatient center about $9 million) and other about $8 million (EV-053). The hospital had about 42,000 ED visits (about 115 a day), 7,800 admissions, 950 births and 6,200 surgical and endoscopy cases, and the EHR holds records for about 310,000 individuals (EV-054).

**AI tools and HIM.** Discovery found 6 AI and decision support uses: the sepsis prediction model (switched on in the November 2025 EHR upgrade), the FDA-cleared imaging triage software, the ED ambient scribe pilot, the EHR rule library (186 rules), coding assistance, and staff use of public generative AI sites. No AI inventory, review, local validation or Section 1557 record was provided (EV-055). HIM flags Part 2 records received from outside programs for the Privacy Officer (EV-056).

**PACS, help desk and organization.** The PACS vendor administers the application and its server operating systems with its own support accounts, study access is logged in the application only, and the triage module's model version is logged on each study (EV-071). Help desk and interface queue reports cover hospital and practice users (EV-072). The organization chart shows the vCISO reporting line, and a responsibility matrix covers the affiliated practice service (EV-073).

**Reconciliation across sources.**
- **Workforce.** The 600 employees on the HR roster each have an identity provider account (EV-001, EV-003). Non-employee users come from the Medical Staff Office, staffing and practice rosters (EV-005); because their accounts carry no end date, the rosters were matched against active accounts in P03 fieldwork, which found 19 accounts of people who had left (EV-062).
- **Endpoints.** The endpoint console counts (960 workstations and laptops, 380 smartphones, 120 scanners) match the SSP boundary and the P07 sampling population (EV-010).
- **Medical devices.** The maintenance inventory lists about 1,650 networked devices and the security inventory about 1,150, so about 500 devices (30%) have no security attributes (EV-013). That difference stays an open request; P07 passive capture found 64 more devices missing from the security inventory (EV-CM-8).
- **Network closets.** Network documentation lists 41 closets (EV-014) and the badge system has readers on 32 (EV-050), so 9 closets use keys. The P03 walk-through confirmed the 9 keyed closets and found no key log (EV-063).
- **Vendors.** About 1,400 vendors in accounts payable, about 210 of them flagged with PHI access, 168 with BAAs located (EV-040, EV-041). The named vendors and a summary row in [`vendor-register.csv`](vendor-register.csv) add up to the about 210 and the 168.
- **Remote access.** 32 platform vendors and 14 VPN vendors make the 46 in the remote access inventory (EV-015).
- **AI tools.** Two AI vendors in accounts payable (scribe, coding), the triage software bought with the PACS integration, and the sepsis model and rule library that came with the EHR give the 5 purchased or bundled tools in [`asset-inventory.csv`](asset-inventory.csv). Public generative AI use comes from the department survey only (EV-040, EV-055).
