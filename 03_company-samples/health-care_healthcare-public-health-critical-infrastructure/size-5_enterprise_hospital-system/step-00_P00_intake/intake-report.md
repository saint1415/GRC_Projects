## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the hospital system's own records before the BIA, the enterprise risk analysis and the regulatory analysis began on 2026-05-04, and before Internal Audit's control assessment kickoff on 2026-06-22. It covers the organization (8 hospitals, including H-08, acquired on 2025-10-01; 3 freestanding EDs; 46 clinics; 4 imaging centers; 2 data centers; 2 clouds), the systems that create, receive, maintain or transmit ePHI or support tier-1 processes, the vendors that touch them, and the rules that may bind the system and its two service lines (SL-1 affiliate EHR hosting and SL-2 tele-critical care). Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (BIA interviews, risk workshops, regulatory analysis samples and walk-throughs, Internal Audit tests) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

At this size the sources are enterprise systems of record across the hospitals and business units: the identity platform, the HR system and the medical staff credentialing system, the CMDB and the clinical engineering asset system, the EDR, endpoint and network consoles, the cloud consoles and the SIEM, the third-party risk register, the accounts payable vendor master and the contract repository, board and committee records, prior Internal Audit and SOX workpapers, regulator correspondence, and counsel's applicability memo.

Each register row names the owning business unit's system of record and owner. The [asset inventory](asset-inventory.csv) reconciles the CMDB and the clinical engineering system with the EDR and endpoint consoles, the cloud account inventory, the network platform and the AI governance register; the [vendor register](vendor-register.csv) reconciles the third-party risk register with the accounts payable vendor master and the contract repository.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the regulatory analysis (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**Identity and access.** About 19,500 workforce identities have unique identifiers and MFA registered; the about 640 H-08 accounts sit in the H-08 legacy directory, which is not federated and is run by a local IT support firm with disable dates entered by hand (EV-001, EV-007). MFA is required for remote, affiliate, cloud and privileged access, with badge tap plus PIN on site and FIDO2 keys for privileged roles; most of the workforce uses push MFA with number matching (EV-002). Privileged access to ECIS servers and databases is just in time through PAM with session recording; there are 22 privileged ECIS accounts. About 265 of about 1,150 service accounts (23%) are not in the vault, and 31 integration engine service passwords were last changed more than 2 years ago (EV-003). Joiner, mover and leaver events flow from the HR and credentialing systems; agency and contracted accounts carry end dates taken from staffing agency notices (EV-004, EV-006). HR holds about 12,000 employees; about 2,600 practitioners hold privileges and about 1,100 agency and contracted staff work on a typical day under 42 agency contracts (EV-005, EV-006). About 1,450 SL-1 affiliate accounts at about 70 practices are managed by the practices and sit outside the quarterly workforce certification (EV-008). About 19,000 EHR users map to security classes, 38 hold build or migration roles, and a sensitive-record flag exists for H-03 Part 2 records (EV-009).

**Endpoints, devices and network.** Outside H-08, 97% of endpoints report an EDR agent; at H-08 about 330 of about 1,100 endpoints report none (EV-010). Laptops at H-01 to H-07 are encrypted; about 160 H-08 endpoints report no disk encryption (EV-011). The device inventory holds about 88% of an estimated 41,000 networked medical devices, with passive discovery at 6 of 8 hospitals and H-08 assets taken from the due diligence list; about 2,600 devices run unsupported operating systems, 140 of them feeding the ECIS (EV-012). NAC is enforced at H-01 to H-05, device VLANs exist at 7 of 8 hospitals, H-08 runs one flat network on a legacy site VPN, and 19 clinics have a single carrier (EV-013). The H-08 VPN rules let the H-08 subnet reach the integration engine subnet with broad port ranges, and HL7 over MLLP inside the data center integration segment runs without TLS (EV-014). Scans run weekly against a 15-day standard for critical findings, and the 2026-03 penetration test covered the external surfaces and an internal segment (EV-015). Of 7 remote access paths, 3 device and analyzer vendor platforms use always-on vendor tools outside PAM (EV-016). OT is segmented at 6 hospitals and not at H-07 or H-08 (EV-017).

**Cloud, logging, backup and recovery.** Both clouds use account vending, guardrails as code and daily posture scans; the isolated recovery environment account is in build (EV-018, EV-024). EHR databases back up nightly and every 15 minutes to an immutable vault in a separate account with a 35-day retention lock and a weekly offline copy at DC-2 (EV-019). Databases, backups and BCA caches are encrypted at rest (EV-020). The SIEM does not list the H-08 legacy EHR, the clinical device integration gateways or building OT as log sources; logs are kept 13 months online and 6 years in a write-once archive (EV-021). The SOC runs 24x7 with MSSP overflow (EV-022). Failover to DC-2 on 2026-03-21 took 2.6 hours; a full restore from the vault on 2026-04-18 took 41 hours (EV-023). Contingency plan v6 covers failover, BCA downtime and restoration, and its diversion section refers only to the H-01 protocol (EV-024). About 900 BCA computers serve every unit and ED, with monthly check logs at 6 of 8 hospitals (EV-025). A 5-hour network outage at H-06 on 2026-01-22 stopped EHR access there; no after-action report for it is in the emergency program binder (EV-026). ED fallback communications (analog lines, EMS radio) are tested one hospital at a time (EV-027).

**Governance, prior assurance and disclosure.** The policy portal holds the policy hierarchy in force with an exception register and attestation reports (EV-028). The 2025 enterprise risk analysis rolled into the ERM register, with H-08 entered from due diligence data (EV-029). The board risk committee received the CISO's report in each of the last 4 quarters and approved the risk appetite in 2026-02 (EV-030). Internal Audit's 2025 ECIS assessment and the FY2025 SOX IT general control testing are on file with their workpapers (EV-031). The affiliated covered entity and officer designations were updated to include H-08 on 2025-11-14 (EV-032). The disclosure committee has 8 members, including the COO, who joined in 2026; the playbook's quantitative thresholds are dated before the H-08 acquisition, it was exercised in a 2025 tabletop, and the minutes record no session with hospital incident command (EV-033). The FY2025 Form 10-K includes Item 1C (EV-034). The 2026-02-26 ransomware tabletop involved H-01 and H-02 clinical leaders, without the disclosure committee or EMS (EV-035).

**Workforce and privacy records.** Annual training, quarterly phishing exercises and monthly reminders are recorded; annual downtime training is required for about 3,900 charge nurses, ED staff and unit clerks (EV-036). The Privacy Office recorded 37 sanctions in 2025, keeps four-factor breach assessments and notification files, submitted the 2025 small-breach log to HHS on 2026-02-17, and reviews exported H-08 access reports weekly (EV-037).

**Third parties, contracts and service lines.** The vendor register lists about 1,500 vendors, 430 with PHI, all tiered; 41 tier-1 and tier-2 vendors with PHI are past their reassessment date (EV-038, EV-039). The BAA template requires breach notice within 10 days and tier-1 security incident notice within 72 hours; there are no federal procurement contracts (EV-040). The primary clearinghouse carries about 80% of claims (about $74 million a week); the secondary clearinghouse contract has no surge clause, and there is no record of a payer-portal fallback test (EV-041). SOC reports are on file for both clouds, the DC-2 colocation provider, the primary clearinghouse, the productivity suite, the tele-ICU vendor and the ERP vendor (EV-042). SL-1 serves about 70 practices under hosting agreements with 99.9% availability and 10-day breach notice, and has a SOC 2 Type 1 report as of 2025-12-31 (EV-043). SL-2 serves 14 partner hospitals with 15-minute interruption notice, a single virtual care center at H-01 and no system description (EV-044); clinicians at 4 partner hospitals sign in with local accounts without MFA (EV-045); partner notices do not describe remote monitoring by the system (EV-046). The payer agreements cover the hospitals' own standard transactions (EV-047).

**Facilities and disposal.** Data centers use badge and biometric entry with quarterly access reviews; IT closet door alarms are installed at H-01 to H-05 (EV-048). DC-1 has 96 hours of generator fuel and DC-2, more than 300 miles away in Georgia, 72 hours (EV-049). Media are wiped or destroyed with certificates (EV-050).

**Emergency preparedness and EMTALA.** The unified program covers all 8 hospitals; the 2025 hazard vulnerability analyses list hurricanes, surge and utility loss and do not score a cyberattack or a multi-hospital IT outage; an IT-outage strategy appears only in the H-01 annex; the contact roster has no FBI, CISA or HHS cyber contacts (EV-051). Of 11 dedicated emergency departments, only H-01 has IT-outage diversion criteria agreed with county EMS (EV-052).

**Business volume and structure.** FY2025 revenue was about $4.8 billion, about $13.2 million per calendar day, with Medicare about 44% (EV-053). The system sees about 1,530 ED visits and 310 ambulance arrivals a day (EV-054). It runs 8 hospitals with 1,970 licensed beds, each laboratory holds a CLIA certificate, and no hospital has a transplant program (EV-055). H-08 converts to the ECIS on 2027-03-01; its legacy EHR vendor backs up every 4 hours under a contract with a 24-hour RTO (EV-056).

**ECIS and clinical records.** Changes go through the clinical change advisory board, and emergency changes need retrospective approval within 5 business days (EV-057). Audit generation, signatures and file integrity monitoring are configured on the ECIS (EV-058). About 640 interfaces are validated with error queues reviewed every shift; results are not reconciled automatically between analyzer, middleware and the EHR (EV-059). SSP version 2.0 and the configuration management plan are on file (EV-060). Laboratory critical value and corrected report records (EV-061), HIM back-entry records (EV-062) and the records retention schedule (EV-064) are on file. H-03's Part 2 procedures were last revised before the 2024 rule changes (EV-063).

**Obligations.** Counsel's memo gives a view on each candidate rule (EV-065), and the state breach matrix was updated in 2026-03 to add Alabama (EV-066). The employee health plan is a separate covered entity (EV-067). The cyber insurance policy requires an OFAC check and insurer consent before any ransom payment (EV-068). All 8 hospitals attested for Promoting Interoperability in 2025, H-08 on its own certified EHR (EV-069). The results are in [`obligations-register.csv`](obligations-register.csv).

**Other operating records.** Threat intelligence summaries cover ransomware against hospitals, third-party and clearinghouse attacks, and device exploitation (EV-070). The common control catalog names CCP-01 to CCP-10, and monthly metric packages go to the executive risk committee (EV-071). Email filtering, DMARC and data loss prevention are configured (EV-072). Analytics sandboxes have no retention rule (EV-073). ERP bank changes need call-back and dual approval (EV-074). The portal requires identity verification at enrollment and offers MFA (EV-075).

**AI.** The AI governance committee register lists 11 use cases, 8 with a completed review; AI-008, AI-010 and AI-012 await review, and the minutes record no review of the sepsis model version 2 update (EV-076). Since 2026-03, AI features without an inventory ID are blocked at procurement and change control, and unapproved generative AI domains are blocked (EV-077). Sepsis model version 2 went live on 2026-04-14 with the routine EHR upgrade; its inputs include date of birth, and version 1 measured 76% sensitivity in 2025 (EV-078).
