## 1. Purpose
Make sure the brokerage spots, contains, and recovers from security incidents quickly; protects client money first; and meets every notice deadline.

## 2. Scope
All employees and contractor sales associates, the MSP when it acts for the brokerage, and every system and copy of brokerage information. A "security incident" includes any attempted or successful unauthorized access to an account or data, any altered or suspicious payment instruction, a lost or stolen device, client information sent to the wrong person, and any interference with a brokerage system.

## 3. Roles and responsibilities
| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Office Manager | Incident lead; keeps the incident log; with counsel, decides whether a breach notice is required |
| Broker-owner | Backup incident lead; calls the cyber insurer; contacts the bank on any payment issue; decides on outside communications, spending, and escrow dispute notices |
| MSP | Technical response: secures accounts, preserves logs, cleans devices |
| Cyber insurer and its panel vendors | Breach counsel and forensics, engaged through the insurer's hotline |
| Employees and contractor agents | Report suspected incidents at once; never act on a changed payment instruction without verification |

## 4. Policy statements
4.1 The brokerage must keep an incident response runbook for its most likely serious incident, business email compromise targeting closing funds (P08), with a printed contact card (bank fraud desk, insurer hotline, MSP, title company contacts) at the office and at the Office Manager's and Broker-owner's homes. (IR-8; RS.MA-01; benchmark 314.4(h))

4.2 Employees and agents must report any suspected incident to the Office Manager **at once, and within 1 hour at most**, by phone, not by email. If the Office Manager cannot be reached, call the Broker-owner. Examples: a client asks about "new" wire instructions; you typed your password into an unexpected page or approved an MFA prompt you did not start; your mailbox shows rules or sent messages you did not create; a phone or laptop with brokerage email is lost; an email went to the wrong client. (IR-6; RS.MA-02)

4.3 The Office Manager must log every incident and near miss, including the date and time first known and by whom, what happened, what was done, and the outcome. (IR-5; RS.MA-02)

4.4 For any incident that may involve personal information, the Office Manager, with breach counsel, must decide and record whether personal information of Florida residents (or residents of other states) was accessed, and the date of that determination. A decision that notice is not required because the breach will not likely result in identity theft or financial harm must follow consultation with law enforcement, be written, be kept for 5 years, and be sent to the Department of Legal Affairs within 30 days (Fla. Stat. 501.171(4)(c)). (IR-6; RS.AN-03)

4.5 Notices to individuals, the Department of Legal Affairs, and consumer reporting agencies must meet the deadlines in the P08 notification matrix. Breach counsel reviews each notice before it is sent. (IR-6; RS.CO-02; Fla. Stat. 501.171(3)-(5))

4.6 For any suspected account takeover, diverted payment, or data theft, the Broker-owner must call the cyber insurer's hotline before hiring any outside firm, and the MSP must be engaged under its contract. (IR-4; RS.MA-02)

4.7 **Money first.** If money may have gone to the wrong account, the person who learns of it must make sure the sending bank is called within 1 hour to request a recall (for the brokerage's own escrow accounts, the Broker-owner or Office Manager calls the escrow bank's fraud desk; for a client's wire, the client is told to call their bank immediately). An FBI IC3 complaint must be filed the same day. (IR-4; RS.MI-01)

4.8 **Escrow disputes.** If an incident leaves conflicting demands, or good-faith doubt, about funds held in an escrow account, the Broker-owner must notify the Florida Real Estate Commission in writing within 15 business days and start a settlement procedure within 30 business days (Fla. Stat. 475.25(1)(d)1.; r. 61J2-10.032(1)). (IR-6; RS.CO-03)

4.9 **Payment instruction verification.** No one may send, approve, or tell a client to send money based on new or changed payment instructions until the instructions are confirmed by a phone call to a number taken from the contract file, the owner file, or the party's published website, never from the message that carried the instructions. The person who verifies records the call (date, number, name) in the file, and the Broker-owner checks that record before approving an escrow payment. This covers wires to title companies, owner payout bank changes, and refunds. (IA-8; RS.MI-01; Fla. Stat. 475.25(1)(k))

4.10 Vendors that hold brokerage data must report incidents to the Office Manager as their contracts and Fla. Stat. 501.171(6)(a) require. The Office Manager logs each report and handles it under this policy. (IR-6; SA-9)

4.11 The runbook must be tested every year with a tabletop exercise that includes the MSP, and after any real incident that used it. A written lessons-learned summary is due within 30 days of closing any incident that involved client funds, notice, or outside help, and its actions go to the risk register and training. (IR-3; IR-4; ID.IM-02)

4.12 No extortion or ransom payment may be made without the Broker-owner's approval, advice from breach counsel and the insurer, and an OFAC sanctions check. (IR-4)

## 5. Compliance and enforcement
Breaking this policy leads to sanctions under POL-02 A.4. Failing to report is a violation; reporting a mistake in good faith is never sanctioned. Compliance is checked in the annual assessment (P07) and the annual tabletop.

## 6. Exceptions
Exceptions follow POL-02 A.9. No exception may delay a legally required notice or waive the verification rule in 4.9.
