## 1. Applicability
**Primary business line:** residential real estate transactions from contract to closing, including the title and settlement services that Title and Closing provides. Property management and leasing are covered where a rule reaches them (Florida escrow, FCRA, Fla. Stat. 501.171).

### 1.1 Which entity is a "financial institution" under the Safeguards Rule?
The rule applies to financial institutions under FTC jurisdiction: institutions whose business is engaging in an activity that is financial in nature under section 4(k) of the Bank Holding Company Act, including the activities listed in 12 CFR 225.28 and 225.86, when they are **significantly engaged** in it (314.1(b); 314.2(h)(1)). The test was applied to each legal entity and each line of business:

| Entity and line of business | Financial activity? | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Cris Santos Title and Closing, LLC: settlement (closing) services | **Yes** | "An entity that provides real estate settlement services is a financial institution" (314.2(h)(2)(x), citing 12 CFR 225.28(b)(2)(viii)). It is a separately staffed business (112 staff, about 8,400 closings and 24% of group receipts), so it is significantly engaged. The conclusion rests on the settlement and disbursement work; the title insurance agency license by itself is not what makes it a financial institution |
| Cris Santos Company, Inc.: residential sales brokerage | **No** | Real estate brokerage is not listed in 12 CFR 225.28. The nearest listed activity, acting as a finder (314.2(h)(2)(xiii)), excludes any activity that requires a real estate broker license (12 CFR 225.86(d)(1)(iii)(D)) |
| Cris Santos Company, Inc.: property management and leasing | **No** | Leasing is a financial activity only on a nonoperating basis (12 CFR 225.28(b)(3)(i)); the company manages and repairs the homes it leases |
| Mortgage brokerage joint venture (49% owned) | **Yes, for the joint venture itself** | A mortgage broker is a financial institution (314.2(h)(2)(xi)). The joint venture runs on the partner lender's systems under the partner's program; the company only sends consented leads to it (P02 section 8). Out of scope here |

**The parent is still inside the analysis.** Customer information means records about a financial institution's customers "handled or maintained by or on behalf of you or your affiliates" (314.2(d)). The parent runs every system Title and Closing uses, so it maintains that information on Title and Closing's behalf as its **affiliate and service provider** (314.2(r)). Two consequences:
- The Qualified Individual (the Security Manager) is a parent employee, so 314.4(a)(1)-(3) applies: Title and Closing must retain responsibility, designate a senior member to oversee the Qualified Individual, and **require the parent to maintain a compliant program** (rows G-004 to G-006).
- Title and Closing must oversee the parent like any service provider (314.4(f)).

**Decision.** The company runs **one enterprise program** for both entities and applies it to every system in the TMCC boundary (P02), because the same email, identity provider, and devices carry Title and Closing's customer information, brokerage client data, and tenant data. An information system includes one "connected to a system containing customer information" (314.2(j)), which brings company email and the transaction platform in. Contractor agents who access those systems are authorized users (314.2(a)).

**Which customers.** A consumer who obtains real estate settlement services has a customer relationship with Title and Closing (314.2(e)(2)(i)(K)). The rule covers all customer information in its possession, including information about lenders' borrowers in closing packages (314.1(b)).

**Size exception.** 314.6 exempts institutions with customer information on fewer than 5,000 consumers from 314.4(b)(1), (d)(2), (h), and (i). Title and Closing holds customer information on about 98,000 consumers, so **the full rule applies**. FTC notice under 314.4(j) applies to notification events involving at least 500 consumers.

### 1.3 FinCEN residential real estate reporting rule (31 CFR 1031.320)
- **Status as of 2026-10-06: not in effect.** FinCEN's website states that on March 19, 2026 the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Texas vacated the rule, that FinCEN and the Department of Justice have appealed, and that while the order remains in force reporting persons are not required to file and are not subject to liability. A Federal Register search for FinCEN documents from 2026-03-01 to 2026-10-06 found no rescission or new effective date. The rule text remains in eCFR.
- **Who would report.** The reporting person cascade starts with the person listed as the closing or settlement agent on the closing or settlement statement (1031.320(c)(1)(i)). It would reach **Title and Closing**, not the brokerage as such.
- **Plan.** P01 R-046 accepts the risk at Low; if the appeal restores the rule, Title and Closing will build a reporting procedure from SYS-02 data.

## 2. Method
1. **Requirements.** Each paragraph of 16 CFR 314.3, 314.4, and 314.6 became a row, split where one paragraph holds separable duties (for example, encryption in transit and at rest in 314.4(c)(3), and the three affiliate conditions in 314.4(a)(1)-(3)). The Florida rows cover the provisions that bear on how funds are received, held, verified, disbursed, and reconciled, and on data security and disposal; non-cyber parts (such as interest-bearing account rules) were left out.
2. **Requirement type.** The Safeguards Rule has no required/addressable split. Its elements are mandatory, with three built-in alternatives: compensating controls for encryption approved by the Qualified Individual (314.4(c)(3)), written approval of equivalent controls instead of MFA (314.4(c)(5)), and continuous monitoring instead of annual penetration testing and six-monthly vulnerability assessments (314.4(d)(2)). **No alternative is relied on today.** The Qualified Individual decided not to approve an MFA equivalent for contractor agents, and the company follows the periodic testing path.
3. **Crosswalk.** Each row is mapped to CSF 2.0 and SP 800-53 Rev. 5. **This is an author mapping.** No official NIST mapping of 16 CFR 314 or of the Florida rules was found.
4. **Evidence sampling.** Where a requirement operates many times, a random sample was tested from a system-generated population, using the co-sourced internal audit firm's attribute sampling table (25 items for a moderate-risk control operating many times a year; 40 for high-risk funds controls):
   - sales escrow deposits: 25 of about 3,800;
   - deposit verification requests: 25;
   - title trust account wires: 40 of about 35,000 (dual approval, callback record, payee edits);
   - contractor agent departures: 25 of 410; employee terminations: 25 of 96; transfers: 25 of 40;
   - service provider contracts: 20 of 42;
   - escrow reconciliations: 12 of 12 months for the sales and property management escrow accounts; 20 daily title trust reconciliations;
   - outbound emails from Title and Closing: 30;
   - destruction certificates: 10;
   - incidents: 10 of 14;
   - company laptops for encryption: 30.
   Each `evidence` cell names the sample and its result.
5. **Status.** Each row was rated Met, Partially met, Not met, or Not applicable. Gaps were rated with the P01 risk scale.

**Reading the results.** The company has a defined program with gaps in scale. The risk assessment rows are all Met, and Title and Closing's own wires are well controlled. The gaps concentrate in four places:
- **contractor agents** (MFA, offboarding, training, reporting duty): 314.4(c)(1)(i), (c)(5), (e)(1), (j)(2);
- **SaaS monitoring and payee integrity**: 314.4(c)(8) and Fla. Stat. 626.8473(4);
- **the portal's development and the affiliate structure**: 314.4(c)(4) and (a)(3), two of the four Not met rows;
- **retention and disposal**: 314.4(c)(6), the other two Not met rows.

## 6. Pending changes to watch
- **Safeguards Rule.** A Federal Register search for documents affecting 16 CFR Part 314 from 2023-12-01 to 2026-10-06 found none. The last change was the notification amendment (88 FR 77499, Nov. 13, 2023).
- **FinCEN residential real estate rule.** Vacated, with the government's appeal pending (section 1.3). The FinCEN row carries a watch note.
- **HUD disparate impact rule.** HUD proposed on 2026-01-14 (91 FR 1475) and in a supplemental proposal on 2026-08-10 (91 FR 51416, comments close 2026-10-09) to change its implementation of the Fair Housing Act's disparate impact standard, including removing its discriminatory effects regulation (24 CFR 100.500). No final rule had been published as of 2026-10-06. This affects P10, not the Safeguards Rule.
