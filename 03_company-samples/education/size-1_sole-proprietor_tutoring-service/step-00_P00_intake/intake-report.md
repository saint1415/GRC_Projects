## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the business's own records before the self-assessment began on 2026-07-13. A one-person tutoring business has no HR system, asset database or internal audit, so the systems of record are the SaaS vendors' admin consoles and reports, the website and portal pages, the device and router settings screens, the email inbox, the signed agreements folder, bank and card statements, the tax files, and the insurance agent's portal. Intake covers the organization, the systems that collect, store or transmit children's and student information, the vendors that touch them, and the rules that may bind the business. Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (self-reviews, walk-throughs, tests) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the gap analysis (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**Students, sessions and billing.** The client-management SaaS lists 55 active students, 36 in grades 2 to 7 and 19 in grades 8 to 12, all in Florida, with about 32 booked session hours a week in the spring term and about 60% of hours online (EV-001). It bills about $3,500 a week, or about $700 per weekday; most families prepay 10-session packages; card payments run through the processor's hosted page, and the account holds no full card numbers (EV-002). The vendor enforces MFA on the owner's administrator account, and the owner's phone holds the only second factor (EV-003). Every active family has an e-signed enrollment agreement. It says family information is used to teach the child, mentions the portal in one line, and has a photo and recording permission checkbox. It does not list what the portal collects, how it is used, or who receives it (EV-004).

**Email and student files.** The email and files suite is a paid business plan with one user, the owner, who is also the administrator. MFA is available and turned off, version history lasts 30 days, desktop sync covers every student folder, and no other backup service is connected (EV-005). Storage holds folders for about 265 students (55 current, about 210 former since 2019, none deleted). About 39 folders hold IEP or 504 plans or evaluations, and a spreadsheet lists every portal user name and password (EV-006). 12 student folders are shared through "anyone with the link" links (EV-007). The browser saves the business passwords, two of them reused, and no password manager is installed (EV-008).

**Website and student portal.** The builder's administrator sign-in is password only, with MFA offered and turned off. The portal launched in January 2025, and reading-aloud audio uploads were added in August 2025 (EV-009). The member list has 61 enabled student accounts: 34 current and 27 for students who stopped tutoring. 2 of the 36 families with children under 13 have no account, and the owner set every member password (EV-010). The members area is built for grades 2 to 7 and collects names, user names, photos of work, reading audio and messages. No children's privacy notice or link appears on any page, and the sign-up form requires the child's birth date and school name (EV-011). Site-wide analytics sets cookies on the members pages too, and the builder's standard terms let it use analytics data to improve its services (EV-012).

**Online sessions.** The video platform account signs in with a password only, with MFA available and turned off. Cloud recordings are stored for 11 families, none deleted, and the automatic deletion setting is off. 3 of those 11 families did not tick the recording permission checkbox (EV-013, EV-004).

**Devices, network and paper.** The laptop has one user account, the owner's, which family members also use, with games they installed. Full-disk encryption is off; antivirus, automatic updates and the host firewall are on; the screen locks after 15 minutes; the downloads folder holds student files; no backup is set up; and the IT technician's remote-support tool is installed (EV-014). The phone is encrypted, locks after 1 minute, holds parent text threads and the second factors for two services, and syncs photos of student work to a personal photo cloud (EV-015). The router admin password is the label default, and the laptop shares the network with family phones and smart-home devices; a guest network is available and off (EV-016). The accounting SaaS has MFA on and holds parent names and payment amounts only (EV-017). Paper is kept in a closed cabinet and cross-cut shredded; the laptop replaced in 2024 is in a desk drawer with student files on it; the business laptop sits open on the family room table between sessions (EV-020).

**AI tools.** A free personal AI assistant account, with chat history and model-improvement use turned on, holds chats since March 2026 with student first names, grades, scores and accommodations: support levels for 31 students, boot camp starting levels for 12, and about 45 draft progress reports (EV-018). Its consumer terms let the vendor use chats to improve its models (EV-019). No payment to an AI vendor appears in the statements (EV-023).

**Vendors and contracts.** On file are the IT technician's confidentiality and data-handling agreement (signed 2026-07-08), the client-management SaaS terms with a data processing addendum, the tax preparer's engagement letter, and subscription terms. No data processing addendum or written security assurance was located for the website builder, the video platform or the AI assistant vendor, and no record of a vendor review (EV-021). The client-management SaaS vendor's SOC 2 Type 2 report covers Security and Availability for the 12 months ending 2026-03-31, with an unqualified opinion and one remediated exception; its system description states RTO 12 hours and RPO 1 hour (EV-022). The former web designer's last invoice and handover email date from December 2024 (EV-028).

**Finances and insurance.** Statements show recurring payments to five SaaS vendors, hourly payments to the IT technician, the tax preparer's fee and a general liability premium, and no cyber insurance premium. Deposits are card settlements from parents, with no payment from a school, district or government agency (EV-023). Gross receipts were about $180,000 in 2025, with no wages paid (EV-024). The insurance agent confirmed that the general liability policy does not cover data breach costs (EV-026).

**Documents and parent requests.** No written security program, policy, risk assessment, retention policy, incident plan, contact list, children's privacy notice, vendor review, training record, or sign-in history review was found (EV-025). One parent asked in 2025 what the portal keeps and received an informal email answer (EV-027).
