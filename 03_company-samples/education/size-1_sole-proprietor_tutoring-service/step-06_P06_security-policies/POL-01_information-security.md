## 1. Purpose
Protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information about the children and families the business serves, and meet the COPPA Rule and Florida law in a way one person can actually run. Each rule below is written so it can be checked (P07).

## 2. Scope
All business information in any form (electronic, paper, spoken), including personal information collected online from children through the student portal and online sessions, and records parents provide (report cards, IEP and 504 plans, evaluations). It covers every system in the Core Business SaaS Stack (SYS-01 to SYS-09), paper at home, and every vendor that handles this information. It applies to the owner-tutor and to any future employee, substitute tutor, or volunteer. Contractors are bound through their agreements.

## 3. Roles and responsibilities
| Role | Held by | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Information security program coordinator (312.8(b)(1)) and privacy contact for parents | Owner-tutor | Runs this policy; answers parent requests; decides breach questions; keeps records |
| Risk acceptor | Owner-tutor | Accepts or treats every risk (section 4.4) |
| On-call IT technician | Contractor under a confidentiality and data-handling agreement | Technical help on request; no standing access |
| Vendors | Email and files suite, client-management SaaS, website builder, video platform, accounting SaaS | Operate their safeguards under written assurances (section 6) |
| Emergency contact | A designated family member | Holds the sealed emergency sheet (11.2); may only notify families |

Because one person writes, follows, and checks these rules, independence is limited. Section 4.5 adds an outside check where it is affordable.

## 4. Governance and risk
4.1 The business must maintain a written information security program made up of this policy, the system profile (P02), and the risk register (P01), with safeguards appropriate to the sensitivity of children's information and the size of the business. (PM-1; GV.PO-01; 312.8(b))
4.2 The owner-tutor is designated in writing, by this policy, as the coordinator of the information security program and the privacy contact for parents. (PM-2; GV.RR-02; 312.8(b)(1))
4.3 A risk assessment must be completed every July and after any major change (a new system, vendor, portal feature, or hire), using NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1. Every risk must have a treatment and a due date. (RA-3; PM-9; 312.8(b)(2)-(3))
4.4 Risk decisions: Low risks may be accepted with a written reason. Moderate risks may be accepted only with a dated plan or a written reason. High and Very High risks must be treated with a dated plan and must not be accepted as they are. A High risk to children's information must have its free fixes in place before the next term starts. (PM-9; GV.RM-01)
4.5 Safeguards must be tested every July (P07) and monitored monthly (7.7). At least every second year, the test must include review by someone outside the business, such as the IT technician. (CA-2; CA-7; 312.8(b)(4))
4.6 This policy and the program must be evaluated and updated at least every July, and after test results, incidents, or material changes. (PL-1; GV.PO-02; 312.8(b)(5))

## 5. Children's privacy
5.1 **Online notice.** A children's privacy notice must be linked, clearly labeled, from the website home page, the portal sign-in page, and next to every upload and message form. It must give the business's name, address, phone, and email; what the portal and online sessions collect from children; how it is used; that it is disclosed to no third party; the service providers that support the portal and sessions; the retention policy (Appendix A); and how parents review, delete, or stop collection. (PT-5; GV.OC-03; 312.4(d); 312.10)
5.2 **Direct notice and consent.** Before a child gets a portal account or a session is recorded, a parent must receive the direct notice (312.4(c)(1) content) and give consent by e-signing the consent form and paying by card in the same enrollment record in the client-management SaaS. Both records are kept for as long as the student is enrolled plus 3 years. (PT-4; 312.4(b)-(c); 312.5(a)-(b))
5.3 **Material changes.** No new kind of information (for example video, location, or a new upload type) may be collected from children until every affected parent has received a new direct notice and consented. (PT-4; 312.4(b); 312.5(a)(1))
5.4 **No third-party disclosure.** Children's personal information must not be disclosed to any third party, including AI tools, advertisers, or other families. Service providers may receive it only to run the portal or sessions and only under section 6. (PT-4; SA-9; 312.5(a)(2))
5.5 **Collect only what is needed.** The portal must ask a child only for what the activity needs: a first and last name, a user name, and the work submitted. No birth date, school name, location, or contact information from the child. Site analytics must be off for the members area. (SI-12(1); ID.AM-07; 312.7; 312.5(c)(7))
5.6 **Parent requests.** A parent may ask what is held about their child, review it, have it deleted, or stop further collection at any time. The owner must verify the request comes from the parent email or phone on file, answer within 10 business days, follow the deletion checklist across every system (SYS-01, SYS-03, SYS-04, SYS-06), and log the request. (SI-18(4); 312.6)
5.7 **Recording.** An online session may be recorded only when the parent's recording consent is on file and the owner says at the start that the session is being recorded. Recordings are deleted automatically after 30 days. (PT-4; SI-12; Fla. Stat. 934.03(2)(d); 312.10)

## 6. Vendors
6.1 **No assurances, no children's data.** Before a vendor collects or keeps children's personal information or student records for the business, the owner must check that it can protect the information (for example a SOC 2 report, a security page, or a questionnaire) and obtain written assurances in its terms or a data processing addendum that it will use reasonable security, use the data only to serve the business, and delete it on request. (SA-9; GV.SC-05; 312.8(c))
6.2 The owner must keep a vendor list showing each vendor, the data it holds, and where its assurances are filed. (SA-9; ID.AM-07)
6.3 The client-management SaaS vendor's SOC 2 report must be reviewed every year, and the owner must operate the customer controls it lists. (SA-9; GV.SC-07)
6.4 Remote support sessions must be started and watched by the owner. Unattended remote access must be off. (AC-17)

## 7. Access control
7.1 The owner must use a personal named account in every service and on the laptop. Credentials must never be shared. Family members must not use the owner's laptop account. (IA-2; AC-2; PR.AA-01)
7.2 MFA must be on for every service that offers it, starting with the email and files suite, the website builder, the video platform, the client-management SaaS, and the accounting SaaS. (IA-2(1); IA-2(2); PR.AA-03; 312.8(b)(3))
7.3 Passwords must be unique passphrases of at least 14 characters, stored in a password manager. Account recovery codes are kept in the password manager and the sealed envelope (11.2). (IA-5)
7.4 Students' portal passwords are set by their parents at account creation. The owner must not keep a list of them. (IA-5; Fla. Stat. 501.171(2))
7.5 Portal member accounts must be closed within 30 days after a student stops tutoring. Collaborator accounts for contractors must be removed when the work ends. The owner reviews portal members and collaborators at the start of each term. (AC-2; PR.AA-05; 312.10)
7.6 Files must be shared only with named parents, never by an open link. (AC-3; PR.AA-05)
7.7 On the first Monday of each month, the owner must review the sign-in history of the email suite, website builder, video platform, and client-management SaaS and note the review in the security log. (AU-6; DE.AE-02; 312.8(b)(4))
7.8 The laptop must lock after 5 minutes idle; the phone after 1 minute. (AC-11)

## 8. Data handling and retention
8.1 Information is classified in three levels. (RA-2; ID.AM-07)

| Level | Examples | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| **Restricted** | Portal content, recordings, IEP and 504 plans, evaluations, assessments, progress notes, credentials | Approved systems only (8.2); MFA; encrypted devices; deleted on schedule |
| **Confidential** | Parent contact details, invoices, contracts, this policy set | Approved systems only; owner only |
| **Public** | Website pages, rates, schedule of public workshops | No restriction |

8.2 Restricted data may be kept only in the email and files suite (student folders), the client-management SaaS, the website builder (portal), and the video platform (recordings, 30 days). It must not be kept in a personal account, the phone camera roll after 7 days, a personal photo cloud, or a consumer app. (AC-3; SA-9)
8.3 Every device that can hold Restricted data must use full-disk encryption. (SC-28; PR.DS-01; Fla. Stat. 501.171(2))
8.4 Progress information must not be sent by ordinary text message. Use the client-management SaaS messages or the business email. Scheduling texts with no student information are allowed. (SC-8; PR.DS-02)
8.5 Student folders must have a backup that desktop sync cannot overwrite, kept current at least weekly, in addition to the suite's version history. (CP-9; PR.DS-11)
8.6 **Retention.** Information must be kept only for the periods in Appendix A and then deleted. Children's personal information must never be kept indefinitely. The owner runs the deletion at the end of each term and logs it. (SI-12; ID.AM-08; 312.10)
8.7 **Disposal.** Paper with student information must be cross-cut shredded. Old devices must be wiped (encrypted, then reset) before reuse or trade-in, or destroyed by a recycler that gives a certificate. Each disposal is recorded. (MP-6; Fla. Stat. 501.171(8); 312.10)

## 9. Acceptable use and training
9.1 Business devices and accounts are for business work. Family members must use their own device or a separate standard user account with no access to business files. (PL-4)
9.2 Devices must never be left in a vehicle or unattended in a student's home or the library. (PL-4)
9.3 Automatic updates and the built-in antivirus must stay on. Software must come only from official app stores or the vendor's site. (SI-2; SI-3)
9.4 The home router must have a unique admin password; smart-home and guest devices must use the guest network. Away from home, use the phone hotspot rather than open Wi-Fi when opening Restricted data. (SC-7; PR.IR-01)
9.5 **AI tools.** No student's name, initials, school, scores, accommodations, or any portal content may be entered into an AI tool. An AI tool may be used only with de-identified text, only under a business plan whose terms bar training on customer data, and only after a written assessment (P10). Placement, support level, and any other decision about a student is made by the owner, not by an AI tool. (PL-4; SA-9; 312.5(a)(2))
9.6 The owner must complete a security course every year and read monthly security reminders. Any future employee or substitute tutor must be trained and sign this policy before getting access. (AT-2; PR.AT-01)

## 10. Incident response
10.1 The business must keep an incident runbook (P08) with a printed contact list at home and in the teaching bag. (IR-8; RS.MA-01)
10.2 Every suspected incident (lost device, phishing click, misdirected email, strange portal message, vendor notice) must be written in the incident log the same day, with the time it was discovered. (IR-5; IR-6; RS.MA-02)
10.3 Notices to families and the Florida Department of Legal Affairs must meet the deadlines in the P08 notification matrix, confirmed by legal counsel. Plan to the 30-day Florida clock. (IR-6; RS.CO-02; Fla. Stat. 501.171(3)-(4))
10.4 No ransom may be paid without legal counsel's advice and an OFAC sanctions check. (IR-4)
10.5 The runbook must be walked through every year and after any real incident. Lessons learned must be recorded within 30 days of closing an incident. (IR-8)

## 11. Contingency
11.1 Recovery follows the BIA (P05) priorities. A printed weekly schedule and parent contact sheet are kept at home. (CP-2; RC.RP-01)
11.2 A sealed emergency sheet with account recovery codes and instructions for messaging all families must be held by the designated emergency contact. It must not be used to read student records. (CP-2; AC-2)
11.3 The owner must keep a written arrangement with another independent tutor to take urgent SAT and ACT students for up to four weeks if the owner cannot teach. (CP-2)

## 12. Exceptions and compliance
12.1 Exceptions must be written, risk-rated under 4.4, recorded in the risk register, and expire within 12 months. (PL-1)
12.2 Any future employee, substitute tutor, or volunteer who breaks this policy must be dealt with in proportion to intent and harm (retraining, written warning, or ending the engagement). A vendor that breaks its assurances is dealt with under its terms, up to ending the service. (SA-9)
12.3 Compliance is checked in the annual control assessment (P07) and the monthly review in 7.7. Security documentation (this policy, risk assessments, assessments, incident records, consent records, deletion and disposal logs) is kept for at least 3 years.
