## 1. Purpose
Establish the enterprise information security program, which is the written information security program required by the FTC Safeguards Rule (16 CFR 314.3(a)); assign accountability from the board to every workforce member; set the policy hierarchy and the exceptions process; and give every other security policy its authority. The program protects the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of student, customer, and company information and supports the company's Safeguards Rule, FERPA, Title IV, SEC, and state law obligations.

## 2. Scope
All Cris Santos Company workforce members (employees, including full-time and adjunct faculty, contractors, student workers, and volunteers) at headquarters, the 23 campuses, and the 4 student support centers, and all remote workers in any state. Covers all systems and data, including cloud, colocation, SaaS, and systems that vendors and the Title IV third-party servicer operate for the company, and the services the company offers to other organizations (SL-1 Workforce Education Services and SL-2 Online Program Services).

## 4. Policy statements
Each statement is testable and tagged with its SP 800-53 control(s) and CSF 2.0 subcategory. `policy-control-map.csv` traces each statement to its regulatory driver and shows whether Internal Audit tested it in 2026 (P07).

4.1 The company must maintain an information security program aligned to NIST CSF 2.0 that serves as its written information security program under 16 CFR 314.3(a), documented in this policy hierarchy and in system security plans for tier-1 systems. (PM-1; PL-2; GV.PO-01)
4.2 The CISO must be designated in writing by the board risk committee as the Qualified Individual who oversees, implements, and enforces the program. (PM-2; GV.RR-02)
4.3 An enterprise risk assessment must be performed at least annually and after any material change (a new service line or partner institution, a new program modality, a new AI feature that uses student data, or an acquisition), using NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1, and rolled up into the enterprise risk register following NIST IR 8286 Rev. 1. (RA-3; PM-9; GV.RM-01; ID.RA-01)
4.4 Risk may be accepted only at the authority levels in STD-01.1: risk owner (Low), CISO with the accountable executive (Moderate), executive risk committee (High), CEO and CFO jointly (Very High). Title IV eligibility and student fraud risks at High or above must not be accepted without a dated treatment plan. (PM-9; GV.RM-06)
4.5 Policies must be reviewed at least annually; standards and procedures at least annually or when the controls they describe change. (PL-1; GV.PO-02)
4.6 Any deviation from a policy or standard must be approved through the exception process (section 7) before it takes effect. (PL-1; CA-5; GV.PO-02)
4.7 Workforce members who violate security, privacy, or FERPA policies must be sanctioned under PRC-01.1 in proportion to intent and harm, and each sanction must be documented. (PS-8; GV.RR-04)
4.8 No vendor may receive customer information or education records until its contract requires Safeguards Rule safeguards, FERPA school-official and direct-control terms where the vendor acts as a school official, no training of vendor models on company data, and incident notice within 72 hours, and the vendor has been tiered and assessed under STD-01.3. Vendors must be reassessed at least annually for tier 1 and every two years for tier 2. (SA-9; SR-6; SA-4; GV.SC-05; GV.SC-07)
4.9 Security controls for tier-1 systems and common control providers must be assessed at least annually by an assessor independent of their operation; internet-facing systems that handle customer information must have an independent penetration test at least annually and vulnerability assessments at least every six months and after material changes. (CA-2; CA-2(1); CA-8; RA-5; ID.IM-01; ID.RA-01)
4.10 Changes to systems that hold customer information or education records must follow STD-01.5 and PRC-01.3; emergency changes must be approved by the change advisory board within 2 business days after implementation. (CM-3; CM-4; PR.PS-01; ID.RA-07)
4.11 Security documentation, including risk assessments, assessments, board reports, and incident records, must be retained for at least 7 years from creation or last effective date, whichever is later. (SI-12; GV.PO-02)
4.12 The Qualified Individual must report in writing to the board risk committee at least annually on the overall status of the program and compliance with 16 CFR Part 314, and on material matters (risk assessment, risk decisions, service providers, testing, security events, and recommended changes). The CISO must also report cybersecurity risk to the committee at least quarterly. (PM-9; CA-7; GV.OV-01; GV.OV-02)

## 6. Compliance and enforcement
Compliance is monitored through continuous control monitoring, the annual Internal Audit assessment (P07), access certifications, and the Qualified Individual's annual report to the board. Violations are handled under the sanctions procedure (PRC-01.1, POL-01 statement 4.7), from retraining to termination of employment or contract, in proportion to intent and harm.

## 7. Exceptions
This section defines the exception process for every policy and standard in the hierarchy. Exceptions follow PRC-01.2: requested in the GRC platform, risk-rated with the P01 method, approved at the authority level for the residual risk, recorded in the exception register with compensating controls, and limited to 12 months. Requests that would leave a Title IV eligibility or student fraud risk at High or above are refused unless a dated treatment plan exists. An exception to a Safeguards Rule element that the rule itself allows (encryption under 314.4(c)(3) or MFA under 314.4(c)(5)) also needs the Qualified Individual's written approval of the compensating or equivalent control. Expired exceptions are escalated to the CISO, and the executive risk committee reviews the register monthly. Details are in `policy-hierarchy.md` section 5.
