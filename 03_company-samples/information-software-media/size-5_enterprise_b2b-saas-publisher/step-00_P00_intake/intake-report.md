## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the company's own records before the enterprise risk analysis, the BIA and the regulatory gap analysis began on 2026-06-01, and before Internal Audit's control assessment fieldwork began on 2026-07-13. It covers the organization (12,000 employees, about 9,800 business customers, and the acquired company AQ-01), the systems that hold customer data or support tier-1 processes, the vendors and sub-processors that touch them, the commitments the company makes in contracts and on its trust page, and the rules that may bind the company and its products. Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (BIA interviews, risk workshops, gap analysis samples, Internal Audit tests) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

At this size the sources are enterprise systems of record: the identity platform, PAM and identity governance, the HR system, the cloud organization consoles and posture management, the SIEM and SOC case management, the software factory and secret scanning, the tenant access tool, the third-party risk register and the accounts payable vendor master, the contract repository, the GRC platform, board and committee records, SEC filings, prior Internal Audit and SOX workpapers, the FedRAMP package, and the AI council register.

The [asset inventory](asset-inventory.csv) reconciles the cloud organization inventory with the OCP system documentation, the identity and endpoint consoles and the AI council register; the [vendor register](vendor-register.csv) reconciles the third-party risk register with the accounts payable vendor master and the contract repository.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the gap analysis (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**Identity and access.** Workforce accounts for the 12,000 employees are federated through SSO with a phishing-resistant authenticator registered, except the about 420 AQ-01 staff, who sign in through AQ-01's own identity provider; about 1,100 accounts hold OCP roles (EV-001). Phishing-resistant MFA is required for every workforce sign-in, lockout follows 10 failed attempts in 15 minutes, and session tokens last 12 hours (EV-002). Production access is just in time through PAM, with 214 privileged OCP roles and every elevation logged (EV-003). Joiner, mover and leaver events flow from the HR system; accounts inactive 45 days are disabled; quarterly certification campaigns cover workforce accounts and cloud roles, and their scope lists no non-human identities (EV-004). There are about 46,000 non-human identities, about 17% of them with permissions unused for 90 days (EV-005). HR holds 12,000 employees, about 1,100 in California, and contractor end dates are entered from vendor portal notices (EV-006). The tenant access tool needs a ticket and manager approval for each session and enforces customer approval only for tenants that opted into restricted access (about 9%) (EV-007); 82% of sessions have a recording, and sessions on the legacy console path have none (EV-008). About 38% of tenants do not require MFA for all customer users, and customer administrators can run full exports without step-up authentication (EV-009).

**Endpoints and remote access.** EDR reports on 99% of about 14,500 laptops and test devices, with full-disk encryption, remote wipe and staged EDR update rings (EV-010). All workforce access to production goes through the zero-trust gateway with device posture checks, with no network VPN into production (EV-011).

**Cloud, network, encryption, backup and recovery.** Both clouds use account vending, guardrails as code, daily drift detection and posture scans; the automated inventory holds about 86,000 OCP cloud resources; the AQ-01 cloud organization is not enrolled (EV-012). Network policies deny by default and cells cannot reach each other; public DNS for all product domains runs on one managed DNS provider with no secondary provider (EV-013). Customer data is encrypted at rest with per-tenant keys, with a customer-managed key option for Enterprise customers (EV-014). Backups sit in separate accounts in another region with write-once retention, and restores are tested quarterly per cell (EV-015). In the cell DR test on 2026-05-16, 13 of 14 cells recovered within the 4-hour RTO and Cell 4 (about 1,240 tenants) took 6.5 hours; the Government Edition failed over to its second region on 2026-04-25 (EV-016). Data Cloud monthly snapshots are kept 13 months (EV-017).

**Logging and security operations.** Logs from enrolled accounts, OCP services, the tenant access tool and the export service reach a write-once archive, kept 1 year searchable and 7 years archived. The SIEM use case list includes cross-tenant anomaly detections for 2 of the 5 OCP data stores and no detection for bulk reads of the export bucket, and there are no log sources from the AQ-01 cloud organization (EV-018). The in-house SOC runs 24x7 from three U.S. sites with an incident retainer for forensics and outside counsel (EV-019).

**Platform, software factory and product security.** OCP SSP version 2.1 (2026-02-13) is on the Moderate baseline, with the AQ-01 export added after the acquisition (EV-020). Platform configuration covers the service mesh, tenant-bound sessions and API schema validation (EV-021). About 3,800 repositories have protected branches; 9 legacy OCP pipelines deploy with long-lived static cloud keys, and artifacts are signed and verified at admission (EV-022). Push protection has run on OCP repositories since 2026-04, and each live-secret alert carries detection and revocation dates (EV-023). Critical findings are due in 15 days and emergency patches in 72 hours (EV-024). The 2026-03 third-party penetration test covered multi-tenant isolation and the tenant access tool, and a bug bounty runs continuously (EV-025). The isolation test suite runs on every release across about 400 services (EV-026). Emergency changes need a post-implementation review within 5 business days (EV-027).

**AQ-01 and the export interconnection.** AQ-01 runs in its own cloud organization outside the landing zone with its own identity provider; its CI pipelines use 31 long-lived static access keys older than one year; its cloud audit logs stay at the provider's 90-day default (EV-028). A nightly job exports case transcripts and conversation records for the 1,150 shared customers to a bucket in AQ-01's organization, and the bucket policy lets the AQ-01 cross-account role read every customer prefix (EV-029). The interconnection register has no entry for the AQ-01 export (EV-030). Identity federation is planned for 2027-01-31 and landing zone migration for 2027-03-31; AQ-01 regional recovery has not been tested (EV-031).

**Governance, prior assurance and disclosure.** The 2025 policies, standards and procedures sit in the policy portal in a hierarchy aligned to NIST CSF 2.0, with an exception register (EV-032). The 2025 risk analysis rolled into the ERM register under NIST IR 8286 (EV-033). Internal Audit reports to the audit committee; its 2025 project records show IT auditors helped design the GRC control framework (EV-034). The cybersecurity and risk committee receives quarterly reports and approved the risk appetite in 2026-02 (EV-035). The disclosure committee has 7 members, three of whom joined in 2026; its playbook has no section on how bank, FedRAMP or customer contract notices interact with the materiality determination; the 2025-10-21 tabletop included the committee (EV-036). The FY2025 Form 10-K includes Item 1C (EV-037). Incident response plan v5 has no step for the bank service provider notice or FedRAMP incident communications, and the Government Edition's incident procedure is not linked to it (EV-038). Training records show annual awareness, role-based and phishing training (EV-039). Common controls are published by 9 providers (EV-040). The Operations Cloud holds a SOC 2 Type 2 report for 2024-10-01 to 2025-09-30 and an ISO/IEC 27001 certificate; the Data Cloud has a Security-only SOC 2 report; the Conversational AI service has none (EV-041). The Government Edition holds a FedRAMP Moderate authorization; 6 of its 41 open POA&M items are past their scheduled dates (EV-042).

**Third parties, contracts and public statements.** The vendor register lists about 1,450 vendors and 58 sub-processors; 14 sub-processor SOC report reviews are past their due dates, and three AI model providers were added in 2026 (EV-043). The follow-the-sun support vendor uses agents in two countries that are not countries of concern; its subcontractor list has not been received (EV-044). Payments reconcile to the vendor register (EV-045). The standard DPA sets 48-hour incident notice, 30-day sub-processor notice, deletion within 30 and 90 days, and no cross-customer AI training; about 2,600 older contracts say 72 hours and about 310 negotiated contracts say 24 hours, and the repository has no field for notice terms (EV-046). AQ-01 customer contracts keep pre-acquisition terms that allow model improvement from transcripts, and the AQ-01 model registry lists pooled models trained on shared customers' transcripts (EV-047). The trust page makes three statements on staff access, recording of administrative access and AI training (EV-048). Vendor SOC reports on file cover the cloud providers and key SaaS vendors (EV-049).

**Business volume, customers and data.** FY2025 revenue was about $4.8 billion, 94% subscription, about $13.2 million per calendar day (EV-050). There are about 9,800 customers, including about 140 banking organizations, about 1,100 health care organizations and 45 federal agencies on the Government Edition (EV-051). The OCP runs 14 cells, handles about 2.4 billion API calls and 6 million new cases a day, and met 99.95% availability in each of the last 12 months (EV-052). The CRM holds about 3.4 million business contacts, about 420,000 of them California residents (EV-053). Customer data is classified Restricted and used only to serve that customer, and the data map shows the AQ-01 export and model provider flows (EV-054).

**Retention, continuity and support.** The offboarding job has no failure alert, and the monthly deletion report is not compared with billing's terminated-tenant list (EV-055). Contingency plan v7 covers cell, region and cloud dependency failures; its contingencies do not list the managed DNS provider, the AQ-01 export path or the AI model providers, and its last revision predates the AQ-01 acquisition (EV-056). One telephony provider serves the contact center, and customer assistance requests under the DPA have no defined response time (EV-057).

**AI.** The council register lists 16 use cases, 11 with completed council review; AI-002, AI-006, AI-007, AI-008 and AI-012 are not yet reviewed (EV-058). The AI Assist red-team report records 6 findings, including indirect prompt injection through case content (EV-059). AI Assist is generally available to about 2,300 customers, each feature calls one model provider, and no customer documentation pack exists for decisions about individuals (EV-060). Internal AI tools include the coding assistant, the productivity suite assistant and the HR system's resume matching feature; the web filter blocks unapproved generative AI domains (EV-061).

**Facilities and disposal.** Offices use badge access and visitor logs, and no office hosts production systems (EV-062). Retired laptops are wiped and certified (EV-063).

**Obligations.** Counsel's applicability memo covers each candidate rule (EV-064). The terms prohibit protected health information and place COPPA duties on customers (EV-065). The company holds no carrier or VoIP registration, stores no card numbers, has no Department of Defense contracts, and its Government Edition contracts carry FAR covered telecommunications clauses (EV-066). About 140 banking customers use the Operations Cloud, and a bank-designated incident contact is recorded for some of them (EV-067). Outside counsel's state breach matrix is dated 2025-03 (EV-068). Cyber insurance is in force (EV-069). The results are in [`obligations-register.csv`](obligations-register.csv).
