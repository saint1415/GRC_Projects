## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the business's own records before the self-assessment began on 2026-07-20. A one-person business has no HR system, asset database or internal audit, so the systems of record are the SaaS vendors' admin settings and reports (the ATS and its AI match add-on, email and files, the partner portal, accounting, e-signature), bank and card statements, the 2025 Schedule C, the signed agreements folder, the laptop, phone and router settings screens, the phone carrier's and insurance agent's portals, and a walk-through of the home office. Intake covers the organization, the systems that hold candidate, contractor and client information, the vendors that touch them, and the rules that may bind the business. Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (self-reviews, a vendor call, the mailbox search, tests) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the gap analysis (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**ATS and AI match add-on.** The ATS has one user, the owner's administrator account, with password sign-in and MFA available and turned off. A password reset link goes to the business email address (EV-001). It holds about 3,400 candidate records, the oldest from 2019 and about 1,200 created in 2025, some with ID image attachments, and no retention or deletion rule is set. About 40 job orders were opened in the last 12 months for about 15 Florida clients, each listing 50 to 500 employees (EV-002). The activity log records sign-ins, exports and downloads, and the export page lists no previous full export (EV-003). The vendor's trust page states a SOC 2 Type 2 report is available on request and that customer data is backed up daily. The terms delete customer data 30 days after the subscription ends (EV-004). The AI match add-on was turned on 2026-02-02. Automatic rejection below a score of 40 is set on for two job orders, and the disposition log lists 412 applicants from 2026-04-06 to 2026-06-26, of whom 236 received an automatic rejection email with no reviewer recorded. The model-improvement opt-out is off (EV-005). The add-on terms allow the vendor to use customer data to improve its models unless the opt-out is on, and the help documentation lists the scoring inputs without weights (EV-006). The active job ads do not say that software sorts applications and give no contact for an accommodation or a review by a person (EV-007).

**Email, files and e-signature.** Email and files are on a business plan with one user. The second factor is an SMS code to the owner's phone, the only registered method, and no recovery codes have been generated. The provider keeps a sign-in history and records mailbox rule changes; the optional new-sign-in alert is off. File versions are kept 30 days (EV-008). The mailbox holds about 70 messages from 2019 to 2025 with contractor start forms showing full SSNs and dates of birth, ID images sent by candidates, and two background check reports forwarded by two clients (2025-11 and 2026-03). The sent folder shows start forms sent to the partner as attachments (EV-009). The provider documents platform backups, encryption and 30 days of deleted items (EV-010). The e-signature service signs in through the email account (EV-011).

**Back-office partner.** The partner portal has one recruiter login, signed in with a password and a one-time code sent to the owner's email. The start request form takes the SSN and date of birth and masks the SSN after submission, and background checks show only as "cleared to start" or "not cleared" (EV-012). The back-office services agreement makes the partner the employer of record (Forms I-9, E-Verify, background checks, payroll, benefits, client invoicing). It has a general confidentiality clause and no security requirements or breach notice clause (EV-013). About 24 contractors were paid through the partner in 2025, and 11 were on assignment at 6 clients in June 2026; the margin share is about $9,750 a month (EV-014).

**Money and business volume.** The accounting SaaS has named accounts for the owner and the bookkeeper, with MFA enforced by the vendor. 2025 income was about $117,000 in margin share and about $63,000 from 5 direct-hire fees, paid by ACH or check (EV-015). The 2025 Schedule C shows gross receipts of about $180,000 and no wages (EV-016). The statements show recurring payments to the SaaS vendors, the networking site and two job boards, the phone carrier, the internet provider and the professional liability insurer, plus the bookkeeper and the IT shop. No payment appears for a cyber insurance policy, a password manager, a security course, a background screening company or an AI chatbot (EV-017).

**Contracts.** Fee agreements with about 15 clients include a confidentiality clause and require notice of a security incident affecting client information "promptly", with no fixed number of hours (EV-018). The bookkeeper's engagement letter has a confidentiality clause, and the IT support technician signed a confidentiality agreement on 2026-07-17. IT shop invoices show remote sessions started by the owner with one-time session codes (EV-019).

**Devices, network and office.** The laptop has full-disk encryption, automatic updates, built-in antivirus, a host firewall and a 5-minute lock. Its downloads folder holds start forms and resumes. Business passwords are saved in the browser, no password manager is installed, and the browser flags the ATS password as also used on an old job board account (EV-020). The phone has a passcode, biometric unlock, encryption and a 30-second lock. The device locator is on, and no remote erase has been run. Email and ATS apps stay signed in, and the camera roll holds photos of candidate ID documents (EV-021). The previous phone was traded in through the carrier in 2024 with no wipe confirmation kept (EV-022). The router comes from the internet provider and runs one network shared with family phones, a game console and smart-home devices. It shows a firmware version and no update history (EV-023). In the home office, current fee agreements are in a locked drawer, paper goes through a cross-cut shredder, and a surge protector is in use (EV-024).

**Documents, insurance and AI tools.** No written security policy, risk assessment, account or device list, vendor list, incident plan, contact list, continuity plan, retention schedule, disposal procedure or AI add-on review was found, and no security training certificate. The 2019 business plan has one page on computer use (EV-025). The professional liability declarations list no cyber endorsement (EV-026). The free consumer AI chatbot account has been used since 2025-11; its history lists about 30 conversations with pasted resumes, and chat history and model training are on (EV-027).
