## 1. Purpose
Make sure the firm spots, contains, reports, and recovers from security incidents quickly and lawfully, stops any diversion of pay, and meets every notice deadline.

## 2. Scope
All staff of Cris Santos Company and every system and copy of firm information, including systems the MSP and SaaS vendors run for the firm. A "security incident" includes any attempted or successful unauthorized access to, use, disclosure, change, or destruction of firm information, or interference with a system. It also includes lost or stolen phones and laptops, misdirected emails or scans with personal information, and any bank-account change that the associate or staff member did not ask for.

## 3. Roles and responsibilities
| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Operations Manager | Incident lead; keeps the incident log; with breach counsel, decides whether a breach has occurred and when |
| Owner | Backup incident lead; calls the cyber insurer; approves outside communications and spending; decides on any extortion demand |
| Onboarding and Payroll Coordinator | Payroll containment with the payroll vendor (freeze bank changes, recall deposits); associate call-backs |
| MSP | Technical response: isolate, investigate, reset, rebuild, restore; preserves logs |
| Cyber insurer and its panel vendors | Breach counsel and forensic firm, engaged through the insurer's hotline |
| All staff | Report suspected incidents at once |

## 4. Policy statements
4.1 The firm must keep an incident response runbook for its most likely serious incident, a payroll and HR system breach exposing worker personal information (P08), with a printed copy and contact card in the locked records room and at the Operations Manager's and Owner's homes. (IR-8; RS.MA-01)

4.2 Staff must report any suspected incident to the Operations Manager **at once, and within 1 hour at most**, by phone or in person. If the Operations Manager cannot be reached, report to the Owner. Examples: typing a password into a page reached from an email or text; an MFA prompt or code you did not ask for; an associate saying their pay went somewhere else; a lost phone or laptop; an email or scan sent to the wrong person; a caller asking for SSNs or bank details. (IR-6; RS.MA-02)

4.3 The Operations Manager must log every incident, including those that turn out to be harmless, with the date found, what happened, what was done, and the outcome. (IR-5)

4.4 An incident must be declared when any of the following is confirmed: an unauthorized bank-account change; a sign-in to a firm account by someone other than its owner; an export or download of personal information by someone not authorized; a lost device holding personal information that cannot be shown to be locked and encrypted; or a vendor's notice of a breach involving firm data. (IR-4; DE.AE-08)

4.5 For any suspected payroll diversion, the Coordinator must ask the payroll vendor at once to freeze bank-account changes and hold or recall pending deposits to changed accounts. Pay to affected associates must be made whole on the normal payday by an off-cycle payment approved by the Owner. (IR-4; RS.MI-01)

4.6 For any suspected account takeover, data theft, or ransomware, the Owner must call the cyber insurer's breach hotline before hiring any outside firm, and the MSP must be engaged under its contract. (IR-4; RS.MA-01)

4.7 **Breach decision and clocks.** The Operations Manager, with breach counsel, must record the date and time the firm determined that a breach occurred or had reason to believe one occurred. Florida's 30-day clocks for notice to individuals and, for 500 or more Floridians, to the Department of Legal Affairs run from that point (Fla. Stat. 501.171(3)-(4)). A no-harm determination may be made only after investigation and consultation with law enforcement, must be in writing, kept at least 5 years, and sent to the department within 30 days (501.171(4)(c)). (IR-6; RS.AN-08)

4.8 **E-Verify.** If E-Verify personal data or E-Verify credentials may have been exposed, the Operations Manager must notify DHS immediately as the MOU requires (Art. II.A.16). (IR-6; RS.CO-02)

4.9 All outside notices (individuals, regulators, DHS, consumer reporting agencies, clients, and other states) must follow the P08 notification matrix. Breach counsel must review each notice before it is sent. (IR-6; RS.CO-02; RS.CO-03)

4.10 Vendors must report incidents affecting firm data to the Operations Manager. Florida law requires a third-party agent to notify the firm no later than 10 days after it determines a breach (501.171(6)(a)); the firm still sends the notices. The Operations Manager must log and handle each vendor report under this policy. (IR-6; SA-9; GV.SC-08)

4.11 No extortion payment may be made without the Owner's approval, advice from breach counsel and the insurer, and an OFAC sanctions check. (IR-4)

4.12 The runbook must be tested every year with a tabletop exercise that includes the MSP, and after any real incident that used it. (IR-3; ID.IM-02)

4.13 Lessons learned must be written up within 30 days of closing any incident that required outside notice or outside help, and fed into the risk register and training. (IR-4; ID.IM-02)

## 5. Compliance and enforcement
Breaking this policy leads to sanctions under POL-02 A.4. Failing to report is a violation; reporting a mistake in good faith is never sanctioned. Compliance is checked in the annual assessment (P07) and the annual tabletop.

## 6. Exceptions
Exceptions follow POL-02 A.9. No exception may delay a legally required notice.
