## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the group's own records before any assessment work began on 2026-05-01. It covers the legal and employing entities, the systems that hold worker, candidate, client, and patient data in each division and in corporate shared services, the suppliers that touch them, and the rules that may bind each entity. Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Items are grouped by division: the title of each row starts with Group, Group Workforce Platform, Staffing, Consulting or Home Health. Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (interviews, document reviews, samples, tests) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the gap analyses (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**Entities and scale.** The holding company is an SEC registrant with three separate subsidiaries; each subsidiary and corporate is an employing entity, and Home Health was acquired in 2023 (EV-001). The core workforce is 45,000: Staffing about 14,000, Consulting about 9,000, Home Health about 18,000, corporate about 4,000 (EV-002). FY2025 revenue was about $18.0 billion: Staffing $13.5 billion, Consulting $2.7 billion, Home Health $1.8 billion (EV-003). Staffing has about 230,000 temporary associates on assignment in an average week and paid about 860,000 different associates in 2025; associates are paid every Friday, about $190 million a week, through two ACH banks and a paycard program manager (EV-004). The intercompany BAA between corporate and Home Health (2023-10) lists IT hosting, identity, SOC and EHR integration, not payroll; about 1,100 Healthcare Staffing per diem clinicians a week work under Home Health's direct control (EV-005). The FY2025 Form 10-K includes Item 1C; no cross-division exercise of the disclosure playbook was provided (EV-006).

**Governance, policies and common controls.** The risk management strategy, risk standard, registers, minutes, metrics and the legal and regulatory register are on file; the legal register has no entries for the Colorado SB26-189 or CPPA ADMT duties that start 2027-01-01 (EV-007). The 2025 group policies are aligned to CSF 2.0; the Home Health supplement is v2023, written at acquisition, with no later alignment record (EV-008). Inheritance matrices exist for Staffing (2025) and Consulting (2026); none was provided for Home Health (EV-009).

**Group identity and access.** All workforce applications use SSO with number-matching MFA; administrators use hardware keys; GWP applications accept managed devices only (EV-010). Identity governance runs joiner-mover-leaver workflows from HR events; quarterly certification covers SOX and tier-1 applications but not service accounts or E-Verify accounts; 41 GWP integration service accounts sit in a separate register outside identity governance (EV-011, EV-013). Privileged access is just in time through PAM with session recording (EV-012). Laptops lock after 10 minutes idle (EV-014).

**Group security operations.** The SOC runs 24x7. Home Health EHR access logs, field tablet telemetry and the VMS tenant audit logs are not in the SIEM data source list, and no rule covers bank-change velocity, repeat destination accounts or SaaS bulk exports (EV-015). Home Health cases use Home Health's own 3-level severity scale (EV-016). Group EDR is on managed laptops, servers, cloud hosts and kiosks, and on 72% of about 11,500 Home Health tablets (EV-017). Scans run monthly and patch service levels are met for 96% of systems (EV-018); the 2026-03 penetration test covered the career sites, the self-service app and the payroll engine (EV-019). The 2025 incident response plan has no GWP notification matrix, E-Verify notice or Home Health path for visit-pay data; the draft notification matrix has not been exercised, and exercises to date are technical (EV-020).

**Group cloud platform.** Provider A hosts the hub and the GWP's customer-managed parts, including the visit-pay interface; provider B hosts the DR replica, the backup vault and the Federal Solutions enclave (EV-021). Guardrails, a deny-by-default hub firewall and WAF rules on the self-service APIs are configured (EV-022). Keys are customer-managed, and SSNs and bank account numbers are tokenized in the payroll engine (EV-023). Backups are immutable in a separate provider B account (EV-024); logs are write-once (EV-025); endpoints accept TLS 1.2 or higher only (EV-026).

**Group Workforce Platform.** The GWP has about 9,600 staff users and about 1.1 million self-service accounts; the 4 payroll roles (about 640 users) include the screen that shows visit-pay records (EV-027, EV-028). It holds about 3.4 million worker records with SSNs and bank accounts since 2014, about 7.2 million candidate profiles, about 1.9 million electronic and 0.9 million scanned Forms I-9 and credential files for about 96,000 clinicians; the data map does not list the visit-pay records (EV-029). Since 2024-04 each Home Health visit record sent for per-visit pay carries the patient's name, address, visit date and visit type, for about 214,000 patients so far, and copies load to the payroll engine and the data hub (EV-030). The processing register has no entry for that patient data, and the PIA register has no assessment of the visit-pay interface or the AI ranking add-on (EV-031). Associates sign in to self-service with an SMS one-time code, and the same code approves a bank change (EV-032); 690 fraudulent bank changes diverted about $1.9 million in 2025, which the group repaid (EV-033). Object-level access logging is off for the scanned I-9 archive (EV-034). The 2026-02 DR failover met the 12-hour RTO with the identity platform assumed available; no exercise of the repeat-payroll procedure was provided (EV-035). 9 of the 41 integration service account secrets were created more than 1 year before the export (EV-036). The AI ranking add-on was enabled through the ATS marketplace in 2025-05 for all divisions; its terms allow training on candidate data, its vendor file has no SOC 2 report or bias results, and auto-advance has been on for Commercial requisitions since 2025-09 (EV-037). The 2026-04 bias audit covers NYC requisitions only (EV-038).

**Vendors, workforce, records and AI.** Vendors are tiered and contract templates carry security and breach notice terms (EV-039); SOC 2 reports are on file for the main vendors, none for the AI ranking vendor (EV-040). Training, phishing and acknowledgment records cover all staff (EV-041); screening, confidentiality terms and the sanctions log are on file (EV-042), as are facilities and disposal records (EV-043). The retention schedule has no automated deletion for candidate profiles, consumer reports or visit-pay records (EV-044). The group hired about 640,000 people in 2025, with requisitions in New York City, Illinois, Colorado and California (EV-045). AI discovery found 9 use cases in operation or pilot (EV-046).

**Staffing.** Each employing entity has its own E-Verify MOU; about 1,400 named users at 9 onboarding centers sign in on the DHS website, outside SSO; termination checklists at 5 of the 9 centers include E-Verify account removal (EV-047). Form I-9 records, the quarterly self-audit and the outage procedure are on file; no description of the scanned archive's indexing and retrieval was provided (EV-048). FCRA certifications and a standalone disclosure are in use, and consumer report PDFs have no deletion setting (EV-049). Staffing runs about 520 sites and serves about 14,000 clients (EV-050); kiosks share the staff network at 61 legacy branches (EV-051). 214 hospital agreements contain BAA or confidentiality terms, with a shortest notice term of 48 hours (EV-052). The Managed Workforce Solutions program serves 48 clients on one VMS tenant; 31 contracts require a SOC 2 Type 2 report by 2027-12-31; no review record of the exported tenant logs was provided (EV-053). About 400 client VMS integrations use shared service accounts and long-lived keys (EV-054). Contact center agents change bank accounts after knowledge-based checks (EV-055).

**Consulting.** Health IT Advisory serves about 160 hospital and health-system clients; Federal Solutions works on 11 prime contracts and 4 subcontracts, none with DoD; about 240 independent subcontractors work for the division (EV-056). All hospital clients have signed BAAs, 23 with 72-hour breach notice; the subcontract template has no BAA terms (EV-057). There is no central register of client-issued accounts (about 6,300 by managers' estimate), and the roll-off log shows some client notices after the roll-off date (EV-058). The repository has no classification labels and allows sharing links (EV-059). The Federal Solutions enclave is a separate account with its own firewall, MFA and EDR (EV-060). The business associate program has an annual risk analysis and self-assessment (EV-061).

**Home Health.** 64 Medicare-certified locations in 5 states serve an average daily census of about 30,000 (EV-062). The v2023 supplement ends EHR access the next business day after termination, keeps logs 90 days and has its own severity scale (EV-063). The 2025 risk analysis does not mention the visit-pay records, and the evaluation plan does not list inherited controls (EV-064). About 11,500 tablets sit in the legacy device tool, and its detections go to a Home Health mailbox (EV-065). The EHR is vendor-hosted under a BAA; Staffing assignment end dates are not fed to it, and some offices share OASIS submission accounts (EV-066). The emergency plan was updated in 2025; priority lists are printed weekly; no prolonged cyber outage is in its hazard assessment (EV-067). There are minimum-necessary protocols for referrals and payer disclosures, none for the visit-pay records (EV-068). The hospitalization risk model's inputs include age and disability-related items, with no review on file; the ambient documentation pilot uses a verbal consent reminder (EV-069).
