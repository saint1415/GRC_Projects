## 1. Purpose
Set up the bank's written information security program, as the Interagency Guidelines Establishing Information Security Standards require (12 CFR Part 30, Appendix B, section II.A; N52-R02), assign who is accountable, and give every other security policy its authority. The program protects the security and confidentiality of customer information, protects against anticipated threats to it, and protects against unauthorized access to or use of it that could result in substantial harm or inconvenience to any customer (section II.B). It also covers the bank's own information and the funds that move through its payment systems.

## 2. Scope
All Cris Santos Bank workforce members (directors, officers, employees, contractors, and temporary staff) at the six branches and the operations center. Covers all systems and data, including systems that service providers operate for the bank (the core processor, the digital banking provider, the payments service provider, the card processor, and SaaS and cloud providers). It applies to customer information and all other bank information.

## 4. Policy statements
4.1 The bank must maintain a comprehensive written information security program, appropriate to its size and complexity and the nature and scope of its activities, documented in this policy set, the System Security Plan, and the risk register. The board, or the Audit and Risk Committee on its behalf, must approve the program. (PM-1; GV.PO-01; II.A; III.A.1)
4.2 The board designates the Information Security Officer in writing. The ISO has a direct reporting line to the Audit and Risk Committee for security matters, in addition to the operational line to the COO. (PM-2; GV.RR-02; III.A.2)
4.3 A risk assessment must be performed at least annually and after major changes, using NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1. It must identify foreseeable internal and external threats, including payment fraud through customer channels and service provider failures, and assess the sufficiency of controls. Risks must be tracked in the risk register with an owner and treatment. (RA-3; ID.RA-01; III.B)
4.4 **Risk acceptance authority:** the ISO may accept Low and Very Low risks; the COO, Moderate; the President and CEO, High, temporarily and with a dated treatment plan, reporting each acceptance to the Audit and Risk Committee; the board, Very High. Residual risk must be compared with the tolerances in the board's risk appetite statement. (PM-9; GV.RM-01; III.A.2)
4.5 Security policies must be reviewed at least annually and updated after major changes or incidents. (PL-1; GV.PO-02)
4.6 Exceptions to any security policy must be requested in writing, risk-rated, approved per 4.4, recorded in the risk register, and time-limited to 12 months or less.
4.7 **Sanctions.** Workforce members who fail to comply with security policies must be sanctioned in proportion to intent and harm: retraining, written warning, suspension, or termination. HR must document each sanction. (PS-8; GV.RR-04)
4.8 **Service providers.** Before a service provider receives customer information or performs a critical service, the COO must complete due diligence. Contracts must require appropriate safeguards (III.D.2), incident notice to the bank within a stated time frame, and the right to receive SOC reports and bridge letters. For critical providers, the ISO must review SOC reports every year, map complementary user entity controls (CUECs) to bank controls, and follow up on exceptions (III.D.3). The ISO must give each bank service provider a designated point of contact for incident notices under 12 CFR 53.4. (SA-9; SR-6; GV.SC-05; GV.SC-07)
4.9 Key controls must be tested regularly, at a frequency set by the risk assessment, by people independent of those who operate or design them: at least an annual independent IT audit, an annual external penetration test, and an annual control assessment (P07). (CA-2; ID.IM-02; III.C.3)
4.10 The ISO must report to the board or the Audit and Risk Committee at least annually on the overall status of the program and compliance with the Guidelines, covering risk assessment, risk management and control decisions, service provider arrangements, test results, security incidents and management's responses, and recommended changes. (PM-9; GV.OV-01; III.F)
4.11 The program must be adjusted when there are relevant changes in technology, threats, business arrangements, outsourcing, or law. Triggers include a new customer-facing channel, a new AI model used in decisions about customers, a new or renewed critical service provider, a new regulation, and any notification incident. (GV.OV-02; III.E)
4.12 **Models and AI.** Any model or AI system that makes or supports credit decisions or other decisions about customers must be in the AI and model inventory, independently validated, and fair lending tested before production use, and monitored while in use (P10). (SA-9; GV.RM-01)
4.13 Security policies, risk assessments, test results, and board reports must be retained for at least five years (bank policy choice). (SI-12)

## 5. Compliance and enforcement
Violations are handled under the sanctions procedure (POL-01 section 4.7). Sanctions range from retraining to termination, depending on intent and harm. Compliance is checked through the annual control assessment (P07), the annual independent IT audit, and the access reviews in this policy set.

## 6. Exceptions
Exceptions follow POL-01 section 4.6. They must be written, risk-rated, approved at the level in section 4.4, and expire within 12 months.
