## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the group's own records before the BIA, the enterprise cyber risk assessment and the regulatory gap analysis began on 2026-05-04, and before Internal Audit's control assessment fieldwork began on 2026-06-15. It covers the organization (the parent, the bank, the broker-dealer and adviser, and the acquired bank's remaining platform), the systems that hold customer information or support tier-1 processes, the third parties that touch them, and the rules that may bind each entity and the two service lines offered to institutional clients (SL-1 and SL-2). Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (BIA interviews, risk workshops, gap analysis samples, Internal Audit tests, the AI portfolio review) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

At this size the sources are enterprise systems of record across the bank and the broker-dealer: the identity platform, the mainframe security product and the HR system, the CMDB and endpoint consoles, the cloud consoles and the SIEM, the third-party risk register and the accounts payable vendor master, the contract repository, the payments, treasury and fraud platforms, board and committee records, regulatory reports, prior Internal Audit and SOX workpapers, and regulator correspondence.

Each register row names the owning business unit's system of record and owner. The [asset inventory](asset-inventory.csv) reconciles the CMDB with the cloud account inventory, the endpoint consoles and the AI governance committee register; the [vendor register](vendor-register.csv) reconciles the third-party risk register with the accounts payable vendor master and the contract repository.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the gap analysis (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**Identity and access.** About 12,000 workforce accounts are federated through SSO, and MFA is required for all workforce access (EV-001). About 310 privileged administrators on distributed and cloud components use just-in-time elevation through PAM with session recording; the mainframe privileged IDs are not in the vault (EV-002). Joiner, mover and leaver events flow from the HR system, 4 quarterly certification campaigns closed in the last 12 months, and mainframe entitlements are not connected to identity governance (EV-003). The mainframe security product holds 41 IDs with standing system-level privileges; mainframe entitlements are certified on spreadsheets and leaver removals are entered by hand (EV-004). HR lists 12,000 employees, including about 640 commercial client service staff (EV-005). About 2.4 million consumer and 180,000 business digital users sign in through CIAM; 23% of consumer users receive one-time passcodes by SMS, and payee adds on devices marked trusted are configured without step-up (EV-006). The service identity register lists 214 service accounts on CBDC components (EV-007).

**Endpoints, servers and network.** The EDR agent reports on all of about 22,000 endpoints and 6,800 servers (EV-008). Of the 48 core middleware servers, 4 run an operating system in vendor extended support until 2027-03-31 (EV-009). Baselines are held as code and as hardened images, and posture tools check them daily (EV-010). The network serves 610 branches over SD-WAN, about 1,480 ATMs and a dedicated payments segment, with dual carriers at each data center (EV-011). Scans run weekly on distributed and cloud components and monthly on the mainframe, against a 30-day remediation target (EV-012).

**Cloud, logging, backup and recovery.** Both clouds use account vending, guardrails as code and daily posture scans (EV-013), with customer-managed keys (EV-014). The SIEM has 214 tier-1 log sources; core maintenance transactions (contact data, beneficiary and limit changes made by staff) are logged in the core and are not listed as SIEM sources, and the legacy commercial platform sends login events only (EV-015). The Cyber Defense Center runs 24x7, and its cases include business email compromise and account takeover (EV-016). The core failover from DC-1 to DC-2 on 2026-04-25 took 3 hours 40 minutes against a 4-hour RTO (EV-017). Cloud backups are immutable in separate accounts; core data is replicated to DC-2 and backed up to virtual tape, and no logically isolated immutable copy of core data is listed (EV-018). The CBDC contingency plan v7 is on file with coordination records (EV-019).

**Governance, prior assurance and disclosure.** The board approved the information security program and the risk appetite statement on 2026-01-27 (EV-020). The board risk committee is chaired by an independent director and receives the Chief Risk Officer's reports at least quarterly (EV-021). The risk governance framework, its limits and the 2026-01-19 Appendix D assessment memo are on file; third-party concentration is reported without a limit (EV-022). The CISO reports to the CIO, and the cyber risk oversight team that the Chief Risk Officer relies on for second-line challenge of cybersecurity sits in the CISO's organization (EV-023). The 2025 independent assessment of the risk governance framework sampled credit and liquidity risk (EV-024). The 2025 cyber risk assessment rolled into 8 enterprise risks (EV-025). The 2025 policy set, standards, procedures and exception register are in the policy portal (EV-026). Cyber key risk indicators are compiled by hand from 11 source extracts (EV-027). The incident response plan 2026.1 includes the 12 CFR 53.3 and 225.302 determination criteria (EV-028). The disclosure committee exercised the materiality playbook on 2026-03-26; its worksheet assesses each incident on its own (EV-029). The FY2025 Form 10-K includes Item 1C, and 11 of 13 directors are independent (EV-030).

**Workforce and privacy records.** Annual training completion is 98.7%, with monthly phishing simulations; no role-based business email compromise module is assigned to the commercial client service staff (EV-031). Background checks and fingerprinting are completed before hire (EV-032). The Privacy Office documents customer notice decisions, notification events and counsel-reviewed templates, and outside counsel refreshed the state matrix in 2026-04 (EV-033).

**Third parties, contracts and service lines.** The third-party risk register lists about 2,400 third parties, 460 with customer information and 41 critical; SOC report reviews for 7 critical third parties, including the card processor, are past their annual due date, and complementary user entity controls are mapped for 29 of 41 (EV-034, EV-035). The contract repository indexes the 41 critical contracts and the broker-dealer service provider contracts; 5 critical contracts were inherited from the acquired bank (EV-036). SOC reports are on file for the clouds, the treasury and loan origination vendors, the trust platform vendor, the core vendor, the card processor and the clearing firm (EV-037). The treasury platform contract states a 4-hour RTO, and its fallback was tested at 200 instructions in 2026-02 (EV-038). The legacy commercial platform serves about 2,900 business clients with SMS one-time passcodes only and no out-of-band confirmation of new beneficiaries; its contract states a 24-hour RTO and there is no record of a restore test by the bank (EV-039). One card processor handles all authorizations, with stand-in for core outages (EV-040). The broker-dealer has 31 service providers with customer information, including the clearing firm (EV-041). SL-1 issued a 2025 SOC 2 Type 2 report with no exceptions, and SL-2 issues a SOC 1 Type 2 report each year (EV-042).

**Payments and fraud.** The treasury platform requires out-of-band confirmation of a new beneficiary only for wires of $100,000 or more (EV-043). A callback standard covers wire requests received by email or phone; the central callback team handles treasury requests, and branches perform their own callbacks for branch-originated requests (EV-044). Fraud records show business email compromise and account takeover losses in each month of the period, and the SAR log links security and fraud cases (EV-045).

**Business volume and structure.** The bank handles about 11,000 outgoing wires and about 3.2 million originated ACH entries per business day (EV-046). FY2025 revenue was about $4.8 billion, about $13.2 million per calendar day (EV-047). The regulatory reports for the four quarters to 2025-12-31 show the parent's assets between $50 billion and $100 billion and the bank's at more than 95% of the parent's (EV-048). The legal entity register lists the three entities, no New York license or office and no insurance license (EV-049). The acquired bank's core conversion is scheduled for 2026-05-16, and its legacy commercial platform runs until migration, due 2027-02-26 (EV-050).

**AI.** The AI governance committee register lists 16 use cases, with review completed for 11 and scheduled for AI-013; AI-009, AI-012, AI-014 and AI-015 are not yet reviewed, and the generative AI provisions of STD-01.8 are in draft (EV-053). AI-001 was validated in 2026-02 and fair lending tested for personal loans in 2026-04; the credit card segment has no fair lending test on file, and 3 of its 40 reason codes are worded generally (EV-054). Public generative AI tools are blocked at the web proxy, and AI features are listed for several SaaS tools (EV-055).

**Obligations.** Counsel's view on each candidate rule is in EV-051, and the regulator contacts for incident notices are in EV-052. The results are in [`obligations-register.csv`](obligations-register.csv).
