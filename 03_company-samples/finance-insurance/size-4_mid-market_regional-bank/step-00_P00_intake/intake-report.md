## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the bank's own records before any assessment work began on 2026-06-29. It covers the organization, the systems that hold or move customer information and funds, the third parties that touch them, and the rules that may bind the bank and its holding company. At this size the sources are systems of record: the identity provider, the HR system, the core security module, the payments hub, the digital banking admin console, the endpoint and EDR consoles, the cloud organization console, the SIEM, the third-party inventory and contract register, board and committee records, prior risk assessments and audit reports, scan results, the incident register and the BSA case system. Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (interviews, samples, tests) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the gap analysis (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**Users and access.** The identity provider holds active named accounts for all 600 employees plus contractor, vendor and service accounts, and every active workforce account has MFA registered (EV-001). Conditional access requires app-based MFA with number matching and a managed device; administrators use hardware keys; accounts inactive for 60 days are disabled; respondent users sign in to the correspondent portal through the customer identity service with app-based MFA (EV-002). 41 privileged accounts for the directory, identity provider and cloud are in PAM. The 5 core security administrators and the 6 payments hub administrators use their everyday accounts, and 2 of the core security administrators also process account maintenance (EV-003). The core has 212 roles assigned user by user with no job-role template, allows 8-character passwords, and has no automatic inactivity disable (EV-004). The payments hub has 64 users, 6 of them administrators; maker-checker and the callback field are enforced, and application logs are kept 90 days (EV-005). The Q1 2026 core entitlement review shows manager approvals with no role template to compare against (EV-006). The HR feed disables federated access at termination; the core and the portal vendor accounts are outside it, termination tickets reach the core security administrators by email with no time frame, and transfers do not notify them (EV-008). HR recorded 142 terminations and 88 transfers in the 12 months to 2026-06-30 (EV-009).

**Devices, network and vulnerabilities.** The endpoint console lists 640 workstations and laptops, including 170 teller and 4 payments workstations. EDR reports on all managed endpoints and servers, and laptops are encrypted (EV-010). Of about 60 servers at headquarters, 14 check imaging and item processing servers run an operating system past vendor support, on their own segment (EV-011). The 28 branches and 2 operations sites run on SD-WAN with private circuits to the core processor; the payments workstation segment reaches only the Federal Reserve connection and the payments hub; the payments hub vendor connects through an always-on VPN (EV-012). Monthly authenticated scans recorded 212 Critical findings in the first half of 2026, and critical patches went in after the 30-day target on 18% of on-premises servers (EV-013). Annual external and internal penetration tests ran in 2026-03 (EV-014).

**Cloud, backups and recovery.** The landing zone has 6 accounts under organization guardrails, daily posture checks, customer-managed keys and a write-once log archive kept 3 years (EV-015). Daily backups go to a separate backup account in a second region with 35-day write-once retention and separate credentials; file-level restores are tested monthly, and no full restore of the payments database or the item processing servers is recorded (EV-016). The 2025-05 payments hub failover took 9 hours against a 4-hour target, with manual DNS, Federal Reserve connection and reconciliation steps, and no corrective actions were opened (EV-017). The 2025 continuity plan covers the branches, the contact center and the wire rooms with the Georgia alternate site; correspondent services and the item processing servers have no recovery objectives in it, there are no failover roles or wire reconciliation runbook, and alternate site exercises cover customer wires only (EV-018). The core processor's SOC reports state RTO 4 hours and RPO 15 minutes, and the bank took part in its 2025 disaster recovery test (EV-019).

**Monitoring and change.** The SIEM receives identity provider, EDR, firewall, remote access and cloud audit logs. Payments hub, correspondent portal and core security reports are not connected, and no use cases cover beneficiary, template, limit or contact-information changes. The MSSP contract requires a call to the ISO within 30 minutes of a high-severity alert (EV-020, EV-021). About 400 infrastructure and application changes went through the change advisory board. Payments hub limit, approval-rule and callback-setting changes appear only in the application log, with one administrator per change (EV-022).

**Programs, documents and records.** The board approved the written information security program in October 2025; the ISO is designated by board resolution; the Board Risk Committee receives an annual report each December and qualitative quarterly cyber reports since 2026 Q1 (EV-023). The 2025 risk appetite statement is qualitative, with no measures (EV-024). The annual risk assessment was completed in December 2025, and the 2025 co-sourced IT audit report records CUEC testing for the core processor only (EV-025). Policies POL-01 to POL-05 were adopted in 2024; no configuration, logging, cryptography, third-party tiering or AI use standard was located (EV-026). The 2024 incident response plan includes the 36-hour OCC notice and OCC contacts. It has no notification incident criteria, no Federal Reserve step for the holding company, no notice to respondents, and a Florida-only notification matrix; the 2025 tabletop involved IT only (EV-027). The incident register holds 31 incidents in 12 months and the 2025 core processor outage (6 hours, hardware); no notification incident determinations are recorded (EV-028). The BSA case system shows 212 SARs in 12 months and 2026 fraud cases in which the customer's phone number was changed before the wire; fraud cases are not linked to the incident register (EV-029). Annual training is 98% complete and the June 2026 phishing click rate was 5.9%; wire room and treasury operations staff receive payment-fraud training, and branch and contact center staff who process contact changes have no role-based fraud training assigned (EV-030). The Red Flags program dates from 2022 (EV-031).

**Customers and payments.** Contact-information changes are accepted at branches, by email and by phone after knowledge-based questions, with no out-of-band verification step and no customer alert; about 1,100 business contact changes were made in Q1 2026 (EV-032). The payments hub sent about 380 outgoing wires a business day (about $165 million), about 120 of them for respondents, and received about 1,900 non-face-to-face wire requests in May 2026 (EV-033). About 68,000 consumer and 9,200 business users are enrolled in online banking; 12% of business users are on SMS codes, consumers get an SMS code only on a new device, and no out-of-band confirmation is set for new beneficiaries (EV-034).

**Third parties and contracts.** The third-party inventory lists about 260 third parties: 34 Tier 1, 71 Tier 2, and 7 bank service providers. SOC reports are collected for the 34 critical vendors, CUEC mapping exists for the core processor only, and designated 53.4 contacts were sent to 3 of the 7 bank service providers (EV-035). Nine of the 34 critical contracts state no incident notice time frame; the core processor contract states no recovery time or notice time frame; the 2019 respondent agreement template promises "commercially reasonable" availability with no recovery, notice or security commitments (EV-036). The cyber policy has a $15 million limit and a $500,000 retention, with notice through the carrier hotline first (EV-037). Several respondents and their examiners asked in 2026 for an independent report on the correspondent service, and no respondent has given a designated incident contact (EV-038).

**Business volume and charter.** Net revenue was about $105 million in FY2025, about $420,000 per business day over about 250 business days, with fee income of about $150,000 a day split by line (EV-039). The bank has $2.5 billion in total assets, is an OCC-supervised national bank, and is owned by a Federal Reserve-supervised bank holding company with no Exchange Act registration (EV-040). There are about 96,000 consumer and 11,500 business deposit customers, a $1.9 billion loan portfolio, about 420 small business originations a year, and 58 ATMs and interactive teller machines (EV-041).

**AI tools, facilities and other.** Five AI tools are in use (EV-042): the small business credit model (AI-001) and AML alert scoring (AI-002) are in the model inventory; the generative AI assistant pilot (AI-004) and the chatbot (AI-005) were found in the identity provider app list and accounts payable, with no model risk or compliance review record; fraud scoring (AI-003) is embedded in the digital banking service. AI-001 has a 2025 conceptual review and the vendor's fair lending test on national data, and no outcomes validation on bank data is recorded. Public generative AI sites are reachable from bank devices. Walk-throughs of the headquarters campus, the Georgia regional office and 4 branches (2026-06-16 to 2026-06-18) found badge readers and cameras on the wire rooms, the operations center and the server room, which share the headquarters campus (EV-043, EV-044). Media is destroyed by a certified vendor with serial-number certificates (EV-045). Outbound data loss prevention is in monitor mode, and its reports show loan documents with NPI sent as email attachments (EV-046).

**Reconciliation across sources.**
- **Workforce.** The 600 employees on the HR roster each have an identity provider account (EV-001, EV-007).
- **Endpoints.** The endpoint console count (640, including 170 teller and 4 payments workstations) includes the operations center and wire room endpoints (about 110) and the branch endpoints used for payments and maintenance (about 280) that the SSP places inside its boundary (EV-010).
- **Payments.** The 380 outgoing wires a day split into about 260 for bank customers and about 120 for respondents, the figures the BIA uses for BP-01 and BP-02 (EV-033).
- **Revenue.** The fee lines in EV-039 add up to the $150,000 a day of fee income used in the BIA.
- **Third parties.** About 260 third parties, 34 Tier 1, 71 Tier 2 and 7 bank service providers (EV-035). The named vendors and a summary row in [`vendor-register.csv`](vendor-register.csv) add up to the 34 Tier 1 vendors.
- **AI tools.** Two models in the model inventory, two tools in the app list and accounts payable, and one embedded provider service give the 5 tools in [`asset-inventory.csv`](asset-inventory.csv). Use of public generative AI sites was not measured (EV-042).
