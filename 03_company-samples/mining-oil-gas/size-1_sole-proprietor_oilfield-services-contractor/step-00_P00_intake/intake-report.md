## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the business's own records before the self-assessment began on 2026-07-20. A one-person contractor has no HR system, asset database or internal audit, so the systems of record are the SaaS admin consoles, the accounting service's reports, bank and card statements, the email inbox, the signed agreements folder, the laptop and phone settings screens, the internet provider's app, and the insurance agent's portal. Intake covers the organization, the systems that hold customer information or connect to customer equipment, the providers that touch them, and the rules and contracts that may bind the business. Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (self-reviews, walkthroughs, tests) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the gap analysis (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**SaaS accounts and settings.** The productivity suite is a business plan with one account, the owner's, which is also the administrator. MFA is on with text-message codes to the owner's phone, the phone is the only second factor, and no backup codes have been generated. Email is the recovery address for the other service accounts (EV-001). File storage holds gauge sheets, run tickets, a synced program folder with program copies for Customers A and B, customer network notes, and scanned W-9 forms for the 2 helpers. Version history is kept 30 days, no other backup or retention setting is configured, and the program copies carry no record of which version was loaded where (EV-002). The accounting service has one administrator account with password sign-in, and MFA is available and off. It holds the helpers' Social Security numbers for 1099 reporting (EV-003).

**Business volume.** 2025 receipts were about $180,000: contract pumping about $115,000 and automation service about $65,000. Customer A is about 45% of receipts. About $15,000 is invoiced a month on 30-day terms, paid by ACH. June 2026 invoices bill 34 wells (Customer A 18, B 6, C 6, D 4), and automation service is billed to Customers A and B (EV-004). The 2025 Schedule C shows no wages and contract labor paid to 2 day helpers (EV-005).

**Devices, media and home network.** The laptop has one local account, an administrator, used for email, web browsing and control work. It carries the configuration software, the Customer A VPN client, the Customer B remote-desktop client, an attended remote-support app, and the sync client. Full-disk encryption, automatic updates, the built-in antivirus and the host firewall are on, the screen locks after 5 minutes, no backup is configured other than file sync, and a spreadsheet of customer site passwords is stored on the laptop (EV-006). The phone uses a passcode and fingerprint, is encrypted, has no remote locate or wipe set up, and holds email, the MFA codes, the hotspot, the Customer A alarm viewer, gauge photos and a free AI chatbot app (EV-007). There are 3 unencrypted USB drives; one holds personal files and customer programs together (EV-008). The home router came from the internet provider, and the laptop, phone and family devices share one Wi-Fi network (EV-009).

**Customer contracts and access.** Customer A's 2025 MSA has an 8-item security schedule, including 24-hour incident notice, email approval and a record of every logic or setpoint change, written consent before sharing data with software services, and cyber coverage of at least $1 million while the owner holds remote access (EV-010). The MSAs with Customers B, C and D have confidentiality clauses only (EV-011). Customer A issued a named VPN account and MFA token after confirming the owner's identity (EV-012). Customer B's operations manager emailed a shared remote-desktop login in 2024; Customer B staff use the same login, the tool is left on for unattended access, and sign-in is by password only. No terms are attached, and the inbox holds no later message about it (EV-013). The inbox holds email approvals from Customer A for some changes, and no change log was found (EV-014). Customer A's 2026 questionnaire is due 2026-09-30 (EV-015). Customer site packets show hardwired high-level shutdowns at the tank batteries, not connected to the owner's devices or accounts, and storm shut-in steps run by each customer (EV-016).

**Payments, insurance and support.** Statements show recurring payments to the productivity suite, accounting service, phone carrier, internet provider, configuration software makers and the general liability and auto insurer, per-well payments to the dynamometer service from March 2026, and hourly payments to the IT technician. No payment for cyber coverage appears (EV-017). The declarations list general liability and commercial auto only (EV-018). The IT technician's 2024 setup invoice lists disk encryption turned on, and later invoices describe attended sessions (EV-019).

**Documents and training.** No written security policy, risk assessment, incident plan, security contact list, backup procedure, change log, customer device or credential list, provider list, or written coverage agreement with the nearby pumper was found (EV-020). Training certificates cover oilfield safety only (EV-021).

**AI tools.** The dynamometer service has held data for 14 Customer A and B wells since March 2026, uploaded under lease and well names, with password sign-in. Its automatic optimization feature is off and has pushed no setpoints (EV-022). Its terms let the provider use uploads to improve its models, and a paid no-training tier costs about $300 a year more (EV-023). No written consent from Customer A or B was found (EV-024). The free chatbot account shows about 12 chats from April to July 2026 with well names and production for Customers A and C (EV-025).

**Daily records.** The paper gauge book is carried on rounds and a daily gauge sheet is emailed to each customer (EV-026).
