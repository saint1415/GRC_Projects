## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the company's own records before the enterprise BIA, risk analysis and gap analysis began on 2026-06-01, and before Internal Audit's control assessment began on 2026-07-13. It covers the organization (three operating areas, including the AQ-MC assets acquired in 2025-10), the IT and OT systems that monitor and control the field and turn production into sales, royalty payments and partner bills, the two service lines offered to outside parties (SL-1 and SL-2), the vendors that touch them, and the rules that may bind the company. Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (BIA interviews and a recovery test observation, risk workshops, gap analysis samples, walk-throughs and scans, Internal Audit tests, and the AI portfolio review) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

At this size the sources are enterprise systems of record across IT, OT and field operations: the identity platform, PAM and identity governance, the HR system, the OT directory and SCADA configuration, the OT asset inventory and passive OT monitoring, the OT remote access gateway, the SIEM and SOC case system, the cloud consoles, hydrocarbon accounting, the third-party risk register and accounts payable vendor master, the contract repository, pipeline and HSE compliance files, board and committee records, prior Internal Audit and SOX workpapers, and regulator correspondence.

Each register row names the owning system of record and owner. The [asset inventory](asset-inventory.csv) reconciles the OT asset inventory with the identity, endpoint, CMDB and cloud exports; the [vendor register](vendor-register.csv) reconciles the third-party risk register with the accounts payable vendor master and the contract repository.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the gap analysis (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**Identity and access.** Corporate workforce and contractor accounts are federated through SSO with MFA registered; privileged users hold FIDO2 keys, push MFA remains enabled for some non-privileged users, and AQ-MC staff sign in through the seller's directory (EV-001). MFA is required for remote, cloud and privileged access, with lockout after 10 failed attempts (EV-002). Privileged access is vaulted and granted just in time through PAM with session recording; logic downloads on the enterprise platform run through PAM, with no PAM-brokered path at Florida or AQ-MC (EV-003). Identity governance provisions corporate roles from HR events, disables corporate accounts after 60 days of inactivity and ran 4 quarterly IT certifications; the OT identity domain and local SCADA accounts are not connected to it (EV-004). The OT directory disables accounts after 90 days without logon; the Florida HMIs use shared operator logins and Florida local accounts have no inactivity setting (EV-005). HR holds about 12,000 employees; OT account removals are requested by manual ticket, and Florida and AQ-MC receive emailed HR lists (EV-006). The AQ-MC records show the seller's legacy SCADA on one server pair with no restore test record, a flat network reaching the WAN over a site-to-site VPN, shared logins and Integrator B's always-on remote tool (EV-007). The AQ-MC migration is due 2027-06-30, and the AQ-MC deal checklist had no OT section (EV-008).

**Endpoints, OT assets and network.** EDR reports on 97% of about 14,000 corporate endpoints and runs on SCADA servers and HMIs on the enterprise platform (EV-009). Tablets and phones are encrypted with remote wipe (EV-010). The field device inventory lists about 11,000 controllers and is about 82% complete by model and firmware, with firmware not recorded for about 40% of controllers; 2 Florida SCADA servers, 18 Florida HMIs and 8 AQ-MC HMIs run an unsupported operating system (EV-011). OT DMZs sit at the IOC and BCC; the Florida control room has one firewall between corporate and SCADA networks; safety shutdowns run outside SCADA (EV-012). Boundary firewalls are deny by default at the IOC and BCC, and the Florida rule set includes direct corporate-to-SCADA rules (EV-013). Private LTE covers about 1,900 Permian sites and licensed radio about 1,400 sites (EV-014). One carrier serves 88% of about 6,500 field modems, about 70% of cellular sites have no second path, about 780 modems (12%) are on the carrier's public network, and a carrier outage on 2026-02-11 cut polling to about 1,900 sites for 5 hours (EV-015). The OT remote access gateway serves the Permian, the IOC and the BCC; the ESP vendor cloud path and the AQ-MC integrator tool do not pass through it (EV-016). The ESP vendor monitors about 1,800 drives through its own cloud, with setpoint write enabled on 260 drives and no security schedule in the agreement (EV-017). Passive OT monitoring covers the IOC, the BCC and about 70% of Permian facilities; Florida and AQ-MC sources are not listed in the SIEM (EV-018).

**Security operations, cloud, backup and recovery.** The SOC runs 24x7 with OT analysts and MSSP overflow (EV-019). OT vulnerability identification runs at monitored sites only, and OT patches are qualified on the IOC test rack (EV-020). Cloud accounts are created by account vending with guardrails as code and daily posture scans (EV-021). Backups are write-once in separate accounts with customer-managed keys (EV-022). Permian controller programs are in the central repository; Mid-Continent and Florida programs are not, and the Florida servers back up to a storage device in the same room (EV-023). The 2026-04-25 IOC-to-BCC failover took 9 hours against a 4-hour RTO (EV-024). The 2026-05-16 tier-1 DR test recovered the identity platform within 1 hour and hydrocarbon accounting within its 48-hour RTO (EV-025). FSPA contingency plan v3 covers failover, regional manual operations and accounting recovery (EV-026).

**Governance, prior assurance and disclosure.** The 2025 policy set (POL-01 to POL-05) sits in the policy portal with standards, procedures and an exception register (EV-027). The OT security program has been aligned to SP 800-82 Rev. 3 since 2024, with the tailoring register TR-01 to TR-07; the library holds baselines for the enterprise platform and none for the Florida control room or AQ-MC (EV-028). The 2025 risk analysis rolled into 8 enterprise risks, and the board approved the risk appetite in 2026-02 (EV-029, EV-030). Internal Audit's 2025 assessment and the FY2025 SOX ITGC results are on file (EV-031). The CISO reports to the CEO; the GRC team has 10 people and the OT security team 7 (EV-032). The disclosure committee has 7 members, including the COO since 2026; the materiality playbook's scenarios and worksheets do not include an OT scenario or a deferred production estimate, and the minutes record no exercise with an OT scenario (EV-033). The FY2025 10-K includes Item 1C (EV-034). The 2026-03-19 tabletop used a ransomware scenario without the disclosure committee, and the IR plan names no AQ-MC OT incident roles (EV-035). Role-based OT security training is complete for 64% of staff in OT roles, and AQ-MC staff are not enrolled (EV-036).

**Third parties and service lines.** The vendor register lists about 1,600 vendors, 210 with remote access, and holds no assessment for the ESP vendor or Integrator B (EV-037). The AP vendor master shows AI-related subscriptions with four vendors (EV-038). Integrator B's agreement has no incident notice term, the carrier contract has no priority restoration term, and 1 of 3 major purchaser ticket exchanges has no security terms (EV-039). SOC reports are on file for both clouds, both colocation providers, the print and mail vendor, the hydrocarbon accounting software vendor, the ERP vendor and the bank, and not for the ESP vendor or the rig data aggregation SaaS (EV-040). SL-1's first SOC 2 Type 2 report covers 2025 (EV-041). SL-2 serves about 35 operators without a SOC 2 report (EV-042).

**Facilities, media and disposal.** Control rooms use badge plus PIN with quarterly reviews; Permian RTU cabinets have tamper switches (EV-043). Destruction certificates and USB media and kiosk records are on file (EV-044).

**Business volume and structure.** Revenue was about $4.8 billion in FY2025, about $13.2 million per calendar day, with distributions of about $520 million a month (EV-045). The company operates about 8,900 wells, 375 tank batteries, 46 compressor stations, 34 water facilities and 16 LACT units (EV-046). All operations are onshore, and all leases are fee or state (EV-047).

**Pipeline, HSE and obligations.** 44 of about 850 miles of crude gathering lines meet the regulated rural criteria, and the release estimate procedure uses the historian (EV-048). SPCC plans for 31 tank batteries name the SCADA high-level alarm (EV-049). Release and notice records carry discovery and notice times (EV-050). Counsel's view on each candidate rule is in EV-051; the results are in [`obligations-register.csv`](obligations-register.csv). The regulatory log holds no TSA or Coast Guard correspondence (EV-052).

**Other operating records.** Threat intelligence covers IT-to-OT ransomware, supplier and remote access compromise and attacks on internet-exposed field devices (EV-055). Hydrocarbon accounting separates volume entry, allocation, owner record maintenance and payment release (EV-057). Run ticket edits carry an optional reason code, and about 14% of volume is reconciled by hand (EV-058). Web proxy reports show public generative AI sites reachable from some field office networks (EV-065). SSP v2.0 added the AQ-MC interconnection in 2025-11 (EV-067).

**AI.** The AI council register lists 7 use cases, with review records for 6; AI-005 is an advisory pilot with a proposal to write setpoints, and AI-001 was extended to about 2,400 more Permian wells in 2026-05 (EV-072).
