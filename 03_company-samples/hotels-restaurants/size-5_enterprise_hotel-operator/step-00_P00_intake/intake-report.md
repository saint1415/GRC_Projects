## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the company's own records before the enterprise BIA update, the risk analysis and the gap analysis began on 2026-06-01, and before Internal Audit's control assessment began on 2026-07-13. It covers the organization (750 hotels, including the 9 resorts still on the seller's systems), the systems that store, process, or transmit card data or guest personal information or support tier-1 processes, the vendors that touch them, the franchisees the company serves as a service provider, and the rules that may bind the company and its two external service lines (SL-1 and SL-2). Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (BIA interviews, risk workshops, gap analysis samples, Internal Audit tests, the AI portfolio review) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

At this size the sources are enterprise systems of record across the business: the identity platform and HR system, the CMDB, the EDR, network and cloud consoles, the SIEM and SOC case system, the PCI DSS program library with the ROCs and AOCs, the third-party risk register and accounts payable vendor master, the contract repository and franchise AOC tracker, board and committee records, prior Internal Audit and QSA work, and the AI governance committee register. The annual penetration and segmentation test ran during the intake window (2026-05-11 to 2026-05-22) under its own engagement, and its report was collected as intake evidence (EV-014).

Each register row names the owning business unit's system of record and owner. The [asset inventory](asset-inventory.csv) reconciles the CMDB with the cloud account inventory, the EDR and endpoint consoles, the POS estate report and the hotel technology records; the [vendor register](vendor-register.csv) reconciles the third-party risk register with the accounts payable vendor master and the contract repository.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the gap analysis (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**Identity and access.** About 12,000 workforce identities and about 26,400 franchisee staff accounts sign in through the identity platform, with MFA for every PMS and CRS user, including franchisee staff, under a requirement dated 2025-02 (EV-001). Lockout follows 6 failed attempts, and passwords need 12 characters with breached-password screening (EV-002). 64 privileged accounts for the vault, PMS tenant administration and hub firewalls use FIDO2 keys through PAM (EV-003). Joiner, mover and leaver events flow from HR, and workforce accounts inactive 90 days are disabled; the seller's directory is not connected (EV-004). The PMS tenant holds about 36,500 accounts, and the franchise portal has no inactivity disable rule configured (EV-005). HR records 12,000 employees and terminations by hotel (EV-006). About 1,300 resort staff accounts sit in the seller's directory, and resort terminations arrive as manual tickets (EV-007).

**Endpoints, POS and network.** The EDR agent reports on 97% of about 21,000 company endpoints; about 470 legacy POS workstations run the vendor's anti-malware without it (EV-008). PCs, tablets and handhelds follow lock, encryption and enrollment settings (EV-009). The unsupported component register lists 31 door lock servers and the legacy POS workstations at 6 hotels (EV-010). Of 264 food and beverage outlets, 153 run the cloud POS with validated P2PE and 111 at 41 hotels run the legacy integrated POS; the replacement program is 58% complete and due 2027-06-30; 82 of 110 front desks use P2PE terminals (EV-011). The data flow diagram shows legacy POS card data in clear text in workstation memory and on the hotel network, and scope document v9 lists neither the group sales and events mailboxes nor the resort systems (EV-012). The managed property network connects 110 company-operated and 410 franchised hotels to two hubs; the other 230 franchised hotels appear in no company network inventory (EV-013). The 2026-05 penetration test found one hub rule that let franchised hotel segments reach the CRS integration tier on 2 ports (EV-014). The test calendar records no service provider segmentation test for the second half of 2025 (EV-015). Scans run weekly and ASV scans quarterly; legacy POS and lock server patches follow the vendors' schedules (EV-016). Of 14 property-system vendors, 5 reach property systems at 37 hotels through their own remote tools with standing vendor accounts, under exception EXC-2026-017 (EV-017).

**Cloud, logging, backup and recovery.** Both clouds use account vending, guardrails as code and daily posture scans; the vault has its own account with no internet route (EV-018). Vault keys sit in HSMs with split knowledge and dual control (EV-019). The SIEM does not list the 41 legacy POS servers or the resorts' seller systems as log sources; hotel controllers review legacy POS reports locally under EXC-2026-026 (EV-020). The SOC runs 24x7 with MSSP overflow (EV-021). Backups are immutable in separate accounts, with a weekly copy to DC-2 (EV-022). The 2026-04-25 DR test recovered the CRS in 1.6 hours and the vault in 1.8 hours against 2-hour RTOs (EV-023). The PMS vendor's contract states RTO 4 hours and RPO 15 minutes; the secondary gateway covers front desk terminals only (EV-024). The transition services agreement for the 9 resorts states a 24-hour RTO and nightly backups, and the 3 resort booking microsites on the seller's hosting have no payment page script inventory or tamper detection (EV-025). Migration of the 9 resorts is due 2027-03-31, and integration funding was not part of the deal budget (EV-026).

**Governance, prior assurance and disclosure.** The 2025 policy set, standards, brand technology standards and exception register are in the policy portal; the brand standards set no technical minimums for hotels outside the managed network (EV-027). The board approved the risk appetite in 2026-02, and 11 targeted risk analyses are on file (EV-028, EV-029). The 2025 merchant ROC was compliant with 3 compensating controls, and the 2026 service provider ROC reported no exceptions for the vault and tokenization service (EV-030). Independence decisions for the co-source firm and the QSA firms are recorded case by case, with no written rule in the Internal Audit charter or the QSA selection procedure (EV-029, EV-031). The disclosure committee has 7 members, 3 changed in 2026, and the minutes record no exercise of the materiality playbook since; the playbook has no section on incidents that start at a franchised hotel (EV-032). The FY2025 10-K includes Item 1C (EV-033). The 2026-03-24 enterprise tabletop ran without the disclosure committee (EV-034). Training completion for the current cycle is 98.6%, with monthly phishing simulations (EV-035).

**Third parties, contracts, franchisees and service lines.** The vendor register lists about 1,100 vendors, 86 with card data or guest personal information at volume; no AOC dated within the last 12 months is on file for the legacy POS vendor, one lock vendor and the seller's hosting provider (EV-036, EV-037). Franchise agreements since 2022 carry 72-hour notice and PCI DSS acknowledgment terms, and 212 earlier agreements do not; the acquirer and management agreements require notice within 24 hours (EV-038). 141 of 640 franchised hotels (22%) have no 2025 AOC on file, and no default notice is recorded (EV-039). AOCs and SOC 2 reports are on file for the main providers; the distribution switch has an AOC and no SOC 2 report (EV-040). SL-1 issued its 2025 SOC 2 Type 2 report with 2 exceptions (EV-041). SL-2 serves about 180 independent hotels, has no system description yet, and 41 client booking pages have no link to the client's own privacy notice (EV-042). About 92% of card volume runs through one gateway (EV-043).

**Data, loyalty and pricing.** The data discovery scan found about 4,800 emails with full card numbers in group sales and events mailboxes at managed hotels, 390 of them with security codes; 29 hotels accept emailed card authorization forms (EV-044). The guest profile hub holds about 61 million profiles with no deletion rule, about 8.7 million with identity document numbers (EV-045), and the retention schedule has no entry for them (EV-046). Loyalty sign-in uses email and password with optional MFA, with 23% of members enrolled, and credential stuffing traffic is recorded (EV-047). The website and app show the total price including mandatory fees; resort collection fees are not loaded in the chatbot knowledge sources or the resort microsite feeds (EV-048).

**AI.** The AI governance committee register lists 8 use cases with completed review (EV-049). The feature discovery sweep found 4 more AI features switched on in vendor products, including applicant ranking at 22 company-operated hotels (AI-006), for 12 in total (EV-050). The revenue-management contract pools non-public rate and occupancy data across client hotels, and the rate rules have no automatic cap for declared emergencies (EV-051). Mobile check-in face matching is disabled at Illinois hotels, where time clocks are badge-based (EV-052).

**Business volume and structure.** Revenue was about $4.8 billion in FY2025, about $13.2 million per calendar day (EV-053). The system books about 24.5 million room nights and about 38 million reservation transactions a year (EV-054). The company has 750 hotels in 33 states and DC, 118 of them in Florida (EV-055).

**Obligations.** Counsel's view on each candidate rule is in EV-056, supported by the co-brand card agreement (EV-057), the background screening records (EV-058), the privacy notice and state breach matrix (EV-059) and the cyber insurance policy (EV-060). The results are in [`obligations-register.csv`](obligations-register.csv).
