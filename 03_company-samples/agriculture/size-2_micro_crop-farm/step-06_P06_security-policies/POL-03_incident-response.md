## 1. Purpose
Make sure the farm spots, contains, reports, and recovers from security incidents quickly and lawfully, keeps people and crops safe while it does, and meets every notice deadline.

## 2. Scope
All workforce members of Cris Santos Company and every system and copy of farm information, including systems the MSP, the irrigation dealer, the equipment dealer, and SaaS vendors run for the farm, and the irrigation equipment. A "security incident" includes any attempted or successful unauthorized access, use, disclosure, change, or destruction of information, any interference with a system or with irrigation equipment, a lost or stolen device, and information sent to the wrong person.

## 3. Roles and responsibilities
| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Office Manager (Security Coordinator) | Incident lead; keeps the incident log; prepares the breach determination with counsel |
| Owner and General Manager | Decision maker; calls the cyber insurer; approves outside communications, spending, and any ransom decision; makes the Florida breach determination |
| Irrigation and Equipment Technician | Safety and OT lead: puts irrigation in a safe state and decides when automation can be trusted again |
| Field Supervisor | Runs paper records for hours and Produce Safety; briefs the crew in English and Spanish |
| MSP | Technical response on the office computers, suite, firewall, and backup: isolate, investigate, rebuild, restore; preserves logs |

| All workforce | Report suspected incidents at once |

## 4. Policy statements
4.1 The farm must keep an incident response runbook for its most likely serious incident, ransomware on the farm-management and irrigation control systems (P08), with printed copies and the contact card in the farm office, at the pump station, and at the Owner and General Manager's home. (IR-8; RS.MA-01)

4.2 Workforce members must report any suspected incident to the Security Coordinator or the Owner and General Manager **at once, and within 1 hour at most**, in person or by phone. Examples: clicking a suspicious link, a strange pop-up or locked files, a lost phone or tablet, an email asking to change payment details, or a pump, pivot, or fertigation pump starting, stopping, or changing when nobody scheduled it. (IR-6; RS.MA-02)

4.3 **Safety first.** If irrigation equipment may be affected, the Technician or the first trained person on site must put pumps and pivots in local or hand control and turn fertigation off before anything else. Automation is not trusted again until the Technician confirms the controller program and settings match the approved version. (IR-4; RS.MI-01)

4.4 The Security Coordinator must log every incident, including those that turn out to be harmless, with the date found, what happened, what was done, and the outcome. (IR-5)

4.5 For any incident that may involve personal information, the Owner and General Manager, advised by breach counsel, must decide whether a breach of security occurred under Fla. Stat. 501.171 and record **the date of that determination**, because the 30-day notice clocks run from it. A decision not to notify individuals because the breach will not likely result in identity theft or other financial harm must be made only after an investigation and consultation with law enforcement, documented in writing, kept for at least 5 years, and sent to the Department of Legal Affairs within 30 days (501.171(4)(c)). (IR-6; RS.AN-03)

4.6 Notices to individuals, the Department of Legal Affairs, consumer reporting agencies, other states' residents, and the packer-shipper must meet the deadlines in the P08 notification matrix. Breach counsel reviews each notice before it is sent. Notices to workers are sent in English and Spanish. (IR-6; RS.CO-02)

4.7 For any suspected ransomware, data theft, account takeover, or tampering with irrigation, the Owner and General Manager must call the cyber insurer's breach hotline before hiring any outside firm, and the MSP must be engaged under its contract. The irrigation dealer works on the pump station only on site, or through a session the Technician approves, until the incident is closed. (IR-4; RS.MA-01; GV.SC-08)

4.8 No ransom may be paid without the Owner and General Manager's approval, advice from breach counsel and the insurer, and an OFAC sanctions check. (IR-4)

4.9 Suppliers must report incidents affecting the farm as their agreements and Fla. Stat. 501.171(6) require. The Security Coordinator logs each report and handles it under this policy. (IR-6; SA-9; GV.SC-08)

4.10 During an outage the farm must keep making its required records on paper: daily hours and Produce Safety records signed by the person who did the work, and H-2A earnings statements issued on or before each payday. (IR-4; 20 CFR 655.122(k); 21 CFR 112.161(a))

4.11 The runbook must be tested every year with a tabletop exercise that includes the MSP and the irrigation dealer, and the hand-operation procedure must be drilled before each watermelon season. (IR-3; ID.IM-02)

4.12 Lessons learned must be written up within 30 days of closing any incident that required outside help or notification, and fed into the risk register and training. (IR-4; ID.IM-03)

## 5. Compliance and enforcement
Breaking this policy leads to action under POL-02 A.4. Failing to report is a violation; reporting a mistake in good faith is never sanctioned. Compliance is checked in the annual assessment (P07), the annual tabletop, and the pre-season drill.

## 6. Exceptions
Exceptions follow POL-02 A.9. No exception may delay a legally required notice or a step needed for worker safety.
