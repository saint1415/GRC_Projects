## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the group's own records before any assessment work began on 2026-05-01. It covers the legal entities, the IT and OT systems in each division and in corporate shared services, the data they hold (worker and H-2A records, farm and food safety records, grower credit and portal data, card data), the suppliers that touch them, and the rules that may bind each division and the group. Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Items are grouped by division: the title of each row starts with Group, Crop Farming, Food Processing or Farm Supply. Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (interviews, record samples, tests) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement or the CSF 2.0 benchmark is decided in the P03 regulatory analyses and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**Entities and scale.** The holding company is an SEC registrant with three operating subsidiaries; corporate shared services sit in the parent; no role is defined for the irrigation integrator (EV-001). The workforce peaks at 45,000: Crop Farming about 19,000 (about 7,500 year-round and about 11,500 seasonal, about 9,000 of them H-2A workers), Food Processing about 17,000, Farm Supply about 5,500, corporate about 3,500. Seasonal assignments close at season end without an HR termination event (EV-002). FY2025 revenue was about $18.0 billion after eliminations: Food Processing about $10.6 billion, Farm Supply about $4.8 billion, Crop Farming about $2.6 billion external plus about $1.5 billion intercompany (EV-003).

**Group identity and access.** MFA covers all workforce email and remote access, administrators use phishing-resistant MFA, and the FMIS web console and cloud administration are federated; the farm operations directory is not (EV-004). Identity governance runs from HR events and disables accounts after 60 days of inactivity; the Q1 2026 certification covered SYS-G4 and division IT applications, not ROC SCADA, the farm directory or the FMIS tenant; local FMIS crew accounts are outside identity governance (EV-005). IT administrators use just-in-time PAM with session recording, and vendor sessions to plant OT and legacy farm ROCs run through the PAM gateway; farm directory domain administrators and the acquired farms' integrator accounts are not in PAM (EV-006).

**Group security operations.** The SOC runs 24x7 over IT sources in all three divisions; the farm operations directory, the FMIS audit trail and the integrator remote tool are not SIEM sources (EV-007). Passive OT sensors cover all 11 food facilities and 9 of 38 farms, all legacy, and none of the 14 acquired farms (EV-008). EDR covers IT, branch POS and legacy ROC SCADA servers, not acquired farm servers or HMIs (EV-009). Farm OT devices are not in the scan scope; acquired farm HMIs have no patch record; no OT penetration test is on record (EV-010). OT advisories reach the OT security managers but have no step to farm technicians (EV-011). IR plan v5 has no farm OT, freeze-season or food safety sections, no exercise has included farm OT, and the notification matrix covers Farm Supply card data only (EV-012). Cases from all divisions are tracked to closure in one system (EV-013).

**Disclosure, board, risk and policies.** Item 1C describes board oversight and the Group CISO's role but not OT or integrators, and no disclosure exercise has used an OT or food safety incident (EV-014). Board cyber reports carry IT metrics only (EV-015). The risk strategy sets one method and acceptance levels by division and group (EV-016). The 2024 group policies are aligned to CSF 2.0; the Crop Farming supplement v2023 has no OT remote access, OT change or seasonal account rules, and the library has no AI standard (EV-017). Inheritance from the 118 common controls is documented for Food Processing, Farm Supply and Crop Farming division IT, with no entries for the Crop Farming OT estate (EV-018).

**Group cloud platform.** Provider A hosts the landing zone, ERP servers, the farm data hub and imagery store, traceability, Farm Supply e-commerce and warehouse management, and the grower credit share; provider B hosts the portal, the DR replica and the immutable vault (EV-019). The hub network denies traffic between division accounts by default, but its route table attaches the farm data hub account to the ROC networks and its rules permit connections from the hub toward ROC SCADA (EV-020). Backups to the provider B vault are immutable and cloud restores are tested quarterly (EV-021). Keys are managed per division account (EV-022).

**Workforce, vendors, contracts, records and AI.** Staff with email take annual awareness training and phishing exercises; seasonal tablet users get food safety training at hire and no security module, and none exists in Spanish (EV-023). Identity is proofed at hire, including H-2A documents (EV-024). H-2A passport, visa and home-country address data sit in the HR and payroll SaaS, and payroll tally exports are staged on the farm data hub (EV-025). The vendor program tiers the FMIS vendor as critical; the integrator, carrier and controller vendors are not tiered, and the SCRM plan covers IT vendors (EV-026). SOC 2 reports are on file for the cloud providers and key IT vendors; the FMIS vendor's 2026 report was requested (EV-027). The IR retainer covers IT only (EV-028). No retention rule covers farm records in the FMIS (EV-029). Internal audit and an independent OT firm are engaged for 2026 (EV-030). There are no DoD contracts; USDA contracts sit in Food Processing; no breach contact is registered with the H-2A filing agent (EV-031). AI in use or proposed: AI-001 to AI-010, with no AI standard or approved-tools list (EV-033).

**Crop Farming.** 38 farms (24 legacy, 14 acquired in 2024) on about 240,000 acres; freeze protection on about 4,200 acres of strawberries served by ROC-1 (EV-034). The acquisition file holds no security review (EV-035), and the integrator agreement has no security, notice or personnel terms; the integrator holds the acquired farms' PLC programs and approves its own changes (EV-036). At all 14 acquired farms a remote tool on the site servers stays connected, with one shared account and no MFA (EV-037). The farm operations directory serves all 3 ROCs and the acquired farms; its domain administrators are outside PAM and have no MFA, and its events are not sent to the SIEM (EV-038). About 2,600 named FMIS users sign in through SYS-G1; crew lead logins are shared at 21 farms, crew roles can edit tally after submission, and no audit trail review report has been run (EV-039). About 3,400 tablets are managed and encrypted (EV-040). HMIs stay signed in on shift accounts, and one shared integrator engineer account covers the acquired farms (EV-041). Baselines, logging, range limits and safe-state settings exist at legacy farms and not at acquired farms (EV-042). The OT inventory lists about 38,000 devices; acquired farms come from integrator spreadsheets; HMIs at 6 acquired farms run an unsupported operating system; two replaced HMIs have no wipe record (EV-043). No OT DMZ is built; each acquired farm runs a flat network, and 5 acquired farm pump houses have direct internet links (EV-044). The farm data hub sends one nightly harvest lot file to Food Processing with no integrity check (EV-045). FMIS module setpoint changes and acquired farm PLC changes have no review record or ticket (EV-046). The contingency plan v2025 has no loss-of-ROC-1 scenario; manual freeze procedures exist for legacy farms only; ROC failover and image restores have no test record (EV-047). Farm records have no retention rule or farm-held export (EV-048). Buyer agreements require notice within 24 hours (EV-049). The FMIS contract requires vendor notice within 72 hours and allows full export (EV-050). Dealer technicians hold standing telematics access (EV-052). Acquired farm pump houses are padlocked with shared keys and field cabinets have no tamper alarms (EV-053). Yield model versions carry no validation record or change ticket and feed three functions (EV-054).

**Food Processing.** 11 registered facilities, with group counsel's 2024 review applying Part 121 to all 11 (EV-056). The food defense vulnerability assessments treat steps that depend on PLC setpoints, dosing and recipes as physical equipment, and the repository is readable by 140 plant staff (EV-057). The food safety plans assume setpoints change only through a work order (EV-058). Some monitoring records come from line historians that allow operator edits (EV-059). Plant MES trusts the plant IT domain; some HMIs use shared operator logins; 12 HMIs are past end of support (EV-060). Ammonia refrigeration exceeds the PSM and RMP thresholds at 2 plants, and contractor logic and setpoint changes appear as PAM sessions, not management of change entries (EV-061). No mock recall has been run with the traceability system or the farm data hub down (EV-062). The recall plan does not list control system tampering as a trigger (EV-063). USDA contracts carry the FAR clauses, and no document maps 52.204-21 to group controls (EV-064). Customer agreements require notice within 24 hours (EV-065). Printed plans are kept at 7 plants and not at the 4 packinghouses (EV-066).

**Farm Supply.** The 2026 report on compliance finds all 12 PCI DSS requirements in place (EV-067). About 12,000 individual growers' credit applications include Social Security numbers, and grower credit is business credit (EV-068). The portal serves about 8,200 growers, about 5,400 of them through 22 reselling cooperatives; MFA is optional for growers and cooperative administrators; the 2025 terms commit to 72-hour cooperative notice and service-only data use (EV-069). The AI prescription feature was released on 2026-03-01 with no security, privacy or validation record attached, trains on pooled grower data, and is marketed as optimizing input rates (EV-070). Cooperatives have asked for a SOC 2 Type 2 report (EV-071).
