At the Micro tier the company keeps three core policies: access control (POL-02), incident response (POL-03), and data classification and records handling (POL-04). A separate Information Security Policy would add little for a 7-person office, so its essential rules live in **POL-02 Part A. Program governance**:

| Essential rule | Where it lives | Rule it supports |
|---|---|---|
| Designation of the Security Coordinator | POL-02 A.1 | 19 CFR 111.28(a) (responsible supervision and control) |
| Annual risk assessment and risk treatment | POL-02 A.2 | Fla. Stat. 501.171(2) (reasonable measures) |
| Who may accept risk | POL-02 A.3 | Internal |
| Sanctions | POL-02 A.4 | 19 CFR 111.28(a)(2) |
| Vendor approval, U.S. storage, and client authorization | POL-02 A.5 | 19 CFR 111.23(a); 111.24 |
| Annual independent evaluation | POL-02 A.6 | 19 CFR 111.28(a)(8) |
| 5-year retention of security records | POL-02 A.7 | 19 CFR 111.23(b) |
| Policy review and availability to staff | POL-02 A.8 | 19 CFR 111.28(a)(2) |
| Exceptions process | POL-02 A.9 | Internal |

Traceability for these rules is in `policy-control-map.csv` under POL-02. Revisit this choice if the company grows past the Micro tier (10 or more employees) or opens a second office.
