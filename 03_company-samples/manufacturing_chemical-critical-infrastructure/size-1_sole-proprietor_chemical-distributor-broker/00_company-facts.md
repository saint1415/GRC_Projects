# Scenario facts: Cris Santos Company | Chemical | Sole Proprietorship

All 10 deliverables in this folder use the facts below. The company is fictitious. This scenario is independent of the other sizes. Regulatory text was read from eCFR (point-in-time 2026-09-23), the Federal Register, and the Florida Legislature's statutes site between 2026-09-25 and 2026-10-05.

## 1. The organization
| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Legal name | Cris Santos Company (sole proprietorship; the owner files Schedule C) |
| Business | Specialty chemical distributor (broker). Buys water treatment and sanitation chemicals from manufacturers and resells them to industrial customers. Every order is **drop-shipped**: a for-hire carrier picks it up at the manufacturer's plant or terminal and delivers it to the customer. The business takes title (merchant wholesaler, NAICS 424690) but never takes physical possession. It has no warehouse, tanks, trucks, or chemical inventory |
| Location | Florida. A home office in the owner's residence. No chemicals are ever at the home |
| Workforce | The owner only (0 employees). The owner started the business in 2022 after 15 years in sales at a regional chemical distributor |
| Customers | About 40 active business customers in Florida and south Georgia: cooling water treatment, food and beverage plant sanitation, commercial laundries, and pool service companies. About 75 orders a year |
| Suppliers | Five manufacturers and terminals: two hydrogen peroxide producers' terminals, one chlor-alkali producer (sodium hypochlorite and sodium hydroxide), one ferric chloride producer, and one specialty polymer formulator |
| Revenue | About $180,000 a year in receipts (fictional). SBA-small (standard 175 employees for NAICS 424690; 13 CFR 121.201) |
| Hazmat role | The business is an **offeror** under the Hazardous Materials Regulations (HMR) for its drop shipments (49 CFR 171.8; 171.1(b)). For each order the owner confirms the hazard class from the manufacturer's safety data sheet (SDS) against the Hazardous Materials Table, prepares the bill of lading (BOL) with the hazmat description, the emergency response telephone number, and the shipper's certification, books the carrier, and sends the supplier a pickup authorization. The supplier fills, marks, labels, placards, and loads. Both are offerors for the functions each performs (171.2(b)) |
| Hazmat registration | Registered with PHMSA (49 CFR 107.601(a)(4) and (a)(6): bulk loads of 3,500 gallons or more and placarded quantities). Current Certificate of Registration covers the registration year 2026-07-01 to 2027-06-30 |
| Hazmat security plan | **Required, and none existed before this project.** Bulk 50% hydrogen peroxide is a large bulk quantity of a Division 5.1 material in Packing Group II (172.800(b)(10)). The owner believed the suppliers' own security plans covered the drop shipments. They cover the suppliers' functions, not the broker's |
| Emergency response information | Contract with a 24-hour emergency response information (ERI) provider under 49 CFR 172.604(b)(2). The BOL template shows the provider's number and the company's contract number |
| CFATS status | Not applicable. The business has no establishment that possesses chemicals (6 CFR 27.105 "chemical facility"), and CFATS authority lapsed on 2023-07-28 and has not been reauthorized (vertical registry, verified 2026-09-25; no CFATS document in the Federal Register in 2026 as of 2026-10-05). Hydrogen peroxide's former status as a CFATS theft and diversion chemical of interest is why diversion matters here. RBPS (a)(5), (a)(6), and (a)(8) are used as a **voluntary benchmark** in P03 |
| EPA RMP and OSHA PSM | Not applicable. No stationary source or process: 40 CFR 68.3 excludes transportation, including storage incident to transportation, from "stationary source", and 68.10(a) applies to owners or operators of stationary sources. OSHA PSM needs a process and employees |
| Not in scope | USCG MTSA cybersecurity rule (no MTSA facility; 33 CFR 101.605). CIRCIA (proposed only; as proposed the business is far below the SBA size criterion). DEA listed chemicals: the product line excludes List I and List II chemicals (21 CFR 1310.02 lists hydrochloric acid, sulfuric acid, and potassium permanganate; the owner declined to add the two acids in 2025). EAR: domestic sales only. Federal contracts: none. SEC: not applicable. HIPAA: not applicable |
| Regulatory driver IDs | **C-CHEMICAL-R01** (CFATS RBPS, voluntary benchmark), **C-CHEMICAL-R02** (USCG MTSA rule, not applicable), **C-CHEMICAL-R03** (CIRCIA, proposed). The binding rules for this business, the DOT HMR (49 CFR Parts 107, 171, 172) and Fla. Stat. 501.171, are cited directly because the vertical registry has no ID for them |
| State law approach | Florida law is cited only where unavoidable: Fla. Stat. 501.171 (driver identity data and customer records). The owner is a "covered entity" (501.171(1)(b) names a sole proprietorship). Drivers from other states are handled under "each state where affected individuals reside" |

## 4. Current security posture: early (few formal controls)
**In place today:**
- MFA on the accounting SaaS (vendor-enforced) and text-message codes on the bank portal
- Built-in disk encryption on the laptop; passcode and encryption on the phone
- Automatic OS updates and built-in antivirus on the laptop and phone
- PHMSA hazmat registration current for 2026-2027
- ERI provider contract; the BOL template carries the provider's number and the contract number
- Initial HMR training (general awareness, function-specific shipping papers, safety, and security awareness) completed and tested in 2022-06, with a certificate
- Pickup authorizations: the owner emails each supplier the carrier, driver name, truck number, and a pickup number for every load
- An end-use statement on file for each hydrogen peroxide customer (required by the producers' product stewardship terms)
- A cross-cut shredder in the home office

**Missing:**
1. No written hazmat transportation security plan or transportation security risk assessment, although bulk 50% hydrogen peroxide requires one (172.800(b)(10); 172.802).
2. Recurrent HMR training overdue since 2025-06 (every three years, 172.704(c)(2)); no in-depth security training (172.704(a)(5)).
3. No MFA on the email account, which holds pickup authorizations, BOLs, and driver data. Its password is reused on a carrier portal.
4. Orders, ship-to changes, carrier or driver changes, and bank-detail changes are accepted by email alone, with no call-back to a known number.
5. The ERI provider's product list was last updated in 2024. Ferric chloride, added in 2025, was not registered with the provider (172.604(b)(2)).
6. No backup of email and files. BOL copies that must be kept for two years (172.201(e)) exist only in one account.
7. Driver license numbers stay in email indefinitely, with no retention or disposal rule.
8. Default router admin password; family members use the business laptop; daily work in an administrator account.
9. No incident plan, contact list, or cyber insurance.
10. No written policies, vendor list, or review of SaaS vendors' security.
11. The generative AI assistant was used with customer prices and SDS text, with the model-improvement setting on, and with no review rule for the hazmat descriptions it drafted.
12. Single-person dependency: no one else can answer customers, hold a release, or reach the accounts if the owner is unavailable. The ERI provider is the only 24-hour coverage.
