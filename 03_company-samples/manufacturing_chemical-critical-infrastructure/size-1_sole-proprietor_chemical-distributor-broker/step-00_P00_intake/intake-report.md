## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the business's own records before the self-assessment began on 2026-09-08. A one-person brokerage has no HR system, asset database or internal audit, so the systems of record are the SaaS admin consoles, the bank portal, the browser and device settings screens, the email inbox and file storage, the order and shipment workbook, bank and card statements, the signed agreements folder, the PHMSA and training provider portals, the insurance agent's portal, and a walk through the home office. Intake covers the organization, the systems that release, describe and pay for each shipment, the vendors and partners that touch them, and the rules that may bind the business. Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (self-reviews, walk-throughs, tests, calls) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the gap analysis (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**Accounts and sign-in.** The email suite has one user, the owner, whose daily account is also the administrator. It signs in with a password; MFA is available and turned off, and account recovery goes by text to the phone. The provider keeps a sign-in history and a settings log and filters spam (EV-001). The accounting SaaS has the owner and the outside accountant as separate users, and the vendor enforces MFA for both (EV-003). The bank portal has one user with sign-in codes by text; payees can be added online, and payee-change alerts are off (EV-004). Sign-ins for 9 supplier, carrier and ERI provider portals are saved in the browser. The browser's password check flags the email password as also used for one carrier portal, and no password manager is installed (EV-005).

**Records in the email and file suite.** Pickup authorizations, BOL PDFs, the order and shipment workbook, the SDS library, end-use statements, the registration and training certificates, and carrier emails with driver names and license numbers back to 2022 are all in the suite. No retention or deletion rule is set, and no copy of these files was found outside the suite. The laptop's disk encryption recovery key is saved there too (EV-002). No third-party backup is connected, and deleted items can be recovered only within the provider's retention period (EV-001).

**Devices and home office.** The laptop has one administrator account, used for daily work and, by the owner's account, sometimes by family members. Disk encryption, antivirus, automatic updates and the host firewall are on (EV-006). The phone has a passcode, encryption, find-my-device and automatic updates on. It receives every sign-in code and holds photos of signed BOLs from drivers. The carrier account has no port-out or SIM-change PIN (EV-007). The home office holds no chemicals. Paper is kept in a locked desk drawer and shredded with a cross-cut shredder. The ISP router's admin credentials are printed on its label, the owner states they were never changed, and the household shares one Wi-Fi network with the business laptop (EV-008).

**Documents, registration and training.** No written hazmat security plan, transportation security risk assessment, security policy, incident plan, contact list, vendor list, record of a vendor security review, retention rule, coverage arrangement, or rule for checking AI-drafted text was found (EV-009). The PHMSA Certificate of Registration covers 2026-07-01 to 2027-06-30 under 107.601(a)(4) and (a)(6), and the portal shows a registration each year since 2022 (EV-010). The training provider account shows one HMR course with test, completed in 2022-06, with general awareness, function-specific, safety and security awareness modules. No later course and no in-depth security training appear (EV-011).

**Shipments and partners.** The ERI provider contract is current, the BOL template prints the provider's number and the contract number, and the latest product list confirmation in the inbox is dated 2024 (EV-012). The workbook shows about 40 customers in Florida and south Georgia, about 75 orders a year, 5 suppliers, about 6 carriers, every order drop-shipped, and about 6 hydrogen peroxide cargo tank loads a year to 2 customers. Ferric chloride was first sold in 2025. No List I or List II chemical and no export order appears (EV-013). An end-use statement is on file for each hydrogen peroxide customer (EV-014). The supplier agreements put filling, marking, placarding, loading and the dock check of the driver with the supplier, and the producers' stewardship terms require end-use statements and a yearly distributor review (EV-015). Each pickup authorization email gives the carrier, driver name, truck number and pickup number. Ship-to, carrier, driver and bank-detail changes arrive by email and were answered by email, with no note of a call-back (EV-016). On 2026-06-17 a message from a lookalike of a customer's domain asked for a hydrogen peroxide tote to go to a new address. It reached the inbox, no order was entered, and nothing was logged or reported (EV-017). A 2026-05 thread shows a supplier's clerk questioning Packing Group II on a ferric chloride BOL, and the owner's corrected BOL with Packing Group III (EV-018).

**Payments, vendors and insurance.** Statements show recurring payments to the email suite, accounting SaaS, ERI provider, phone carrier, ISP, the liability insurer and the AI assistant vendor (monthly from 2026-03), plus the CPA firm, the attorney, the computer shop, suppliers and carriers. No cyber insurance premium appears (EV-019). The computer shop's invoices show hourly help and one remote-support session in 2025 (EV-020). The insurance declarations list general and products liability and no cyber coverage (EV-021). Invoices print the company's bank account for ACH, and about half of customers paid by ACH in the last 12 months (EV-025).

**Business volume.** The 2025 Schedule C shows gross receipts of about $180,000 and no wages (EV-022).

**Vendor assurance and AI tools.** The email suite provider's SOC 2 Type 2 report covers Security, Availability and Confidentiality for the 12 months ending 2026-06-30, with a bridge letter dated 2026-07-15. It lists the customer's own controls, among them enabling MFA, reviewing sign-in logs, and backing up the customer's data (EV-023). The AI assistant is a paid individual plan used since 2026-03. Its terms let the vendor use chats to improve its models unless the user turns that setting off (EV-024).
