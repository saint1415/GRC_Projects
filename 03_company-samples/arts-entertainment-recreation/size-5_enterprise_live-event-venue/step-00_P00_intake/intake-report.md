## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the company's own records before the enterprise risk analysis, the BIA and the gap analysis began on 2026-06-01, and before Internal Audit's control assessment fieldwork began on 2026-07-13. It covers the organization (36 venues, 3 festivals and the six acquired venues AV-01 to AV-06), the systems that store, process or transmit card or patron data or support tier-1 processes, the vendors that touch them, and the rules that may bind the company as a merchant, as a PCI DSS service provider to client venues, as an SEC registrant, and through its two service lines (SL-1 and SL-2). Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (BIA interviews, risk workshops, gap analysis samples and scans, Internal Audit tests, AI monitoring records) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

At this size the sources are enterprise systems of record across business units: the identity platform and the human capital management system, the CMDB, P2PE device inventory and venue OT register, the cloud consoles and SIEM, the third-party risk register and accounts payable vendor master, the contract repository, the PCI DSS program files and the 2025 Reports on Compliance, board and committee records, and SOX and Internal Audit files.

Each register row names the owning business unit's system of record and owner. The [asset inventory](asset-inventory.csv) reconciles the CMDB with the cloud account inventory, the endpoint and EDR consoles, the P2PE device inventory and the venue OT register; the [vendor register](vendor-register.csv) reconciles the third-party risk register with the accounts payable vendor master and the contract repository.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the gap analysis (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**Identity and access.** Workforce accounts are federated through SSO with MFA registered, including about 2,900 with TVOP access; the about 640 workforce members at AV-01 to AV-06 sign in through a legacy directory that is not federated (EV-001). MFA is required for every SSO application, FIDO2 keys for privileged and CDE roles, and lockout follows 10 failed attempts (EV-002). There are 312 privileged and CDE accounts, with just-in-time elevation through PAM and session recording (EV-003). Joiner, mover and leaver events flow from the human capital management system, workforce accounts inactive 60 days are disabled, and quarterly CDE certifications are recorded; the AV directory and client user accounts are not connected to these workflows (EV-004). HR holds about 12,000 employees, and AV terminations reach the local IT support firm by a weekly email (EV-005). The AV directory has about 180 accounts with box office or POS access, disable dates entered by hand, and shared clerk logins on AV terminals (EV-006). The client console has about 9,400 client users at about 340 clients, created by client administrators, with 38% enrolled in MFA (EV-007). There are 127 client integration API keys with no source IP restrictions (EV-008).

**Endpoints, devices, OT and network.** EDR reports on 97% of workstations, servers and box office PCs, and on about 40% of the about 150 AV POS terminals (EV-009). About 410 POS terminals and OT workstations at AV venues and older venues run operating systems past their vendor support date, and AV terminals run local administrator accounts (EV-010). The inventories list about 2,900 P2PE devices at 30 venues, about 7,500 scanners and about 900 turnstile and access control devices; AV devices come from the local IT firm's records (EV-011). The venue OT register holds about 70% of the estimated OT devices, and OT shares network segments with venue corporate networks at 14 venues, 3 of them SL-2 managed venues (EV-012). Four building management integrators hold remote access outside PAM at 9 venues (EV-013). AV-01 to AV-06 run on legacy site VPNs and single carriers, with POS terminals on the same flat networks as office PCs; CDE accounts deny by default with an egress allow-list (EV-014). Scans run weekly, ASV scans have passed each quarter since Q3 2025 (EV-015), and the 2026-03 penetration test and the 2025-11 and 2026-05 segmentation tests are on file (EV-016).

**Cloud, logging, backup and recovery.** Both clouds use account vending with PCI scope tags, guardrails as code and daily posture scans; the edge provider fronts every public endpoint (EV-017). Backups sit in separate accounts with write-once retention and a weekly copy to DC-2, with quarterly restore tests of the order databases (EV-018). Keys are customer-managed, and card data keys are held by the tokenization provider (EV-019). The SIEM does not list the AV POS terminals or venue OT as log sources, and no use case covers client template changes (EV-020). The SOC runs 24x7 with MSSP overflow; AV incidents are handled first by the local IT firm (EV-021). The regional failover test on 2026-04-25 recovered in 3.2 hours against a 2-hour RTO; the processor routing test on 2026-03-11 and the tier-1 DR test on 2026-05-16 are on file (EV-022). Contingency plan v3 describes the edge provider fallback with no test record and has no tokenization provider exit section (EV-023).

**Governance, prior assurance and disclosure.** POL-01 to POL-05 are in force with standards, procedures and an exception register (EV-024). The 2025 risk analysis rolled into 8 enterprise risks, and the board approved the risk appetite and tolerance thresholds in 2026-02 (EV-025, EV-026). The 2025 merchant and service provider Reports on Compliance were Compliant, and the QSA is engaged for 2026 (EV-027). The Internal Audit plan includes the TVOP, and FY2025 SOX IT general control results are on file (EV-028). The 2026 PCI DSS scope document added AV-01 to AV-06 in 2026-03; the contact center platform and case notes are not listed in scope, and service provider scope was last confirmed in 2025-10 (EV-029). The disclosure committee's 2024 materiality playbook addresses incidents in the company's own systems and not incidents where it acts as a service provider, and the minutes record no payment card breach scenario (EV-042). The FY2025 Form 10-K includes Item 1C (EV-043). The incident response plan lists no notification path for client merchants and their acquirers; the last tabletop was on 2025-10-21 (ransomware) (EV-044). The common control catalog names 9 providers (EV-063), and TVOP SSP version 1.1 added the AV box offices on 2026-03-02 (EV-064).

**Payment pages, contact centers and data.** The own-brand checkout has a script inventory, content security policy, integrity hashes and change detection every 15 minutes; client template settings let client administrators add their own tag containers to the checkout page shell, and the controls are configured for the own-brand checkout only (EV-030). Marketing tag vendors are not in the third-party risk register (EV-031). The in-house contact center uses keypad masking; the outsourced overflow center takes card numbers by voice with no keypad masking, and calls are recorded (EV-032). About 6.5 million contacts a year, about 35% at the overflow center (EV-033). The data warehouse holds about 41 million patron records with 10 years of order history and approximate app check-in locations for about 2.3 million patrons; 14 service accounts use passwords without key-pair authentication or network policies (EV-034). The retention schedule sets 7 years for order history (EV-035).

**Third parties, contracts and service lines.** The vendor register lists about 1,450 vendors, 230 with patron, card or employee data; 58 of the 230 are past their reassessment date; 31 service providers have PCI DSS impact; the overflow center's AOC on file expires 2026-05-31 with no renewal on file (EV-036). AP records show payments to marketing tag vendors, the AV legacy POS vendor and the local IT firm, and to AI vendors (EV-037). The tokenization agreement has no exit or token migration clause, and the merchant agreement requires acquirer notice within 24 hours of a suspected compromise (EV-038). SL-1 client agreements commit 99.9% monthly availability and 72-hour client notice; SL-2 owner agreements require monthly reporting, settlement statements and 72-hour notice (EV-039). Provider AOCs and SOC reports are on file, including the tokenization provider's AOC of 2026-03 (EV-040). The SL-1 2025 SOC 2 Type 2 report reported no exceptions (EV-041).

**Workforce, privacy, physical and disposal.** Annual training, quarterly phishing simulations, a card handling module for event staff and annual attestations are recorded (EV-045). The privacy notice was updated in 2025 and does not describe bot detection signals; opt-out preference signals are honored on own-brand sites and not configured on client templates (EV-046). Badge access and quarterly access list reviews cover venue IT closets and colocation cages (EV-047). Certificates of destruction are on file (EV-048).

**Business volume and structure.** Revenue was about $4.8 billion in FY2025, about $13.2 million per calendar day (EV-049). The platform issues about 52 million tickets a year, with gross ticket sales of about $12.6 million on an average day, and about 41 million patron accounts (EV-050). Own card transactions are about 49 million a year (Visa about 25 million), and client orders transmitted are about 15 million (Visa about 7.8 million) (EV-051). The company runs 36 venues in 8 states, and AV-01 to AV-06 were acquired on 2026-02-02 (EV-052). The AV venues keep about 150 legacy POS terminals and readers, local POS servers backed up nightly to local disks with no restore test record, and single carriers; migration is due 2027-03-31 (EV-053). Scanners use an offline mode with ticket lists cached 30 minutes before doors (EV-054).

**AI.** The committee register lists 13 use cases, 8 with completed review; AI-005, AI-006, AI-010, AI-012 and AI-013 have not been reviewed. AI-001 test records do not include accessible seating price parity at every venue, and AI-002 test records do not include a test with assistive technology (EV-055). The review of the SSO catalog and vendor feature settings found the AI features in use already listed in the register (EV-056).

**Obligations.** The company holds no gaming licenses and has no Nevada venues, provides first aid through contracted ambulance services and holds no federal contracts (EV-059). Its sites and app are general audience with accounts for ages 18 and older (EV-060). The acquirer confirmed it is not a designated entity (EV-058). Counsel's view on each candidate rule is in EV-057; the results are in [`obligations-register.csv`](obligations-register.csv).
