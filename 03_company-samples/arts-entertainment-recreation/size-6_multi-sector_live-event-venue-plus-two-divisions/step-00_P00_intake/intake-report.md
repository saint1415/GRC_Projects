## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the group's own records before any assessment work began on 2026-05-01. It covers the legal entities, the systems that store, process, or transmit card data or patron, guest, and subscriber data in each division and in corporate shared services, the suppliers that touch them, and the rules that may bind each division and the group. Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Items are grouped by division: the title of each row starts with Group, Live Venues, Hotels and Restaurants, or Ticketing and Streaming. Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (interviews, scans, document reviews, tests) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the gap analyses (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**Entities and scale.** The holding company is an SEC registrant with three divisions and corporate shared services in the parent; no division holds a gaming license, bank charter, or carrier registration, and the group has no federal contracts (EV-001). The workforce is 45,000: Live Venues about 14,000, Hotels and Restaurants about 19,000, Ticketing and Streaming about 4,500, and corporate about 7,500, plus about 22,000 contractor event-day workers who are not employees (EV-002). FY2025 revenue was about $18.0 billion: Live Venues $9.4 billion, Hotels and Restaurants $6.1 billion, Ticketing and Streaming $2.5 billion (EV-003). Live Venues processed about 61 million card transactions (about 33 million Visa), Hotels and Restaurants about 24 million (about 5.4 million Visa), and streaming about 4.6 million (EV-004). The 2026-04-15 acquirer letters require a QSA ROC for Live Venues by 2026-11-30 and SAQ D for the hotels by 2026-12-31; the streaming acquirer accepts the service provider ROC (EV-005).

**Group identity and access.** All workforce users have unique identities with MFA; administrators and key custodians use hardware keys. Every division system federates to SYS-G1 except the hotel PMS and the 8 acquired theaters (EV-006). Seasonal box office accounts are an exception to automatic disabling of inactive accounts, and about 3,200 of them are created in batches before each season (EV-007). Privileged roles are granted just in time with session recording (EV-008), and the Q1 2026 certification was completed (EV-009). Endpoints, email filtering, device management, and the zero-trust service are configured for all managed devices (EV-010).

**Group security operations.** The SOC runs 24x7. The hotel PMS and the acquired theaters are not in the SIEM source list, and no alert rule covers tenant configuration changes, including checkout tags (EV-011). Incidents are tracked with playbooks, a forensic retainer, and a PFI on the insurer panel (EV-012). EDR covers managed systems but not the acquired theaters' POS servers or office PCs (EV-013). Scans run weekly and ASV scans quarterly; theater addresses are outside ASV scope, theater systems show no patches since the acquisition, and door lock servers at 5 hotels run an unsupported operating system (EV-014). The 2026 penetration tests excluded the theaters; a CDE segmentation test was run in January 2026 (EV-015).

**Policies and common controls.** The 2025 group policies are aligned to CSF 2.0, with a 2025 incident response plan and group standards; the library has no AI standard (EV-016). The common control catalog lists 127 controls; inheritance is recorded for Live Venues (without the acquired theaters) and for Ticketing and Streaming, and no record exists for Hotels and Restaurants (EV-017).

**Group cloud platform and network.** Provider A hosts the hub, the TVOP in two regions with a separate CDE account and managed HSMs, SYS-G4, and hotel workloads; provider B hosts TVOP disaster recovery, streaming, and the immutable backup vault (EV-018). Guardrails enforce secure settings (EV-019). Zones deny by default; hotel front desk terminals share staff networks with back office PCs and door lock servers; the theaters are not on the SD-WAN (EV-020). Token vault keys sit in HSMs with dual control and split knowledge (EV-021). Backups are immutable with quarterly restore tests (EV-022); data stores are encrypted and endpoints accept TLS 1.2 or higher (EV-023); logs are kept 1 year hot and 3 years in archive (EV-024).

**Incident notification, disclosure, risk, and audit.** Exercises to date are technical; none has covered a ticketing platform breach with client, card brand, state, and SEC notices (EV-025). The draft notification matrix has not been exercised and does not list client notice terms (EV-026). The FY2025 Form 10-K includes the Item 106 description, written before the 2026 assessments (EV-027). The board risk committee receives quarterly cyber reports and sets risk acceptance levels (EV-028). The 2025 risk registers and targeted risk analyses are on file (EV-029), and internal audit reports to the board audit committee (EV-030).

**Workforce, vendors, AI, data, and privacy.** Annual training and quarterly phishing exercises cover all workforce; client support has no module on client notice terms; contractor event workers get a short briefing (EV-031). Background checks cover CDE, box office, and cash room roles (EV-032). The TVOP provider list does not name the tag management vendor, 3 other script vendors, the hotel channel manager, or the seller's ticketing vendor (EV-033), and no AOC is on file for the channel manager (EV-034). AI in use includes the pricing and bot modules, hotel revenue management, propensity models, recommendations, fraud scoring, a support assistant, and an enterprise pilot with 3,000 users, with no group AI approval record (EV-035). The retention schedule has no CCTV standard (EV-036). Nightly feeds to SYS-G4 include hotel identity document numbers for about 1.9 million guests and all tenants' purchaser data for model training (EV-037); the catalog has no registered use for the identity numbers and tags client data by tenant only (EV-038). The venue privacy notice is dated 2023 and does not mention advertising tags or bot detection signals (EV-039). The group processes personal information of more than 250,000 California consumers and has no cybersecurity audit plan yet (EV-040). A testing contractor in the United States and India uses synthetic data under a contract that bars access from countries of concern (EV-041). Several venues, hotels, and the headquarters are in Florida (EV-042).

**Live Venues.** 38 venues in 9 states, 30 integrated with the TVOP; about 8,600 events and 26 million attendees; dynamic pricing at 27 venues and a posted limit of 8 tickets (EV-043). The 2025 ROC was Compliant for its scoped channels; its scope document is dated 2025-06, before the theater acquisition (EV-044). About 5,900 P2PE devices are inspected before each event day and reconciled quarterly (EV-045). Venue networks are segmented; 11 venues have a single CCTV recorder (EV-046). Integrators hold persistent tunnels at 6 venues (EV-047). Offline scanner mode was tested at 4 of 30 venues in 12 months, with one 2025 failure (EV-048). 23 marketing tags load on the group venue checkout with no approval recorded (EV-049). The 8 acquired theaters run the seller's ticketing SaaS and about 410 non-P2PE terminals on flat networks, outside SYS-G1, the SIEM, EDR, and the SD-WAN, with migration due 2027-03-31 (EV-050). The legacy POS vendor provided no key management documentation (EV-051). Box office areas are on badge access; settlement sheets are emailed for approval (EV-052).

**Hotels and Restaurants.** 16 owned hotels (about 7,800 rooms), 6 managed hotels on the same PMS, 58 restaurants, and about 180,000 event-and-stay packages a year (EV-053). The PMS has about 2,600 local accounts outside SYS-G1, 340 of them stale, 410 with full card display, and generic front desk logins at 4 hotels (EV-054). PMS logs are kept 90 days by the vendor; the nightly export to SYS-G4 includes about 1.9 million identity document numbers (EV-055). About 180 front desk terminals are not P2PE; about 1,400 restaurant devices are (EV-056). 140 call center agents key card numbers, and group sales accepts authorization forms by email (EV-057). The hotels standards (v2024, aligned 2024-04) allow local and shared PMS logins, emailed forms, and vendor-default log retention (EV-058). Three channels receive rates without the $32 resort fee (EV-059). Paper background reports sit at 4 hotel HR offices with no shredding contract (EV-060).

**Ticketing and Streaming.** The service provider AOC is dated 2026-03-31 (EV-061), and the prior SOC 2 Type 2 report covers the 12 months ending 2025-06-30 (EV-062). About 1,150 clients; 186 negotiated 24-hour incident notice; 212 remain on pre-2024 agreements (EV-063). About 96 million tickets, 68 million patron accounts, 41 million tokens, and 3.1 million streaming subscribers (EV-064). The script inventory lists 14 platform scripts; the content security policy allows the tag manager domain; the number of client tenants with checkout tags is not recorded (EV-065). Tag changes are self-service (EV-066). 38% of client administrators do not use MFA (EV-067). 412 support engineers hold a standing role into any client tenant (EV-068). 37 integration keys are older than 1 year (EV-069). The pipeline has signed builds and security tests (EV-070). Failover to provider B on 2026-02-11 met its RTO (EV-071). Accessible price levels are in auto-apply at the 27 venues, and 310 clients opted into dynamic pricing (EV-072). There is no process for clients to request testing, and the responsibility matrix does not mention tenant tags (EV-073).
