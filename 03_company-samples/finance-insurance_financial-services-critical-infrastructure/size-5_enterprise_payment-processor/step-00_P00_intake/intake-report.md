## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the company's own records before the enterprise BIA, risk analysis and gap analysis began on 2026-06-01, and before Internal Audit's control assessment fieldwork began on 2026-07-13. It covers the organization and its payouts subsidiary, the systems that store, process or transmit cardholder data, merchant data or funding instructions or that support tier-1 processes (Cloud A, Cloud B, DC-1, DC-2 and SaaS), the suppliers that touch them, and the rules that may bind the company, the subsidiary and the two service lines offered to customers (SL-1 and SL-2). Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (BIA interviews, risk workshops, gap analysis samples, Internal Audit tests) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

At this size the sources are enterprise systems of record across the business: the identity platform, PAM and identity governance, the HR system, the CMDB, the cloud consoles and posture management, the SIEM and case management, the change system, the third-party risk register and accounts payable vendor master, the contract repository, PCI program office files, board and committee records, prior Internal Audit and SOX workpapers, and regulatory filings.

Each register row names the owning system of record and owner. The [asset inventory](asset-inventory.csv) reconciles the CMDB with the cloud account inventory, the EDR console and the connectivity register; the [vendor register](vendor-register.csv) reconciles the third-party risk register with the accounts payable vendor master and the contract repository.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the gap analysis (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**Identity and access.** About 12,000 employee and 1,900 contractor accounts are federated through SSO with MFA registered, and FIDO2 keys are enrolled for all Cloud A and data center administrators; about 2,300 accounts hold CPPP roles and 618 administrators hold CDE administrative access (EV-001). 410 Cloud B engineers and operators sign in with push-based MFA, and PAM brokers 62% of Cloud B administrative roles (EV-002). The CPPP has 212 named privileged accounts with just-in-time elevation and session recording in Cloud A and the data centers; the batch scheduler service accounts are not in the vault inventory, and the mainframe vendor's remote support uses an always-on VPN account outside PAM (EV-003). Joiner, mover and leaver events flow from HR through identity governance; contractor departures reach the service desk by email from vendor managers (EV-004). HR lists 12,000 employees and about 1,900 contractors; 5 of the 18 mainframe and settlement batch engineers are eligible to retire by 2028 (EV-005). Since the terminal emulator was replaced on 2026-02-09, about 340 settlement operators sign in to mainframe sessions with a password only (EV-006). 61 users hold rights to create or release funding files, and full PAN view is limited to 14 roles (EV-007). About 1.1 million merchant portal users sign in with MFA for privileged roles and risk-based analysis for the rest (EV-008).

**Assets, endpoints, network and testing.** The CMDB holds about 41,000 asset records; about 7,400 (about 18%) have no support expiration date or recovery time objective (EV-009). 12 of the 46 midrange batch servers run an operating system out of vendor support since 2025-10; the deviation register lists compensating controls for some of them, and the replacement plan is an unapproved draft (EV-010). EDR runs on Cloud A hosts, midrange servers and endpoints; the mainframe, HSMs and the 4 MFT appliances do not run the agent (EV-011). Critical patches on DC-1 midrange servers took a median of 38 days from release to install (EV-012). The data-flow diagrams were updated for the 2026-05-16 interconnect change but do not show the Bank C settlement flows; 2,140 CDE firewall rules each have a justification (EV-013). The 2026-01 penetration and segmentation tests were completed, and the next segmentation test is scheduled for 2026-06 (EV-014). The DC-1 to Cloud A interconnect was re-architected on 2026-05-16 (EV-015). PRC-01.3 requires post-implementation approval of emergency changes within 2 business days and a weekly review (EV-016).

**Monitoring, data protection and cloud.** The SIEM lists 58 CPPP log sources; mainframe security events arrive once a day in a batch, and the MFT appliance logs carry no file-level transfer events (EV-017). The Cyber Fusion Center runs 24x7 with 120 staff (EV-018). Tamper-detection covers the core hosted payment pages and about 64% of about 2,600 ISV integrations (EV-019). PAN is encrypted with AES-256 under keys in 16 company-owned payment HSMs and the Cloud A payment HSM service, with dual control and split knowledge; key exchange guidance goes to 41 enterprise merchants (EV-020, EV-021). Cloud guardrails are written as code for both clouds (EV-022). The Cloud B pipeline service account holds standing administrator rights with a long-lived secret (EV-023).

**Backup and recovery.** Backups are write-once in separate accounts, with virtual tape replicated to DC-2 (EV-024). Authorization failover met its 15-minute target on 2026-02-21, and Cloud B failover met its 1-hour RTO on 2026-03-14. The settlement DR test on 2026-04-25 restored the platform in DC-2 in 9.5 hours against a 6-hour RTO, with manual scheduler restarts and a 22-minute replication lag; no sponsor bank took part in any test (EV-025). Contingency plan v6 has no procedure for coordinating with the sponsor banks during a missed funding cutoff; the manual file upload fallback was drilled with Bank A in 2025-11 (EV-026).

**Governance, prior assurance and disclosure.** POL-01 to POL-05 are in force with standards, procedures and an exception register that has no entry for password-only operator sign-in or for EDR on the MFT appliances (EV-027). The 2025 annual risk analysis rolled up into 8 enterprise risks, and the board approved the risk appetite in 2026-02 (EV-028, EV-029). The 2025 CISO report to the board has no separate section on the payouts subsidiary (EV-030). Designations and the subsidiary's adoption of the parent's program date from 2024 (EV-031). The subsidiary's 2025 NYDFS certification was filed on 2026-04-14 (EV-032). The 2025 AOC is "Compliant" (2025-12-12) and ASV scans passed from Q3 2025 to Q1 2026 (EV-033). The six-month PCI DSS scope confirmation due 2026-04 has not been completed (EV-034). Internal Audit's 2026 plan assigns testing of the Cloud A guardrails to in-house auditors because the co-source firm helped design them in 2024 (EV-035). SL-2 has a SOC 1 report and no SOC 2 report, which two sponsor banks and the largest enterprise merchants have asked for (EV-036, EV-037). The materiality playbook was last exercised in 2025-06; three of the eight disclosure committee members, including the CFO, joined in 2026; the playbook timeline has no NYDFS 72-hour step (EV-038). The FY2025 Form 10-K includes Item 1C (EV-039).

**Incident response and people.** The 2026 incident response plan covers card brand, bank, FTC and SEC notices; the runbook's 4-hour determination step names Banks A and B, and the procedure for unexpected stored PAN does not mention call recordings (EV-040). No Bank C designated contacts are loaded in the incident tooling (EV-041). The last executive exercise was in 2025-06 and the last technical tabletop in 2026-03 (EV-042). Training, phishing simulations and personnel security records are kept in the learning and HR systems (EV-043, EV-044). Threat intelligence tracks mass exploitation of file transfer products, ransomware against payment operators and payment page skimming (EV-045). The cyber insurance tower is $150 million with a $25 million retention (EV-046).

**Third parties and contracts.** The vendor register lists about 1,400 vendors; 11 of the 96 PCI DSS service providers, including the MFT software vendor, have no current AOC or responsibility matrix on file (EV-047, EV-051). The accounts payable master shows the cloud, data center, security, SaaS and AI vendors (EV-048). Each sponsor agreement names the company's processing services as subject to 12 U.S.C. 1867(c), sets the bank's funding cutoff, and requires notice of suspected account data compromise within 24 hours (EV-049). Merchant and ISV agreements promise 99.99% and 99.95% availability (EV-050).

**Business volume, structure and obligations.** Receipts were about $4.8 billion in FY2025, about $13.2 million per calendar day (EV-052). The company processes about 37.8 million transactions and about $2.1 billion of merchant funding per business day, and Cloud A carries about 70% of authorization volume (EV-053). The legal entity register shows an SEC registrant and a wholly owned payouts subsidiary, with no bank, credit union or SCI entity in the group (EV-054). Counsel's view on each candidate rule is in EV-055; the results are in [`obligations-register.csv`](obligations-register.csv).

**Data, contact centers and AI.** The data catalog lists 1,214 data stores in the data and AI platform, and retention rules are recorded for some analytics tables, not all; PAN discovery runs monthly (EV-056). Contact center quality monitoring lists calls where pause-and-resume was missed and card numbers were spoken on the recording (EV-057). The AI inventory records committee review of six use cases; AI-002 was approved on 2026-04-22 with conditions (independent validation and fairness testing) (EV-058). AI-001 was validated in 2025-11; the AI-002 file holds vendor documentation only (EV-059).

**Facilities and other operating records.** DC-1 uses guards, badges, biometrics and video; DC-1 generators have a 96-hour fuel contract; DC-2 is about 800 miles from DC-1 (EV-060, EV-061). Media and destruction records, engineering platform settings, mail and DLP rules, system documentation, the fraud risk assessment, settlement control totals and reconciliations, service line records, capacity reports and infrastructure settings are in EV-062 to EV-070.
