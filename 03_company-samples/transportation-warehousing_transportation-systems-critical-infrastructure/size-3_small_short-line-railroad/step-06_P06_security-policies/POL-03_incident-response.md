## 1. Purpose
Make sure the company detects, contains, reports, and recovers from security incidents quickly, keeps trains moving safely while it does, and meets every reporting deadline.

## 2. Scope
All Cris Santos Company workforce members (owners, employees, and contractors) at Central Yard, North Yard, South Yard, the tower sites, and on trains. Covers all company systems and data, including operational technology (dispatch, radio, wayside, and onboard PTC equipment) and systems that service providers operate for the company. Covers cyber incidents and the physical security events that the Security Coordinators already report to TSA.

## 3. Roles and responsibilities
| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| IT Manager | Incident commander for cyber incidents; coordinates the MSP and the forensic firm |
| Manager of Safety and Security (primary Security Coordinator) | Decides and makes TSA reports; FRA part 225 reports; SSI disclosure reports |
| Chief Dispatcher (alternate Security Coordinator) | Receives incident reports 24/7; starts manual dispatch; makes the TSA report when the primary is unavailable |
| Vice President of Operations | Decides to stop or restrict train movements; runs manual dispatch |
| President and General Manager | Engages counsel and the cyber insurer; approves external communications |
| All workforce | Report suspected incidents to the dispatch center at once |

## 4. Policy statements
4.1 The company must keep an incident response plan and runbooks for its most likely incidents, starting with ransomware on dispatch and PTC systems (P08). (IR-8; RS.MA-01)
4.2 Workforce members must report any suspected incident **immediately**, and within 1 hour at most, to the 24-hour dispatch center. Examples: phishing clicks, strange CAD or radio behavior, lost tablets, an unknown device in a tower shelter. Good-faith reporting is never disciplined. (IR-6; RS.MA-02)
4.3 Every incident must be logged, categorized, and tracked to closure in the ticketing system under the Security category. (IR-5)
4.4 **TSA report.** A cyber attack, meaning "compromising, or attempting to compromise or disrupt the information/technology infrastructure" of the company (Appendix A to 49 CFR part 1570), is a significant security concern. The Security Coordinator must report it to TSA by the method TSA prescribes (telephone to the TSOC) **within 24 hours of initial discovery**, with the information listed in 1570.203(c) as available. When in doubt, report. (IR-6; RS.CO-02; 1570.203)
4.5 If SSI may have been released to unauthorized persons, the Manager of Safety and Security must promptly inform TSA (49 CFR 1520.9(c)). (IR-6)
4.6 If a security incident causes or contributes to an accident/incident reportable under 49 CFR part 225, the Manager of Safety and Security makes the FRA reports on the part 225 timelines, including immediate telephone reports to the National Response Center for events listed in 225.9. (IR-6)
4.7 Personal information breaches (for example, employee records) are assessed with counsel, and notices meet the deadlines in the P08 notification matrix, including Fla. Stat. 501.171. (IR-6; RS.CO-03)
4.8 No ransom may be paid without approval from the majority owner, legal counsel, and the cyber insurer, and an OFAC sanctions check. (IR-4)
4.9 **Safety first.** When any system that supports movement authority is suspect, dispatchers switch to manual dispatch and the Vice President of Operations decides which trains may run. Recovery must not reconnect a system to the dispatch zone until the IT Manager confirms it is clean. (IR-4; RC.RP-01)
4.10 The incident response plan must be tested at least annually by a tabletop exercise that includes a manual dispatch step, and after any major incident. (IR-3; ID.IM-02)
4.11 Lessons learned must be documented within 30 days of closing an incident and added to the risk register. (IR-4; ID.IM-03)

## 5. Compliance and enforcement
Violations are handled under the discipline procedure in POL-01 section 4.8. Consequences range from retraining to termination, depending on intent and harm; for employees covered by a collective bargaining agreement, the agreement's procedures apply. Compliance is checked through the annual control assessment (P07).

## 6. Exceptions
Exceptions follow POL-01 section 4.6. They must be written, risk-rated, approved by the policy owner (or by the majority owner for High risk), and expire within 12 months.
