## 1. Purpose
Set up the Cris Santos Company information security program, assign who is accountable, and give every other security policy its authority. The program protects the safety of train operations and the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of company information and operational technology.

## 2. Scope
All Cris Santos Company workforce members (owners, employees, and contractors) at Central Yard, North Yard, South Yard, the tower sites, and on trains. Covers all company systems and data, including operational technology (dispatch, radio, wayside, and onboard PTC equipment) and systems that service providers operate for the company.

## 4. Policy statements
4.1 The President and General Manager must designate in writing a Cybersecurity Lead who reports to them on security at least quarterly. The IT Manager holds this role. (PM-2; GV.RR-02)
4.2 The company must keep a written security program made up of this policy set, the SSP (P02), and the risk register (P01). Policies are reviewed every year. (PM-1; PL-1; GV.PO-01; GV.PO-02)
4.3 A risk assessment using NIST SP 800-30 must be done every year and after major changes, and its results reported to the President and General Manager. The cyber assessment must be shared with the Manager of Safety and Security for the annual hazmat security plan review (49 CFR 172.802). (RA-3; PM-9; ID.RA-05)
4.4 Security controls must be assessed at least annually by someone who does not operate them. Weaknesses are tracked in the POA&M to closure. (CA-2; ID.IM-02)
4.5 The company uses NIST CSF 2.0, with NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 for operational technology, as its security benchmark. The Manager of Safety and Security must check the TSA and FRA rules that apply each year, when TSA directives are renewed, and when operations change (49 CFR 1570.105(b)). (GV.OC-03)
4.6 **Exceptions.** Any exception to a security policy must be written, risk-rated with the P01 method, approved by the policy owner (or by the majority owner for High risk), and expire within 12 months.
4.7 Contracts with any vendor that connects to company systems or holds company data must include security terms: incident notice within 24 hours, named accounts with MFA for remote access, right to review assurance reports, and data return and deletion. Existing contracts are updated at renewal. (SA-9; GV.SC-05)
4.8 **Discipline.** Violations of security policies are handled through the company's discipline procedure, in proportion to intent and harm. Good-faith reporting of mistakes is never disciplined. (PS-8; GV.RR-04)
4.9 Background checks are done at hire for all positions. (PS-3)
4.10 Security documentation (policies, risk assessments, assessments, incident records, and TSA reports) must be kept for at least 5 years, or longer where a regulation or legal hold requires it. (SI-12; GV.PO-02)
4.11 Sensitive Security Information must be handled under POL-04 and 49 CFR 1520.9. (GV.OC-03)

## 5. Compliance and enforcement
Violations are handled under the discipline procedure in POL-01 section 4.8. Consequences range from retraining to termination, depending on intent and harm; for employees covered by a collective bargaining agreement, the agreement's procedures apply. Compliance is checked through the annual control assessment (P07).

## 6. Exceptions
Exceptions follow POL-01 section 4.6. They must be written, risk-rated, approved by the policy owner (or by the majority owner for High risk), and expire within 12 months.
