## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the company's own records before any assessment work began on 2026-07-06. It covers the organization, the systems that hold or can affect customer data in both partitions of the Hosting Control Plane and Customer Portal (HCP), the systems around it (RMM tool, backup platform, corporate IT), the suppliers that touch them, and the rules that may bind the company. At this size the sources are systems of record: the workforce identity provider, the HR system, PAM and the hypervisor managers, the cloud organization console, the CMDB, the SIEM and scanners, the code hosting and pipeline settings, the contract and vendor registers, the accounts payable vendor master, the legacy FedRAMP repository, the 2026-03 FedRAMP assessment, the CPA firm's SOC 2 exception summary and the incident case records. Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (interviews, samples, tests) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the gap analysis (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**Users and access.** The identity provider holds active named accounts for the 600 employees and about 40 contractors, each with a hardware security key, and 118 accounts were created in the 12 months to 2026-06-30 (EV-001). Hardware keys are required for single sign-on, the cloud consoles, the code repository and the RMM console (EV-002). HR recorded 74 terminations and 41 internal transfers in the same 12 months (EV-003). The HR feed disables identity provider accounts on the termination date; local accounts on DC-1 clusters A and B sit on a separate platform engineering list (EV-004). Background checks, agreements, position risk designations and a sanctions log are on file (EV-005). Government partition access is reviewed quarterly and commercial access semiannually (EV-006). 214 engineers hold privileged roles; just-in-time elevation covers the government partition and every DC-2 and DC-3 cluster, while commercial cloud roles are standing (EV-007). DC-1 clusters A and B (64 hosts) use local passwords, including a shared local administrator account, and are reached through a legacy VPN and jump host outside PAM (EV-008). The portal has about 19,000 customer user accounts; customer administrator MFA is enforced in the government partition and optional in the commercial partition, where 58% are enrolled (EV-009).

**Platform, devices and network.** The CMDB lists 540 hypervisor hosts in 16 clusters and 28 storage arrays across the three data centers, with 9 DC-1 storage controllers past end of support and no partition or federal data tags (EV-010). There are about 24,000 commercial VMs, about 13,000 of them at DC-1 and about 35% in the paid replication tier, and about 2,100 Government Cloud VMs, all replicated between DC-2 and DC-3 (EV-011). About 9,000 commercial volumes created before 2025-01 are not encrypted at rest (EV-012). A firewall rule lets the NOC network reach the management network of DC-1 clusters A and B; each data center has two carriers, and the registrar account has MFA without a registry lock on the portal domain (EV-013). Government clusters are scanned monthly and commercial clusters and DC-1 management interfaces quarterly, with 50 critical and high findings in the first half of 2026 (EV-014). KEV matching is done by hand, and 14% of DC-1 BMCs run outdated firmware (EV-015). DC-1 hosts are checked for drift monthly, and BMC remote logging is off on clusters A and B (EV-016). All 720 endpoints have full-disk encryption and EDR (EV-017).

**Cloud, pipeline, backups and logging.** The landing zone has 10 accounts with organization guardrails and a write-once log archive (EV-018). The commercial control plane holds long-lived service credentials that act on all 13 commercial clusters; the government deployment uses scoped short-lived credentials (EV-019). The government template signing key is in the HSM and the commercial key is a pipeline secret (EV-020). The pipeline verifies template signatures at deployment in the government partition only, and there are no SBOM or provenance records (EV-021). Development and design documents are on file (EV-022). Control plane backups are hourly, immutable and in a second region; the 2026-06 government restore met its 4-hour RTO, and the only commercial restore test (2025) took 9 hours (EV-023). The government contingency plan dates from 2024; no commercial or DC-1 contingency plan or failover test record was provided (EV-024). The SIEM holds complete government sources for 1 year online; DC-1 clusters A and B and the RMM tool are not connected, and commercial logs stay online 90 days (EV-025). AI triage, enabled in March 2026, auto-closes commercial-partition alerts with no sampling (EV-026). The RMM tool reaches about 11,200 servers of 410 customers, has 2 shared super-administrator accounts, runs scripts across customers with no second approval, and keeps its logs with the vendor for 90 days (EV-027).

**Programs and FedRAMP records.** The five security policies and most standards date from 2024 and were written for the government partition (EV-028). The legacy FedRAMP package is document-based, with no machine-readable package or trust center, and the 2024 boundary treats the pipeline, identity provider, SIEM and ITSM tenants as external services (EV-029). Continuous monitoring follows the legacy monthly process (EV-030). The March 2026 annual assessment covered the legacy core controls (EV-031). Penetration tests ran in 2026-02 and 2026-03 (EV-032). The FedRAMP Security Inbox, set up 2026-01-05, is read only by the Director of Security, and the April 2026 Emergency Test action was completed after the timeframe in the message (EV-033). The Marketplace listing does not name the services, and the status page has no history or machine-readable feed (EV-034). The 2024 incident response plan has a bank notice step and no reportability evaluation, PAIN rating or DFARS path; the spillage procedure has never been exercised; the last tabletop was in 2026-02 (EV-035). There were 27 security incidents in 2025-2026 (EV-036). Awareness training completion is 98% (EV-037).

**Contracts, customers and suppliers.** The MSA sets 99.95% availability with service credits, a 72-hour incident notice and prohibits PHI and, in the commercial partition, CUI (EV-038). The bank addendum covers 38 banks; designated contacts were last verified for 31 of them in 2025-11 (EV-039). Defense customer contracts flow down DFARS 252.204-7012(b)(2)(ii)(D) and (c) to (g), and the company holds no DoD-approved medium assurance certificate (EV-040). The GSA Schedule contract carries the FAR safeguarding and reporting clauses (EV-041). Four Tier 1 contracts (RMM, ITSM, backup software, hardware maintenance) have no incident notice terms (EV-042). Of about 260 vendors, 34 have access to customer data or production systems and 14 are Tier 1; review files are current for 9 of the 14 (EV-043, EV-044). The RMM vendor's SOC 2 report lists 2 exceptions on administrator access reviews, and reports for 5 Tier 1 vendors were not on file (EV-045). The CPA firm's summary for the 2025-07-01 to 2026-06-30 SOC 2 period lists 3 exceptions (EV-046); the issued report followed on 2026-08-12 (EV-060). The cyber insurance policy has a $15 million limit, a $500,000 retention and a carrier hotline condition (EV-047). The audit committee receives quarterly cyber risk and FedRAMP reports (EV-048).

**Facilities and media.** Cage access lists, visitor and escort logs, drive logs and destruction certificates are kept; DC-1 destruction certificates are issued in batches (EV-049). The walk-throughs at DC-1 and DC-2 found provider-controlled facility entry, company-locked cages, an override key lock on the DC-1 cage, and no wireless access points or output devices in the cages (EV-050). Vendor maintenance runs through PAM at DC-2 and DC-3; the DC-1 storage vendor uses its own remote tool with standing access (EV-051). Production changes go through the change advisory board (EV-052). Hardware comes from authorized distributors with no inspection on receipt (EV-053).

**Operations, business volume and AI tools.** Legal requests, deletions, restores and backup job monitoring are logged (EV-054). Commercial customer account recovery is verified by knowledge questions, and snapshots are shared by hand in support workflows (EV-055). Email filtering and an advisory log are in place (EV-056). FY2025 revenue was about $100 million, about $274,000 a day (EV-057). There are about 2,300 business customers, including 7 agencies, 19 defense contractors and 38 banks (EV-058). Five AI tools are in use or in pilot, and no review record was found for any of them (EV-059).

**Reconciliation across sources.**
- **Workforce.** The 600 employees on the HR roster and about 40 contractors each have an identity provider account (EV-001, EV-003).
- **Hosts and VMs.** The CMDB host count (540 in 16 clusters) matches the SSP boundary and the P07 configuration scan population; the VM counts by data center add up to about 24,000 commercial VMs (EV-010, EV-011).
- **Privileged access.** The 214 privileged engineers in the PAM and role exports match the P07 rights review population (EV-007).
- **Vendors.** About 260 vendors in accounts payable, 34 with access to customer data or production systems, 14 of them Tier 1 (EV-043, EV-044). The 14 named Tier 1 vendors and a summary row in [`vendor-register.csv`](vendor-register.csv) account for the 34.
- **AI tools.** Two AI purchases in accounts payable (the coding assistant and the applicant tracking add-on), two features turned on in existing SaaS (SIEM triage and the ITSM reply assistant) and one company-built model give the 5 tools in [`asset-inventory.csv`](asset-inventory.csv) (EV-044, EV-059).
