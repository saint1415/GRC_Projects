## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the business's own records before the self-assessment began on 2026-07-27. A one-person holding business has no HR system, asset database or internal audit, so the systems of record are the SaaS vendors' admin consoles and reports, online banking and its statements, the inbox, the signed agreements folder, the device settings screens, the owner's walk-through of the two sites, and the insurance agent's portal. Intake covers the four entities (the sole proprietorship and its three LLCs), the systems that hold their personal and financial information, the vendors and contractors that touch them, and the rules that may bind each entity. Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (self-reviews, walk-throughs, tests) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the gap analysis (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**Accounts, roles and sign-in.** The suite tenant has four email domains and three mailboxes: the owner's, the Storage manager's, and a shared Laundry mailbox. The owner's account is the only administrator and uses text-message MFA, with no recovery codes generated. The other two mailboxes have no MFA, and the shared Laundry mailbox password was last changed before 2026-03-27 (EV-001). The owner holds an administrator account in every system, and each lists the owner's business email as its reset and notice address (EV-003). The accounting and payroll vendors enforce authenticator-app MFA. The bookkeeper's accountant user reaches all four company files, including payee and bank-detail settings (EV-004, EV-006). The owner is the only online banking user. Wires and new payees need a one-time text-message code, and every outgoing payment triggers an email alert (EV-005). Payroll records give a storage assistant's last day as 2026-01-16 and a Laundry attendant's as 2026-03-27 (EV-006). The storage system has three active accounts, including a storage assistant account, and MFA is off for all of them (EV-007). The property management platform has one user, the owner, with text-message MFA (EV-008). The laundry platform administrator signs in with a password only, and both attendants share one 4-digit PIN that has not changed since 2026-03-27 (EV-009).

**Email, files and the AI assistant.** Deleted items are kept 30 days, sign-in history 30 days, and no backup or export is connected (EV-002). The AI assistant add-on was turned on 2026-06-01 for the owner's account only and can open anything that account can open. Its history shows drafting and summarizing work and, on 2026-07-14, a prompt that summarized six unit 4B applications with their screening reports and asked which applicant to choose (EV-027).

**Devices and sites.** The laptop has full-disk encryption, built-in antivirus, automatic updates and a screen lock. Business passwords are saved in the browser and no password manager is installed. The phone is encrypted and receives the text-message codes (EV-010). At the Storage office the desktop signs in to a shared local account with no password, keeps the manager's mailbox open, and has disk encryption off. It is 7 years old. Paper move-in forms are cross-cut shredded. At the laundromat the router's device list shows customer Wi-Fi devices, the POS tablet and the payment hub on one network (EV-011). The installer configured the gate controller, and no later administrator password change or firmware update is recorded (EV-012). One laptop was traded in during 2025 with no wipe record (EV-013).

**Documents and agreements.** None of these was found for any entity: a risk assessment, an inventory, a written security policy, an incident plan, a contact list, a vendor list, a retention or disposal rule, a payment-change procedure, a training record, or successor access instructions (EV-014). The management agreements make the sole proprietorship the back office for each LLC. Each operating agreement names a successor manager and gives no system access instructions (EV-015). The bookkeeper's engagement letter has no confidentiality or security terms. The IT technician signed a confidentiality and security agreement on 2026-07-24 (EV-016). Cards are taken only through the processors' hosted fields and vendor-run readers (EV-017).

**Vendors, assurance and insurance.** Statements show recurring payments to six SaaS vendors, the internet providers and the mobile carrier, and payments to four contractors. They show no payment for a password manager, a backup service or cyber insurance (EV-018). Vendor terms were accepted online as offered (EV-019). The accounting service's SOC 2 Type 2 report covers the 12 months ending 2026-03-31, states RTO 12 hours and RPO 1 hour, and lists one deprovisioning exception at the vendor (EV-020). Each LLC carries general liability coverage only (EV-021).

**Business volume.** Management fees were about $180,000 in FY2025, and the LLCs' combined revenue was about $750,000 (EV-022, EV-023). Storage collects about $850 of rent a day (EV-024), Laundry takes about $660 a day, about 60% by card or app (EV-025), and Rentals collects about $16,700 a month (EV-008).

**Rentals screening.** Screening reports are ordered only after an applicant applies and consents, and are delivered inside the platform. Each report shows a recommendation whose basis the platform does not describe. For unit 4B, two applicants were declined on 2026-07-16 and sent the platform's adverse action notice that day (EV-026).
