## 1. GOVERN
- **Who decides what.** The **county** owns the building and the readers and decides whether to use face recognition on its employees. The **company** holds the tenant where the templates live, configures and operates the feature, and can refuse to run a use it considers unsafe or unlawful. This assessment is the company's decision on whether, and how, it will keep operating AI-001.
- **Accountable owner:** Security Systems Technician. **Decision authority:** the owner (High tier).
- **Policies that apply:**
  - POL-04 4.7: no AI or biometric feature in a customer system without an approved P10 assessment and the customer's written request; face templates deleted within 30 days after a person withdraws or leaves. **AI-001 started before this rule and before any assessment**, which is the main governance gap (company facts, missing item 13).
  - POL-04 4.1: face templates, badge photos, and access history are Restricted data.
  - POL-02 A.5: vendors must agree to security terms and 24-hour incident notice before handling customer data.
- **Approved-tools list:** kept by the Office and Compliance Manager in POL-04 4.7. For customer systems it lists only AI-001, only at the county administration building employee entrance, only for enrolled volunteers, and AI-002 (alarm prioritization, with fixed critical classes).
- **Scale for a Micro company:** there is no AI committee. The owner, the Office and Compliance Manager, and the Security Systems Technician review AI use at the monthly security meeting; the county facilities director joins for AI-001 each quarter.

**How the pilot started.** On 2026-06-12 the county facilities director asked by email for face verification at the employee entrance "to stop badge sharing". The Security Systems Technician turned on the vendor's add-on on 2026-06-15, installed 2 face-capable readers, and enrolled 52 county employees who volunteered after a county HR email. Nobody signed a consent form, and the vendor's default template retention (indefinite) was left on.

## 3. Risk tier
**Tier: High** (repository rubric, `00_universal-framework/projects/step-10_P10_ai-governance/README.md`).
- **Why High, even as a second factor:** it controls physical access to a government facility (physical security of critical infrastructure), it processes biometric data, and repeated false rejections affect employees' access to their own workplace. The rubric's High tier covers AI that "can affect physical safety or critical infrastructure operations."
- **Why not Medium:** a human does not review each match; the reader accepts or rejects on its own, and the badge-and-PIN fallback only limits the harm.

**Minimum controls for High:** human review before action; pre-deployment bias testing; impact assessment (this document); notice to affected people; ongoing monitoring.

## 4. MEASURE
### 4.1 Trustworthy characteristics (pilot, 2026-06-15 to 2026-08-14)
| Characteristic | Test or metric | Result | Pass? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Valid and reliable | False non-match rate (FNMR): genuine employees rejected per attempt. Target 2% or less | 4,160 attempts; FNMR 2.9% (121 rejections) | **No** |

| Explainable and interpretable | Guards can see the match score and the enrolled photo when a match fails | Score and enrolled photo visible at the guard desk | Yes |

### 4.2 Bias testing plan
Face matching accuracy can differ by demographic group; NIST's face recognition vendor test report on demographic effects (NISTIR 8280, December 2019) documented such differences across many algorithms. The plan:
- **Metric:** FNMR per group at the operating threshold, from real attempts; FMR per group from the local impostor test.
- **Groups compared:** sex; age band (under 40, 40-59, 60 and over); self-reported race and ethnicity (voluntary, collected by county HR and reported to the company only in aggregate, and only for groups of 15 or more); people wearing eyeglasses or head coverings.
- **Thresholds:** each group's FNMR at or below 1.5 times the overall FNMR **and** at or below 3%; zero false accepts in each group's impostor test. Groups with fewer than 15 people are reported but not scored.
- **Frequency:** before any expansion, then quarterly while in use, and after any vendor model update.

**Pilot results (overall FNMR 2.9%):**
| Group | People | FNMR | Ratio to overall | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Men | 30 | 2.4% | 0.8 | Pass |
| Women | 22 | 3.6% | 1.2 | **Flagged** (above 3%) |
| Under 40 | 17 | 2.1% | 0.7 | Pass |
| 40-59 | 26 | 2.8% | 1.0 | Pass |
| 60 and over | 9 | 5.0% | 1.7 | Reported, not scored (fewer than 15) |
| Race and ethnicity | 38 of 52 answered | n/a | n/a | No group reached 15 people; county HR released no breakdown |

**Bias finding.** With 52 people the pilot cannot show whether the system treats racial and ethnic groups equally, and the oldest group is too small to score, although its rate is the highest. Women are rejected more often than the 3% limit. Because the badge-and-PIN fallback always works, the harm is delay and repeated friction, not denied entry. It still falls unevenly and is not acceptable for an expansion. A micro-sized pilot must rely on the vendor for most of the fairness evidence: its model's demographic performance data, and whether the algorithm has been evaluated by NIST. Lowering the match threshold to cut rejections would raise the false match rate, so it may not be changed without a new impostor test.

## 5. MANAGE
**Human-in-the-loop design:**
- The face match is never the only factor; the badge is always required.
- A failed match falls back to badge plus PIN, or to the guard desk with a visual check of the enrolled photo. No one is refused entry by the algorithm alone.
- Face match results are never used as evidence for discipline or given to law enforcement without the county's human review of the event and the video.

**Consent and notice:**
- Written notice and a signed consent form before enrollment, stating purpose, retention, and how to withdraw. Templates are deleted for anyone who does not sign by the deadline.
- Signage at the entrance.
- Declining or withdrawing has no effect on employment or access; the badge-and-PIN lane stays open.

**Data handling:**
- Templates are Restricted data (POL-04). Retention set to delete within 30 days after withdrawal or departure, checked monthly against the county's departure list. The 2 templates of employees who left in July are deleted.
- Vendor letter or amendment: no training on templates, encryption at rest, per-person deletion, the module in the next SOC 2 report, and 24-hour incident notice.
- Template export is limited to the 2 SYS-01 administrators (R-002 treatment) and logged; exports are reviewed weekly.

**Monitoring:**
- Monthly FNMR report and quarterly bias report to the county facilities director, tracked in the risk register (R-012).
- Complaints go to county HR and the Security Systems Technician.

**Incident handling:** a template breach follows P08: 24-hour notice to the county under the contract, and third-party agent notice within 10 days of the breach determination (Fla. Stat. 501.171(6)(a)).

**Decommissioning:** stop the pilot and delete all templates (with a deletion confirmation from the vendor) if the vendor terms are not in place by 2026-10-31, if the women's FNMR is still above 3% at the next quarterly report, or if the county attorney concludes the templates would be disclosable public records.

## 6. Decision
**AI-001: approve with conditions.** Owner, 2026-08-31. The pilot may continue for the currently enrolled volunteers, with **no new enrollments**, only if these are met by 2026-10-31:
1. Vendor letter or amendment covering no training on templates, encryption, per-person deletion, the module's SOC 2 coverage, and 24-hour incident notice.
2. Signed consent forms and signage in place; templates deleted for anyone who does not sign.
3. Template retention set to 30 days after withdrawal or departure; the 2 departed employees' templates deleted.
4. Written opinion from the county attorney on the public records status of the templates, and counsel's view on whether they are biometric data under 501.171 and 501.702.
5. Local impostor test (500 attempts) completed and the vendor's demographic performance data received.

Expansion to other entrances or buildings requires a new assessment, a group large enough to score the main demographic groups (or the vendor's evidence in its place), and two consecutive quarterly reports with no flagged group.

**AI-002 (alarm prioritization in SYS-02): Medium tier, approved as configured.** The vendor's model only reorders non-critical alarms; critical alarm classes are fixed by the technicians and always notify the on-call phone. The Lead Controls Technician reviews downgraded alarms monthly (P01 R-022, accepted).

**AI-003 (public generative AI chatbots): prohibited for Restricted data** (POL-02 C.2; POL-04 4.7). Low tier only for text with no customer data.
