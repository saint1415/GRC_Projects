At the Micro tier the company keeps three core policies: access control (POL-02), incident response (POL-03), and data classification (POL-04). A separate Information Security Policy would add little for a 7-person company, so its essential rules live in **POL-02 Part A. Program governance**:

| Essential rule | Where it lives | Benchmark or obligation |
|---|---|---|
| Designation of the security and privacy lead (Property Manager) | POL-02 A.1 | CPG 1.A; PCI DSS 12.1.3 |
| Annual risk assessment and risk register | POL-02 A.2 | CPG 1.B |
| Who may accept risk | POL-02 A.3 | CPG 1.B |
| Sanctions | POL-02 A.4 | CPG 1.B |
| No security terms, no access (vendor security addendum, 24-hour incident notice) | POL-02 A.5 | CPG 1.D, 1.E; Fla. Stat. 501.171(6)(a) |
| Annual independent assessment | POL-02 A.6 | CPG 2.C |
| Retention of security records | POL-02 A.7 | CPG 1.B |
| Annual policy review and availability to staff | POL-02 A.8 | PCI DSS 12.1.1, 12.1.2 |
| Approval of new devices, software, and platform features (including AI) | POL-02 A.9 | CPG 3.P |
| Exceptions process (patching and antivirus exclusions included) | POL-02 A.10 | CPG 1.B |
| Baseline protections: monthly patching, malware protection, supported firmware | POL-02 A.11 | CPG 2.B, 4.A |

Traceability for these rules is in `policy-control-map.csv` under POL-02. PCI DSS 12.1.1 asks for an overall information security policy; POL-02 Part A, together with POL-03 and POL-04, is that policy set. Revisit this choice if the company grows past the Micro tier (10 or more employees) or adds a property with its own staff.
