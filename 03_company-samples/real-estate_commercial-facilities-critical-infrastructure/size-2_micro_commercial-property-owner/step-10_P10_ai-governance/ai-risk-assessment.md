## 1. GOVERN
- **Accountable owner:** the Property Manager (owner of access control and video). **Decision authority:** the Managing Member, for every AI or analytics feature (POL-02 A.9).
- **Policies that apply:**
  - POL-02 A.9: no new platform feature, including analytics, without written approval and, for AI, a P10 assessment
  - POL-04 4.1 and 4.6: face templates are Restricted data; only approved AI features may be used
  - POL-02 C.2: no company information in public AI chatbots (AI-003)
- **Approved list:** kept by the Property Manager in POL-04 4.6. After this decision it lists only AI-002.
- **Scale for a Micro company:** there is no AI committee. The Managing Member, the Property Manager, and the Building Engineer review AI use at the monthly security meeting.

**How the trial started.** In May 2026 a Property A tenant complained about unfamiliar people in the building at night. The security integrator suggested the platform's face match feature, and the vendor turned it on as a free trial on 2026-06-08 at the integrator's request. Nobody at the company approved it, tenants and credential holders were not told, and the platform created face templates from every stored badge photo (about 190 holders). The Property Manager found the setting during the 2026-07-22 walkthrough and paused it on 2026-07-24 (P01 R-016; scenario-facts gap 15).

## 2. MAP
| Item | Description |
|---|---|
| Purpose and intended use | Detect shared or borrowed fobs at night. At each after-hours credential use at the front entrance (weekdays 19:00 to 7:00 and weekends), the reader camera captures a face image, the platform compares it 1:1 with the template from the credential holder's badge photo, and a mismatch sends the Property Manager an alert with the image and the badge photo. The door opens either way |
| Users / operators | The Property Manager (alerts on a phone) |
| Affected people | About 190 Property A credential holders with badge photos, including tenant employees and 6 janitorial contractor staff, plus anyone who walks in with them |
| Data | Inputs: badge photos uploaded by tenants for identification, and video frames from the entrance reader camera. Created: a face template (a numeric representation of facial geometry) for each badge photo and each capture, stored by the vendor. **The vendor's standard terms allow it to use customer video to improve its models unless the customer opts out; the company had not opted out.** Alert images are kept 1 year by default, longer than the 30-day video retention in POL-04 4.8 |
| Build or buy | Buy: a feature of the access control and video platform. Released in May 2026, after the vendor's SOC 2 report period, so not covered by any assurance (P09) |
| Not intended | Denying entry, locking doors, reporting people to tenants for discipline, or identifying anyone who is not a credential holder. Any of these would require a new assessment |

**Applicable laws and rules:**
| Rule | Applies? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| FTC Act Section 5, 15 U.S.C. 45(a) (C-COMMERCIAL-FACILITIES-R02) | **Yes** | Covers unfair or deceptive practices, including undisclosed collection. The FTC's *Policy Statement on Biometric Information and Section 5 of the FTC Act* (2023-05-18) lists practices it may treat as unfair, including failing to assess foreseeable harms before collecting biometric information, surreptitious or unexpected collection, failing to evaluate vendors' claims, and failing to monitor whether the technology works as expected. | Fla. Stat. 501.171 | **Unsettled; treated as applying** | Personal information includes "biometric data as defined in s. 501.702" (501.171(1)(g)1.a.(VI)). Section 501.702 defines biometric data as data generated by automatic measurements of biological characteristics used to identify a specific individual, but **excludes "physical or digital photographs; video or audio recordings or data generated from video or audio recordings"** (text checked on flsenate.gov 2026-10-06). The capture templates are computed from camera video, and the enrollment templates from photographs, so it is unsettled whether either is biometric data under Florida law. Counsel is to confirm. Until then the company **treats the templates as biometric personal information**: a breach of the vendor's template store would follow the 30-day notice steps in P08, and "reasonable measures" (501.171(2)) apply |

## 3. Risk tier
Tiers use the repository rubric in `00_universal-framework/projects/step-10_P10_ai-governance/README.md`.

**AI-001: Medium.**
- **Why not High:** as configured, the feature makes no decision about a person. The door opens regardless, and the Property Manager reviews every alert before anything happens.
- **Why not Low:** it creates face templates of about 190 people without notice, its alerts can lead to a person being challenged at their workplace, and its errors fall unevenly (section 4).
- **Re-tier to High** if a mismatch could deny entry or lock a door, if alerts are sent to tenants or used for discipline, or if the feature is extended to Property B or to all hours.

## 4. MEASURE
The Property Manager and the consultant reviewed all trial alerts against the recorded video on 2026-07-27 and 2026-07-28.

| Trustworthy characteristic | Test or metric | Result | Pass? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Valid and reliable | Precision: at least 30% of alerts show a real shared or borrowed fob; false-alert rate under 2% of after-hours entries | 1,240 after-hours entries with a face capture; 87 alerts; 3 real (one tenant employee lent a fob to a coworker; one janitorial worker used a coworker's fob twice); 84 false. Precision 3.4%; false-alert rate 6.8% | **No** |
| Safe | Alerts cannot lock doors or deny entry; egress unaffected | Confirmed in platform settings and a walk test on 2026-08-11 | Yes |
| Secure and resilient | Feature covered by vendor assurance; template storage and deletion documented | Outside the vendor's SOC 2 report; no documentation of template storage, encryption, or deletion | **No** |
| Accountable and transparent | Holders and tenants told before templates are made; entrance signs describe the feature | No notice; signs say only "video surveillance in use" | **No** |
| Explainable and interpretable | Reviewer sees the capture next to the badge photo before acting | Shown with every alert | Yes |
| Privacy-enhanced | No vendor model training on company data; alert images kept no longer than video (30 days); templates deleted when a credential is disabled | Training opt-out not exercised; alert images kept 1 year; no template deletion rule | **No** |
| Fair, with harmful bias managed | Compare false-alert rates between groups the company can observe without collecting demographic data; flag a ratio of 2 or more | Janitorial contractor staff: 46 false alerts in 470 entries (9.8%) versus 38 in 770 for everyone else (4.9%), a ratio of 2.0. Badge photos older than 3 years: 31 in 260 entries (11.9%) versus 53 in 980 (5.4%), a ratio of 2.2. Main causes of false alerts: hats or hoods (31), night backlighting at the door (22), old or low-quality badge photos (18), glasses or masks (8), other (5) | **No (both flagged)** |

**Bias finding.** The people who come in at night most often, the janitorial crew, were alerted on about twice as often as everyone else, and an old badge photo doubled the chance of a false alert. A contractor's staff would carry most of the burden of being challenged. The company does not record race, skin tone, age, or sex of credential holders and will not start doing so to test this feature, so it cannot measure demographic differences itself. Independent research shows that face recognition error rates can differ across demographic groups (NIST IR 8280, *Face Recognition Vendor Test Part 3: Demographic Effects*, 2019), and the vendor supplied no evidence for its own model.

**Human in the loop during the trial.** The Property Manager reviewed each alert the next morning; no one was stopped or reported on an alert alone. The 3 real cases were handled with a reminder to the tenant contact and to the janitorial contractor about fob sharing. This worked only because the alerts were ignored at night: 87 alerts in 7 weeks, almost all false, is not a stream anyone could act on in real time.

## 5. MANAGE
**Data protection actions (whatever the decision):**
- The feature stays off. The vendor deletes every face template and alert image from the trial and confirms deletion in writing (requested 2026-08-26; due 2026-09-30).
- Opt out of vendor model training for all company video (done in the platform settings on 2026-08-26; written confirmation requested).
- Alert and clip retention for all analytics set to 30 days.
- If the vendor reports a breach that includes trial templates before deletion is confirmed, follow P08 and treat them as biometric personal information.

**Non-AI alternative for the original problem (shared fobs at night):**
- After-hours entry at the Property A front entrance requires fob plus a 4-digit PIN (the reader already supports it), from 2026-10-15.
- Quarterly credential confirmation and the 90-day inactivity rule (POL-02 B.5) remove fobs that should not exist.
- A one-page reminder to tenant contacts and the janitorial contractor that fobs are personal and sharing them is a lease rule violation.

**Requirements for any future proposal (re-assessment needed):**
- Independent evidence of the model's accuracy, including false non-match rates by demographic group, for the exact algorithm version.
- Opt-in enrollment only, with fob plus PIN for anyone who does not opt in; written notice to tenants and holders; updated signs.
- Vendor terms: no training use, template encryption, deletion within 30 days of a credential being disabled or an opt-out, breach notice within 24 hours, and the feature inside the SOC 2 scope.
- Alert only: no automatic door action; the reviewer must compare the capture with the badge photo and the video before any contact, and the only permitted action is a courteous credential check.
- A measured pilot meeting the section 4 thresholds, with every group ratio below 2.

**Decommissioning (done):** feature paused 2026-07-24; deletion confirmation tracked in P01 R-016 and closed when received.

## 6. Decision
**AI-001: Not approved. Retire the trial.** Managing Member, 2026-08-31.

Reasons:
1. It does not work well enough to be useful: 3 real cases in 87 alerts.
2. Its errors fall about twice as often on the janitorial crew and on holders with old photos, and the vendor gave no demographic accuracy evidence.
3. It created face templates of about 190 people without notice, outside any assurance, under vendor terms that allowed model training. Florida's treatment of these templates is unsettled, and the FTC's biometric policy statement describes exactly this kind of collection as a concern.
4. A non-AI control (fob plus PIN at night) meets the business need at almost no cost.

The vendor and the security integrator have been told in writing that no analytics feature may be turned on without the company's written approval (POL-02 A.9).

## 7. Other inventory entries (brief)
- **AI-002, person detection alerts at Property B after hours: approved with conditions (Medium).** In use since 2024. It detects people, not identities, and creates no face templates. The Property Manager reviews each clip before calling the guard company or police. Conditions: clips kept 30 days; Property B signs updated to say that video analytics detect people after hours; no face features. 41 of the last 60 alerts showed a real person, mostly delivery drivers and tenants' staff working late.
- **AI-003, public generative AI chatbots: prohibited for Restricted and Confidential data** (POL-02 C.2; POL-04 4.6). Staff may use them for generic drafting with no tenant, applicant, or guarantor details.
