## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the company's own records before the enterprise BIA interviews began on 2026-05-18, before the risk and gap analysis began on 2026-06-01, and before Internal Audit's control assessment fieldwork began on 2026-07-13. It covers the organization (three plants, the acquired infusion business at MN-1, two R&D centers, two monitoring centers and two colocation data centers), the systems that create, receive, maintain or transmit PHI or consumer health data or that build and release device software, the fielded device fleet, the suppliers that touch them, and the rules that may bind the company and its two service lines (SL-1 and SL-2). Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (BIA interviews, risk workshops, gap analysis samples, Internal Audit tests) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

At this size the sources are enterprise systems of record across business units: the identity platform and HR system, the CMDB and plant systems, the cloud consoles and SIEM, the Device Software Factory (repositories, build orchestrator, signing portal, artifact repository and SBOM service), PLM and the eQMS, the third-party risk register and accounts payable vendor master, the contract repository, board and committee records, prior Internal Audit and SOX workpapers, and regulatory files.

Each register row names the owning business unit's system of record and owner. The [asset inventory](asset-inventory.csv) reconciles the CMDB with the cloud account inventory, the endpoint and plant records, the Device Software Factory exports and the installed base report; the [vendor register](vendor-register.csv) reconciles the third-party risk register with the accounts payable vendor master and the contract repository.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the gap analysis (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**Organization, volumes and obligations basis.** FY2025 revenue was about $4.8 billion, about $13.2 million per calendar day and $19.2 million per business day, across six segments (EV-001). Shipments average about $19.2 million per business day, about 62% of them same-week orders, and every unit has a serial and UDI record (EV-002). Finished goods cover about two weeks of monitors and about 10 days of pumps and patches, with 8 and 10 weeks of safety stock for the two single-source components (EV-003). HR lists 12,000 employees by function; MN-1 terminations reach MN-1 IT through a daily HR report (EV-004). The company serves about 2,300 hospitals and about 5,200 practices under about 3,400 BAAs (EV-005), invoices its customers and sends no standard transactions to health plans (EV-006). All products are class II; the clearance dates and the cyber device classification of each product are on file (EV-007). The installed base includes about 41,000 VM-500 units and 62,000 IV-300 pumps on the first-generation wireless module (EV-008). The company is an SEC registrant that is not a smaller reporting company, and its FY2025 Form 10-K includes Item 1C (EV-009). The employee health plan is a separate covered entity (EV-010). There are no defense or federal contracts, and export matters sit with a separate trade compliance program (EV-011). The consumer app is offered directly to consumers and draws data from the HB-40, the user and the phone's health platform (EV-012). SL-1 has an annual SOC 2 Type 2 report, the 2025 report with no exceptions; SL-2 has none (EV-013). Counsel's review of the candidate rules is in EV-014; the results are in [`obligations-register.csv`](obligations-register.csv).

**Governance, prior assurance and disclosure.** The 2025 policy set (POL-01 to POL-05, approved 2025-09) sits in the policy portal with standards, procedures, an exception register and attestation records (EV-015). The 2025 enterprise risk analysis rolled into the ERM register, and the board approved the risk appetite in 2026-02 (EV-016, EV-017). Internal Audit's 2025 assessment of the build pipeline and signing service and the FY2025 SOX IT general control results are on file (EV-018). The Director of Security Operations is designated in writing as the HIPAA security official; the PSIRT has 14 staff and the GRC team 10 (EV-019). The disclosure committee has 9 members, 3 of whom joined in 2026, and its minutes record one exercise of the materiality playbook, a ransomware scenario in 2025-11; the playbook does not name who briefs the General Counsel for PSIRT-declared incidents (EV-020). Incident response plan v6 and the 2026-03-19 tabletop, which exercised a signing key compromise, are on file (EV-021). The common control catalog lists 11 providers, and DSF-MES SSP version 1.1 is dated 2026-02-20 (EV-022). Insurance policies and a sector threat summary complete the set (EV-023, EV-024).

**Identity and access.** Workforce accounts sign in through SSO with MFA, except about 900 users of the MN-1 legacy directory (EV-025, EV-029). Push MFA with number matching is still allowed for non-privileged users; privileged users use FIDO2 keys (EV-026). 58 privileged build, HSM and MES accounts are checked out just in time through PAM (EV-027). Identity governance provisions roles from HR events, disables accounts inactive 45 days and closed 4 quarterly certification campaigns; the MN-1 directory is not connected (EV-028). The MN-1 directory holds 14 shared operator logins used by about 400 operators, with disable dates entered by hand (EV-029). About 1,400 engineering users, including about 220 contractors, use about 2,600 repositories; 12 approvers and 6 HSM administrators hold signing roles (EV-030). Of 5 remote access paths into DSF-MES, two MN-1 station vendors use their own remote support tools (EV-031).

**Endpoints, plants and network.** EDR reports on about 97% of about 16,000 endpoints (EV-032). The CMDB lists 64 build nodes, MES clusters at FL-1 and TX-1, a single MES server at MN-1 and about 420 stations; MN-1 records are kept by hand (EV-033). FL-1 and TX-1 OT networks are segmented by zone, while the MN-1 OT network shares one segment with the MN-1 office network (EV-034). Passive OT monitoring runs at FL-1 and TX-1 and not at MN-1 (EV-035). The MN-1 MES server runs an operating system past its vendor's support end date, has no restore record, and MN-1 IV-300 stations compare an image checksum before programming (EV-036). FL-1 and TX-1 stations follow baselines as code, block USB storage and verify image signatures (EV-037). Sites have dual carriers with cellular failover (EV-038). Scanning covers build and MES servers monthly; MN-1 OT assets are outside the authenticated scan scope (EV-039).

**Device Software Factory.** Device repositories use branch protection, code-owner review and signed commits (EV-040). Cloud runners receive a 90-day token with write access to the artifact repository, and signed provenance is produced for cloud software builds and not for firmware builds (EV-041). The HSM signing service holds 27 keys and requires two approvers who did not submit the request; secondary HSM failover was last tested on 2025-06-20 (EV-042). The IV-300 1.x signing key sits on a standalone offline workstation at MN-1 with one copy, email approvals and local logs (EV-043). SBOMs are generated in every build for products released since 2024; firmware released before 2024 has none (EV-044). Supplier SBOMs are on file for 15 of 38 third-party firmware modules (EV-045). PLM gates artifact promotion on an approved change record (EV-046). CMO-1 uses the manufacturing PKI; CMO-2 programs HB-40 boards outside it (EV-047).

**Contingency and recovery.** Contingency plans, DR runbooks and the recovery test history are on file, with no restore test recorded for the MN-1 MES server (EV-048). The RCM platform recovered in 3.5 hours against a 2-hour RTO on 2026-04-18, with technician desktops and call routing restored by hand (EV-049). The 2026-05-16 exercise reports arrived after the window: the DDC met its 2-hour RTO, and the build farm rebuild took 30 hours against a 24-hour RTO (EV-081).

**Cloud, logging and backup.** Both clouds use account vending, guardrails as code and daily posture scans (EV-050), with company-managed keys (EV-051). The SIEM does not list the legacy signing workstation or the MN-1 MES server as log sources (EV-052). The SOC runs 24x7 with MSSP overflow and receives a weekly report from MN-1 IT (EV-053). DDC and RCM databases have point-in-time recovery and immutable daily copies in separate accounts (EV-054). IV-300 first-generation modules reach the drug library service on a separate listener with per-hospital pre-shared keys and TLS 1.0, and receive unsigned drug libraries (EV-055). The DDC holds about 14 million patient records with 24-month retention, and RCM about 1.6 million patients (EV-056).

**Product security and quality.** The published CVD policy commits to acknowledgment within 3 business days, and the PSIRT handles about 140 reports a year; IV-300 v2 cases are tracked by the acquired team (EV-057). The company has been an ISAO member since 2023 (EV-058). The IV-300 v2 postmarket plan is run by the acquired team, and the VM-500 and IV-300 first-generation customer guides give no end-of-support dates (EV-059). The VM-500 operating system reaches end of support in 2027-06 (EV-060). 71% of VM-700 units installed security updates within 90 days; field service records give 48% for IV-300 pumps; PSIRT metrics report field deployment for VM-700 only (EV-061). Complaint, MDR and correction and removal procedures include cybersecurity decision points (EV-062), and design history files carry security activities and a security impact field on every change (EV-063).

**Workforce.** Annual awareness training and monthly phishing simulations are recorded; the secure coding module is overdue for about 18% of device software engineers (EV-064). Screening, enhanced screening for signing custodians, sanctions and attestations are recorded (EV-065).

**Third parties and contracts.** The third-party register lists about 2,400 suppliers, about 180 software and cloud vendors with company data and 62 PHI subcontractors; CMO-2's security assessment was due 2026-01 with no completion recorded (EV-066). Payments are listed in the AP vendor master (EV-067). 57 of the 62 PHI subcontractors have current subcontractor BAAs; the 5 inherited with the acquisition are on legacy contracts (EV-068). Customer BAA notice terms range from 5 to 30 days, and the notice-term field is blank for part of the register (EV-069). SOC reports are on file for both clouds, the colocation providers and the repository, PLM, eQMS and ticketing vendors (EV-070). CMO-2's security terms are pending renewal (EV-071). The interconnection register lists the DSF-MES interconnections (EV-072).

**Privacy, consumer app and RCM patients.** The Privacy Office keeps four-factor assessments and customer notice dates; consumer app incidents are handled under the HIPAA procedure and there is no FTC rule log (EV-073). The app release checklist has no standing review of third-party kits, and processor contracts do not refer to 16 CFR Part 318 (EV-074). RCM enrollment notices, consents, request logs and pairing checks are kept (EV-075).

**Facilities and disposal.** Badge-controlled cages and server rooms, quarterly access list reviews, facility security plans for both monitoring centers and certificates of destruction are on file (EV-076).

**AI.** The AI governance committee register lists 11 use cases, 7 with a completed review; since 2026-03 AI features without an inventory ID are blocked at procurement and change management (EV-077). The AI-001 training set holds a smaller share of women and of patients with a body mass index of 35 or more than the intended-use population (EV-078).
