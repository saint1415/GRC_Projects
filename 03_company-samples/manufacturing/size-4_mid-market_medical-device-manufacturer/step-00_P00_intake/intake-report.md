## 1. Purpose and scope
Intake collected the company's own records before any assessment work began on 2026-07-06. It covers the organization, the Device Lifecycle Platform and the systems around it, the fielded devices and their regulatory files, the PHI the Connected Care Cloud (CCC) holds for hospitals, the suppliers that touch these systems, and the rules that may bind the company. At this size the sources are systems of record: the identity provider, the HR system, the endpoint and EDR consoles, the cloud organization console, the SIEM, the SBOM store and match queue, the eQMS and PLM, the MES and OT engineering records, the ERP accounts payable vendor master, the contract and BAA repositories, the incident log, prior test and audit reports, and the current policies. Each item has an ID in [`evidence-register.csv`](evidence-register.csv). Later steps add their own fieldwork evidence (interviews, samples, tests) to the same register, so one list backs every deliverable.

This report records **observations, not findings**. Whether an observation meets a requirement is decided in the gap analysis (P03) and the control assessment (P07).

## 3. Observations by area
**Users and access.** The identity provider holds active named accounts for the 850 employees on the HR roster, plus contractor and service accounts, and every active workforce account has MFA registered (EV-001). Conditional access requires push MFA with number matching for standard users and phishing-resistant hardware keys for cloud, identity, repository and signing service administrators (EV-002). HR recorded 96 terminations and 58 internal transfers in the 12 months to 2026-06-30 (EV-003). The HR feed disables accounts on the termination date; on a transfer it changes department and title, and role removals in repositories, the cloud and the CCC go through tickets (EV-004). The last user access review was the annual campaign in January 2026 (EV-005). 58 privileged accounts exist across the administrative planes; 9 site reliability engineers hold standing write access to CCC production across all hospital tenants, 22 engineers administer the build runner pool, and just-in-time elevation covers the clinical database only (EV-006). Operators sign in to the 14 MES terminals and the test stations with shared accounts per line, with no MFA (EV-007). HR procedures require background checks for hires with CCC access, and the sanctions log records 2 sanctions in 2026 (EV-008).

**Devices, plant and network.** The endpoint console lists 780 laptops and 140 desktops, 920 in total, with an EDR agent and full-disk encryption reported on all of them (EV-009). There are 52 test and calibration stations; 11 run an operating system past its vendor's support end date, 41 have application allowlisting, and golden images exist for 2 of 5 station types (EV-010). The OT Engineering Manager estimates that the OT spreadsheet lists about 70% of about 310 networked OT assets (EV-011). A firewall with 14 broad allow rules separates the plant office network from OT; Line 1 and Line 3 share one flat segment, and the MES servers, the MES backup file server and the factory provisioning server sit on the OT network (EV-012). The line equipment vendor's VPN appliance keeps a persistent tunnel into the Line 1 and Line 3 segment with one shared account and a password, and no session approval or recording (EV-013). The campus walk-through on 2026-06-24 found staffed reception, escorted visitors and badge readers on plant, lab and server room doors; the offline VM-5 signing laptop and the one backup copy of its key are in the same safe; controls engineers edit station scripts on the stations (EV-014). Release signing keys and the device CA root are in the cloud HSM with a two-approver quorum; the issuing intermediate key for device certificates is held in software on the provisioning server, and no HSM key recovery exercise is recorded (EV-015). Cloud workloads are scanned monthly, and plant OT, test stations and MES servers are outside the scan scope (EV-016).

**Cloud, CCC, backups and monitoring.** The landing zone has 7 accounts with organization guardrails, posture management, a write-once log archive and company-managed keys in every account (EV-017). The clinical database has point-in-time recovery with a 5-minute log interval and daily snapshots in a separate backup account in a second region with 35-day write-once retention and separate administrator credentials; monthly restores into non-production are recorded. The MES database is backed up nightly to a plant file server only, with no restore recorded (EV-018). The 2025 CCC regional failover test took 6 hours against a 2-hour target, and the request for recovery tests of the HSM keys, the build pipeline and the MES returned none (EV-019). The SIEM receives identity, cloud, CCC, database, EDR and firewall logs, and nothing from plant OT, the MES, the provisioning server, PLM or the build pipeline; no review schedule exists for CCC privileged activity, bulk exports or signing service logs (EV-020). The MSSP contract covers IT and cloud, excludes OT, and requires a call within 30 minutes of a high-severity alert (EV-021). VM-7 and IP-4 connect with per-device certificates and VM-5 with one API key per hospital; 212 hospitals federate clinician sign-in, 259 use HL7 or FHIR over TLS and 31 use site-to-site VPNs with no written interconnection security terms (EV-022). Purge jobs apply the 24-month PHI retention set by the BAAs (EV-023).

**Product security and quality.** VM-7 (510(k), 2024), IP-4 (510(k), 2025) and AI-001 (De Novo, 2025) each carry a cybersecurity section with a management plan, SBOM, threat model, risk assessment, architecture views and test reports; VM-5 was cleared in 2018 (EV-024). Threat models cover the devices, the CCC and the signing pipeline, not the provisioning server or test stations, and no architecture view covers identity issuance at the factory; the IP-4 management plan states a semiannual release cycle (EV-025). The 2025 SPDF procedure covers firmware, AI-001 and the CCC, and its scope does not list station software or the provisioning path (EV-026). An SBOM is generated in every VM-7, IP-4, AI-001 and CCC build since 2025, and none exists for VM-5 (EV-027). In the match queue, 41 matches older than 90 days have no exploitability assessment, and the KEV comparison is a weekly manual task (EV-028). 14 external vulnerability reports arrived in 2026 through June, with a median acknowledgment of 2 business days and 1 report acknowledged in 6 (EV-029). The company has been an ISAO member since 2024 (EV-030). 38% of IP-4 pumps run firmware more than two releases old, VM-5 adoption is not measured, and the TPLC metrics report shows all three measures for VM-7 only (EV-031). The VM-5 embedded operating system reaches end of support in 2027-06, and no notice has gone to customers (EV-032). The VM-5 disclosure statement dates from 2022, and SBOMs reach customers on request (EV-033). The 2025 IP-4 penetration test used engineering units (EV-034). The MES maintenance log holds 64 station change records in 2026 with no security review or revalidation field (EV-035). The AOI equipment log records 2 model updates in 2025 with no validation record (EV-036). AI-005 was switched on in the eQMS on 2026-05-04 with no validation record (EV-037). The MDR and 806 procedures were revised in 2026-06 (EV-038).

**Programs and documents.** The 2024 incident response plan and the 2025 PSIRT procedure use separate severity scales and do not refer to each other's decisions, and no combined exercise is recorded (EV-039). The incident log holds 29 security incidents for January to June 2026 (EV-040). Five policies were adopted in 2024; standards exist for authenticators, cryptography, vulnerability management and secure development, and none for OT security, secure configuration, supplier security, logging, AI use or recovery; CVD and PSIRT records are not in the retention schedule (EV-041). Awareness training completion is 97%, and the June 2026 phishing click rate was 5.9% (EV-042). The 2025 internal IT audit was reported to the audit committee (EV-043). Designation memos dated 2026-07-01 name the HIPAA security official for the CCC, the 524B owner and the program lead (EV-044). The CCC SOC 2 Type 2 report (Security) for 2025-04-01 to 2026-03-31 is unqualified with 3 exceptions (EV-045).

**Suppliers and contracts.** Accounts payable paid about 160 suppliers in the 12 months; 38 are flagged with system or data access, 22 as critical software component suppliers, and 18 receive PHI (EV-046). Subcontractor BAAs were located for 16 of the 18; none for the call-recording service or the field service scheduling vendor. 6 of the 22 critical software supplier contracts have no vulnerability notification terms, and the line equipment vendor's agreement has no security terms (EV-047). BAAs with about 290 hospitals carry notice terms as short as 5 business days, and no register tracks them (EV-048). SOC 2 reports for the cloud, repository and PLM vendors are on file; the identity provider, MSSP and eQMS reports were requested (EV-049). CCC agreements promise 99.9% monthly availability, and a group purchasing contract covering about 60 hospitals requires SOC 2 Availability and Confidentiality from contract year 2028; there are no defense or federal contracts (EV-050). The cyber policy has a $15 million limit and a $500,000 retention (EV-051). The employee health plan is fully insured (EV-052).

**Facilities and media.** Badge access was reviewed for Q2 2026 and visitor logs are kept for 2 years (EV-053). The facility security plan dates from 2024, and the plant, labs, offices and distribution center share one campus (EV-054). Disposal certificates and a depot wipe log are on file; no sanitization records exist for lab test devices returned from hospital pilots (EV-055).

**Business volume.** Revenue was about $240 million in FY2025: monitors about $118 million, pumps and pump service about $62 million, subscriptions about $34 million and service about $26 million, over about 250 business days (EV-056). About 21,000 VM-7, 11,500 VM-5 and 7,800 IP-4 units are in service, the CCC holds PHI for about 2.4 million patients, and finished goods cover about 3 weeks of monitors and 2 weeks of pumps (EV-057).

**AI tools.** Six AI uses were found: AI-001 and AI-002 in the products, the AI-003 AOI model on Line 1, the AI-004 coding assistant (140 engineers), the AI-005 eQMS feature, and staff use of public AI assistants; the web proxy has no block category for public AI domains, and review records exist for AI-001 only (EV-058). AI-002 is in development, trained on 1.9 million de-identified alarm events from 9 hospitals (EV-059).

**Reconciliation across sources.**
- **Workforce.** The 850 employees on the HR roster each have an identity provider account (EV-001, EV-003).
- **Endpoints.** The endpoint console count (920: 780 laptops and 140 desktops) matches the SSP boundary and the P07 laptop population (EV-009).
- **Plant OT.** The spreadsheet covers about 70% of the about 310 networked OT assets. The difference stays an open request (EV-011).
- **Suppliers.** About 160 suppliers in accounts payable, 38 with system or data access, and 18 PHI subcontractors, 16 with a BAA (EV-046, EV-047). In [`vendor-register.csv`](vendor-register.csv) the named PHI subcontractors and the summary row V-15 add up to the 18.
- **Hospitals.** The about 290 customers in the contract register match the BAA repository (EV-048, EV-050).
- **AI tools.** Accounts payable shows the coding assistant, the eQMS subscription that carries AI-005 and the AOI equipment service; the PLM records add AI-001 and AI-002; public assistant use comes from the survey only (EV-046, EV-058, EV-059).
